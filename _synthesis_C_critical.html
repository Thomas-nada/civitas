2. What Is Working Well

Honest assessment of genuine governance achievements — areas where the data shows the system functioning as designed or better than designed.

Iterative Constitution Improvement

The constitution evolved from ratification (epoch 542) to v2.4 (epoch 609) in 67 epochs, with four drafts, two failures, and one successful amendment. Each version addressed the specific criticisms of the prior one — YUTA's responsive authorship model and EMURGO's v2.3→v2.4 flip demonstrate that targeted concessions can unlock consensus. The community produced a working living document.

Evidence: Constitution ratified 4 epochs after submission (epoch 537→541). v2.4 passed with broad YES including EMURGO reversal.

DRep Feedback as Reform Pressure

NO votes function as reform pressure, not just blocking. InputEndorsers documented this explicitly regarding Intersect: the organization was forced to reduce its ask from ₳20M to ₳15.75M, cut bundled proposals into individual submissions, and change leadership — all in direct response to DRep rejection. The Snek grant failures drove the ₳5M loan structure that ultimately passed. Proposal iteration demonstrably works.

Evidence: Intersect 20M→15.75M reduction; Snek grant failed×2, loan succeeded; DeFi liquidity v1 failed (85% NO), v2 staged version passed (55% YES).

Governance Crisis Resilience

The system has navigated multiple crises without collapsing: a December 2025 CC member retirement halted governance temporarily; the NCL mid-cycle revision debate was resolved; the Christina CC addition controversy produced a failed vote but no governance paralysis; competing NCL proposals were handled through parallel voting rather than deadlock. Each crisis produced a governance response that revealed protocol gaps, which were then addressed.

Evidence: Replace Interim CC (epoch 573), Add CC member (epoch 599), CC size reduction debate (epoch 612), all resolved through governance action without chain halt.

Founding Entity Power Transition

The three founding entities (IO, CF, EMURGO) transitioned from controlling the Constitutional Committee to participating as DReps alongside the community. Their positions genuinely diverge — CF and EMURGO sometimes vote against each other and against IO positions. EMURGO voted NO on v2.3 while CF voted YES; CF abstained on IO Engineering while most DReps voted YES. This is genuine pluralism, not coordinated block voting. The interim CC replacement passed near-unanimously.

Evidence: Replace Interim CC at epoch 573 was described as near-unanimous. CF's IO Engineering abstain contrasted with EMURGO's YES. EMURGO's v2.3 NO while CF voted YES.

Data Integrity Norm Enforcement

The very first governance action (HOSKY Hard Fork, epoch 507) was rejected primarily because external metadata was missing from gov.tools. GAIA and InputEndorsers explicitly cited data integrity grounds. This established an immediate, durable norm: proposals must meet minimum data standards regardless of who submits them or how beloved the cause. The subsequent parameter change failure (epoch 518, unnamed proposal) for lack of transparency reinforced it.

Evidence: HOSKY Hard Fork — "couldn't read the proposal." Unnamed parameter change (gov_action1286ft...) failed for opacity. GAIA cited Article IV §3-5 on multiple proposals.

High-Quality Analytical Cohort

Despite the concentration risk, the quality ceiling of Cardano DRep analysis is genuinely high. YUTA's Summit 2026 NO vote cited specific on-chain transaction data (99% retail usage, zero identifiable enterprise transactions at epoch 622). SIPO compared Cardano's governance to Polkadot OpenGov and Cosmos. jonahkoch conducted institutional-grade analysis of Draper Dragon's waterfall mechanics. Cerkoryn tracked tau parameter history to Shelley launch. This is governance discourse of a quality rare in any public institution.

Evidence: YUTA adatool.net citation; SIPO cross-chain comparison; jonahkoch fund waterfall analysis; Cerkoryn historical parameter research cited in Constitution and NCL rationales.

3. Structural Tensions

Three unresolved tensions that cannot be "fixed" — they are inherent to the design choices Cardano has made. Understanding them is the prerequisite to managing them.

I

Decentralization vs. Organizational Efficiency

The Coordination Paradox

Cardano's governance requires that no single entity can dominate, yet the governance system has proven unable to function without Intersect as a coordinating hub. The epoch 570 batch of 37 proposals — 31% of all governance history — was coordinated, socialized, and submitted through Intersect. Without Intersect, individual proposal submission would have required each project team to independently navigate the 100,000 ADA deposit, secure enough DRep attention for a standalone vote, and survive without the pre-approval signal of the Ekklesia process.

The Decentralization Case

GAIA: "Intersect wants to operate as an MBO with all the benefits of being an MBO, but then they want to be completely community funded. You cannot have this both ways." TriangleForces: "The governance and oversight model remains heavily centralized around Intersect's leadership." Dave rejected the 275M budget because it relied on "unverifiable off-chain metrics" from Ekklesia.

The Efficiency Case

InputEndorsers, despite being a NO on the budget process, praised Intersect's evolution: "A lot of proposals are now dependent on the presence of a rational and functional administrator... Intersect cut the treasury withdrawal from the massive 20M ADA." Without Intersect, the 2025 budget cycle would likely not have produced 43 approved projects.

The Unresolved Core:

The tension cannot be resolved while Intersect controls both the administrative pipeline and the interpretive discretion over off-chain processes. The CF's call for a shift from "vendor-led" to "purpose-led" budgeting points toward a structural solution — but requires removing Intersect's ability to define what counts as a qualifying proposal, which requires replacing Intersect's role with on-chain tooling that doesn't yet exist.

II

Constitutional Purity vs. Pragmatic Progress

The "Good Enough" Doctrine

The dominant governance philosophy in the dataset is what InputEndorsers called "a snapshot in time of an ephemeral consensus" — constitutions are provisional, amendable, and sufficient for moving forward. The Chilean delegate's real-world observation ("seeking perfection left us stuck with the old one") has been internalized by the majority. But this pragmatism has costs.

The Budget Process Framework was applied (epoch 570, ₳271M disbursed) before it was formally ratified — and when it was submitted for formal ratification as an Info action at epoch 616, it failed. The governance process that unlocked the largest single treasury disbursement in Cardano history has never been formally ratified. The rationale that this is fine — because individual withdrawals all cleared the 67% threshold — is internally consistent, but it means the community has committed to a budget methodology without ever formally endorsing that methodology.

The Purity Risk

GAIA: "A significant amount of proposals that were successfully passed did not meet the on-chain threshold of 2.69B ADA. Omnibus budgets are the only way skewed results like this are able to circumvent intended on-chain guardrails." The 275M budget passed with ~60% support — the individual proposals inside it, if voted separately, might have achieved different outcomes.

The Progress Imperative

CF voted YES on the 275M budget "despite our significant reservations" because "the crucial step of establishing a budget is taken." The alternative — waiting for process perfection — would have blocked all 2025 treasury funding. In practice, the pragmatic majority enabled 43 infrastructure projects to receive funding.

The Unresolved Core:

Governance is accumulating precedent faster than it is encoding principles. Each "pragmatic YES" under imperfect conditions sets a precedent that can be cited to justify the next imperfect proposal. The community has not yet drawn a line on what constitutes a process failure serious enough to vote NO despite agreeing with the substance. InputEndorsers drew such a line (NO on 275M budget, NO on Budget Process Framework). Most did not.

III

Small DRep Participation vs. Deep DRep Engagement

The Cognitive Load Trap

SIPO "experienced firsthand the challenges of reviewing 194 proposals in inconsistent formats." The epoch 570 batch submitted 37 proposals simultaneously. Any DRep who attempted genuine analysis of all 37 would have needed to evaluate ₳271M of treasury commitments in roughly 7 epochs (5 weeks). The cognitive load is not compatible with small-DRep participation: part-time governance participants, DReps from time zones without overlap with European working hours, or DReps without institutional staff support cannot match the analytical output of CF's professional governance team or the Japanese DRep community's organized network.

The result is a two-tier system: the ~10 DReps who do deep analysis set the intellectual frame for a proposal; the remaining DReps largely follow that frame without conducting independent analysis. This isn't irrational — Kostas Panagias explicitly states he reads other DReps' rationales before forming opinions, and describes this as epistemically appropriate. But it creates a dependency on the analytical tier that is itself undiversified.

The Unresolved Core:

Cardano has attempted to solve this through the budget process (pre-filtering via Ekklesia before on-chain votes), which reduces the number of proposals DReps must evaluate. But Ekklesia itself is an off-chain tool controlled by Intersect with unspecified counting methodology. The structural solution — granular proposal submission with mandatory minimum spacing, or RFP-style competitive processes — has been recommended by CF but not enacted. The current system rewards institutional capacity, which means governance will increasingly reflect institutional interests unless structural changes reduce the cognitive load for independent DReps.

4. Precedent Audit

The most consequential governance precedents established by these 118 actions. Some constrain future governance; some opened doors that cannot be closed.

P1 · IO Engineering Receives Institutional Trust at Scale

CONSTRAINING

The ₳96.8M IO Engineering approval (epoch 574) with ~88% YES established that the founding technical entity can draw from the treasury at nine-figure scale based primarily on institutional trust and track record, rather than itemized deliverables. CF's abstention — the most process-rigorous DRep — failed to alter the outcome. This precedent will be cited in every future large-scale IO request. Any DRep who now demands itemized breakdowns will face the counterargument that DReps approved ₳96.8M without them in 2025.

Epoch 574 · ₳96,817,080 · 88% YES · CF and Cardanians abstained citing "lack of transparency"

P2 · Loan Structures Unlock Grants That Fail as Grants

DOOR OPENED

The Snek ₳5M loan (epoch 597) succeeded after two direct grant attempts failed, establishing that reframing a disbursement as a recoverable loan can convert a near-unanimous NO into a YES. This precedent created a template for repayable capital requests that was then extended to Draper Dragon (₳50M VC fund, treasury-first repayment). Future commercial proposals that fail as grants will systematically attempt loan structures — whether or not genuine repayment is realistic.

Snek grants failed epoch 573; Snek loan passed epoch 597; Draper Dragon VC fund passed epoch 623

P3 · Constitution Can Be Amended Without In-Person Convention

DOOR OPENED

v2.0 was authored and submitted by YUTA through asynchronous online process, without a global convention. Despite process objections, it established — and v2.3 and v2.4 reinforced — that constitutional amendments can be community-driven online without physical convention. The Buenos Aires convention model is no longer required precedent for constitutional change. This lowers the barrier for future amendments, but also removes the legitimacy signal of an in-person convention where delegates had verifiable geographic representation.

v2.0 by YUTA without convention (epoch 574, failed); v2.4 also by YUTA (epoch 601, passed)

P4 · Omnibus Budget Disbursements Are Constitutionally Viable

DOOR OPENED — DANGEROUS

The 275M budget passed as a single Info action incorporating 39 proposals. GAIA's analysis showed several individual proposals in that bundle "did not meet the on-chain threshold of 2.69B ADA" — but passed because the bundle aggregate did. This precedent proves that bundling can circumvent the intended per-proposal 67% DRep threshold. Future large-scale actors with multiple proposals will be incentivized to bundle rather than submit individually. Almost every DRep who voted YES on the 275M budget simultaneously criticized the bundling — but the YES vote is the precedent that will be cited.

Epoch 557 Info action · 275M ADA bundle · ~60% YES · CF: "despite our significant reservations"

P5 · DeFi Treasury Deployment Is Constitutionally Permissible

DOOR OPENED

The DeFi Liquidity Budget Withdrawal 1 (epoch 624) and Draper Dragon Orion Fund (epoch 623) established that the Cardano treasury can be deployed as investment capital into commercial DeFi operations and VC funds — not merely as grants for public goods. The near-unanimous rejection of the original 50M DeFi proposal and the successful approval of the staged 800K pilot followed by the ₳50M Draper Dragon demonstrates that the question is not "can the treasury do this" but "under what conditions." The conditions (treasury-first waterfall, staged disbursement, named governance) are now established baseline expectations.

DeFi v1 original failed (85% NO epoch 556); DeFi Withdrawal 1 passed (55% YES epoch 617); Draper Dragon passed (65% YES epoch 623)

P6 · Process Can Be Used Before It Is Ratified

CONSTRAINING — SYSTEMIC

The Budget Process Framework (epoch 616) failed as an Info action — yet the process it described had already governed ₳271M in disbursements at epoch 570. This is perhaps the most structurally significant precedent in the dataset: off-chain processes controlled by a single entity can govern massive on-chain spending before those processes receive any formal ratification. Any future Intersect-designed process can be deployed, produce outcomes, and accumulate path dependency before formal governance approval — because by the time approval is sought, the outcomes are already locked in.

Budget Process Framework failed epoch 616; ₳271M already disbursed under that framework at epoch 570

5. Red Flags

Five specific data points that should concern anyone who cares about long-term governance health. Cited directly from the evidence.

01

96.3% of Quantified ADA Requests Were Approved — Including ₳96.8M Without Itemized Justification

The treasury approval rate is so high it indicates the system lacks an effective fiscal filter. Of all quantified ADA requests, ₳356.7M was approved against ₳13.65M rejected — a 96.3% approval rate by volume. The largest single withdrawal in Cardano history (₳96.8M IO Engineering) passed at ~88% despite the CF explicitly abstaining due to "lack of sufficient transparency or accountability" and Cardanians calling it "the largest budget request in Cardano's history" with no itemized breakdown. When the most rigorous institutional DRep's abstention on transparency grounds fails to prevent a ₳96.8M approval, the accountability floor is effectively the proposer's own track record — not verifiable accountability mechanisms.

"Where are the KPIs and deliverables on each line item? This feels like the classic big tech company approach of throwing money at problems until they go away." — Maureen, voting NO on IO Engineering
02

The Governance Process That Disbursed ₳271M Was Never Formally Ratified

The Budget Process Framework that governed the 2025 Cardano Budget — including the Ekklesia voting process, the 67% threshold, the Intersect administrative role, and the 39-proposal structure — was submitted for formal Info action ratification at epoch 616 and failed. The ₳271M had already been disbursed at epoch 570. The governance community approved hundreds of millions of ADA under an informal process that it subsequently declined to formally ratify. This creates a legitimacy gap: if the process was sound, why did the community reject it when asked to ratify it? If it was unsound, how was ₳271M disbursed under it without constitutional challenge?

"There were a lot of improvisations and uncertainties with the Ekklesia voting stage... Too much power to interpret the results is left to Intersect." — InputEndorsers, voting NO on Budget Process Framework
03

EMURGO Controls ~16% of All Governance Voting Power While Receiving Treasury Funding and Routing User Delegation

GAIA's estimate puts EMURGO + YOROI combined at approximately 16% of total governance voting power. EMURGO submitted the Cardano Summit 2026 proposal (which failed at 72% NO, but EMURGO voted YES on its own proposal). EMURGO's Yoroi wallet was documented as routing users to delegate their DRep power to EMURGO itself — a UI decision that extracts governance power from delegators without their explicit awareness. InputEndorsers established a standing NO vote on all EMURGO proposals until March 2027 as a direct consequence. A founding entity systematically capturing user delegation through wallet UI while simultaneously proposing and voting on treasury requests for its own events is a structural conflict of interest without constitutional remedy.

"The Cardano Foundation and EMURGO should be using genesis ADA to pay for building Cardano, not exploiting the treasury every week or two." — GAIA
04

The Plutocracy Lock-In: 1-Lovelace-1-Vote May Be Unreformable

Cardano Advocate's constitutional NO vote included the most structurally prescient critique in the dataset: "When we find a better, fairer, and more representative model, we won't be able to upgrade it as large whales and token holders will likely not be receptive to losing voting power and influence regarding the future of Cardano." This is not a fringe concern — it is a constitutional design problem. The 1-lovelace-1-vote model is now enshrined in v2.4 (InputEndorsers flagged that the wording "DReps have voting power equal to number of Lovelace delegated" would prevent quadratic voting). EMURGO's 16% governance power concentration is precisely the dynamic Cardano Advocate warned about. The entities who would need to vote to reduce their own voting power are the same entities who benefit from the current system.

"The 1-lovelace-1-vote model sadly leads to centralized oligarchy or plutocracy, and we are in this situation right from the start of governance, which was predetermined for CIP 1694." — GAIA
05

YUTA Is the Single Point of Failure for Constitutional Maintenance

The Cardano Constitution has been effectively maintained by a single individual across four drafts, with EMURGO, CF, InputEndorsers, and Cerkoryn all crediting YUTA's responsive authorship. This is governance intellectual property concentrated in one person without constitutional basis, formal role, term limits, or succession plan. Agent 2 notes directly: "YUTA has become the de facto constitutional maintenance lead — credited by EMURGO, InputEndorsers, and Cerkoryn for responsive, iterative improvement. This informal role lacks constitutional basis and creates a single point of dependency." If YUTA becomes inactive, faces a conflict of interest, or introduces a sustained bias in amendments, there is no formal process for identifying or replacing them. The community has implicitly delegated constitutional stewardship to one DRep.

"Evolution waits for no one. Our constitutions are but snapshots in time of an ephemeral consensus — not a national constitution." — InputEndorsers (while inadvertently relying on YUTA to author each snapshot)

6. Green Flags

Five specific data points that demonstrate genuine governance maturity — evidence the system is developing real capacity, not just process theater.

01

KPI Accountability: YUTA's Summit 2026 Vote Cites Prior KPI Failure

YUTA voted NO on Cardano Summit 2026 by citing the specific unmet KPI from the 2025 Summit proposal: "15-20% year-on-year growth in developer numbers — that was the primary reason I voted YES at the time... the number of cases that concretely demonstrate the 2025 goal of 'acquiring and retaining new developers' is effectively zero." YUTA then provided on-chain evidence: at epoch 622, 99% of transaction submissions were retail, with zero identifiable enterprise submissions. This is accountability-based governance — prior commitments evaluated before new requests approved. It is rare in any public institution and indicates at least some DReps are operating a functional accountability cycle, not merely responding to each proposal in isolation.

Evidence: YUTA's Summit 2026 rationale citing adatool.net/tx-stats data; prior 2025 Summit rationale with stated KPI.
02

HOSKY's Governance Evolution: Meme Token Becomes Accountability Watchdog

HOSKY's trajectory from simple YES/Abstain votes to developing a named "Cardano First" framework with five scored pillars, PDF-linked rationales, and one of the sharpest accountability challenges to the Cardano Foundation's Summit funding ("CF committed to making this sustainable, and one year later it's back asking for the same money") is one of the most encouraging signals in the dataset. It demonstrates that governance personas can mature under community pressure. HOSKY also demonstrated exemplary conflict-of-interest handling, abstaining on CC compensation due to being a CC member. A meme token DRep now produces governance analysis comparable to institutional DReps — this is the governance self-improvement mechanism working.

Evidence: Agent 4 HOSKY profile — "evolution description: from simple process-enabler to rigorous accountability watchdog." Summit 2026 NO with CF accountability challenge. CC compensation abstain with explicit COI disclosure.
03

Hard Fork Governance Is Efficient: Protocol Version 10 Ratified in 7 Epochs

The Plomin Hard Fork (epoch 529 → ratified epoch 536) was one of the fastest-resolved major actions in the dataset. Technical protocol changes with genuine engineering consensus — Plutus V3 cost model (6 epochs), transaction memory increase (7 epochs) — move quickly through the system. The governance design correctly distinguishes between technically consensual actions (hard forks, parameter changes with committee endorsement) and politically contested actions (treasury withdrawals, constitutional amendments). Where Intersect's Parameter Committee has done pre-work and engineering consensus exists, the DRep layer ratifies efficiently. This suggests the tripartite governance model (SPOs + DReps + CC) works well for technical consensus questions.

Evidence: Plomin HF epoch 529→536 (7 epochs). Plutus V3 epoch 519→525 (6 epochs). Memory Units epoch 606→613 (7 epochs). All near-unanimous.
04

Intersect Reformed Under Pressure — Validating the NO Vote Mechanism

Intersect's budget request was reduced from ₳20M to ₳15.75M, its bundled proposals were separated into individual submissions, and its leadership changed — all in direct response to DRep rejection. InputEndorsers' analysis is forensic: "Intersect has been greatly shaped into a leaner and meaner machine by pushback, not by support... I want to reciprocate with my own leap of faith." This is the DRep feedback loop functioning as designed: sustained NO votes creating reform pressure, reform triggering conditional YES from previously skeptical DReps, and the system arriving at a more accountable outcome than the original proposal would have produced. It validates the entire premise of delegated representation: DReps who maintain principled positions force organizational improvement.

Evidence: Intersect budget 20M→15.75M reduction. InputEndorsers NO→conditional YES trajectory. Agent 3: "InputEndorsers' analysis — DRep NO votes functioning as reform pressure."

Cross-Chain Comparative Benchmarking Has Entered DRep Discourse

Overall Governance Health: Cautiously Functional

Cardano's post-Chang governance has completed two full budget cycles, ratified a constitution through four drafts, navigated multiple governance crises, and disbursed over ₳356M in treasury funds — all without a chain halt, a governance deadlock, or a constitutional crisis. These are genuine achievements for a governance system less than three years old.

However, the structural vulnerabilities identified in this assessment are not edge cases — they are load-bearing risks. Discourse concentration means the entire intellectual quality of governance depends on fewer than ten individuals. Founding entity dual roles mean the entities most capable of capturing the governance process are the same entities most trusted by the majority. The plutocracy lock-in means the voting model that perpetuates these dynamics is now constitutionally entrenched. The 0% Info action pass rate means the community has no functional non-binding signaling mechanism.

The clearest finding from the data is that Cardano governance is better than it should be given its design constraints — because a small cohort of principled DReps is doing extraordinary work within a system that does not structurally reward that work. The risk is that this cohort is not replaceable by design, and the system has not yet built the institutional infrastructure to make their quality of analysis reproducible at scale.
