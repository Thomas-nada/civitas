The 6 Governance Archetypes

DRep voting behavior clusters into recognizable patterns. Understanding these archetypes helps predict proposal outcomes and governance coalition dynamics.

✅
Aligned Yes
~25% of active DReps

Support most major proposals with short, process-enabling rationales. View info actions as procedural steps. Trust the 67% withdrawal threshold as sufficient protection. Enable governance momentum but contribute little intellectual scrutiny.

Members: CaFi, AdaStat, EMURGO, Eternl DRep Committee, William Doyle, BWtake, AKYO
🔍
Skeptical-Critical
~20% of active DReps

Apply genuine scrutiny, often vote No, but are persuadable by specific structural safeguards. Not dogmatically opposed to spending. Prefer phased, gradual approaches. Write long rationales with specific concerns. Upgrade from No to Yes when structure improves.

Members: InputEndorsers, Cerkoryn, hix_coffeepool, SASA, Nimuë
🚫
Principled No
~10% of active DReps

Consistent opposition based on stated principles — fiscal conservatism, decentralization, anti-bundling. Hard to persuade. Supply/inflation arguments. Occasionally strategic Yes votes framed as "bitter medicine." Risk: principles applied inconsistently can erode credibility.

Members: $brock, TriangleForces, Dave, GAIA (on spending)
⚖️
Process-Focused
~20% of active DReps

Prioritize governance process integrity, constitutional compliance, and proper sequencing above substantive agreement. Vote No when process is wrong even if content is good. Demand NCL → budget → withdrawal sequencing. Most concerned about bundling and granularity.

Members: YUTA, SIPO, Cardano Foundation, GAIA, Kostas, SASA
🔬
Technical
~10% of active DReps

Focus on technical merit, on-chain data, and empirical evidence. Reference TapTools, exchange volumes, DEX data. Demand measurable KPIs with verifiable baselines. Question implementation feasibility. Analytically rigorous but sometimes isolated from broader governance values.

Members: YUTA, CardanoYoda, Nimuë, SIPO
💰
Economic/Financial
~15% of active DReps

Prioritize financial prudence, ADA value preservation, and treasury sustainability. Apply ADA supply/inflation arguments. Often the most sophisticated analysis of monetary mechanics. Concern about sell pressure from treasury withdrawals. Often vote No on marketing and events.

Members: InputEndorsers, $brock, TriangleForces, hix_coffeepool, jonahkoch

Cross-Cutting Themes

Seven recurring themes appear across DRep rationales regardless of proposal type. These are the ideological fault lines of Cardano governance.

1. ADA Supply Preservation vs. Treasury as Growth Capital

HIGH TENSION

The most fundamental ideological divide. One camp treats treasury spending as ADA devaluation — every withdrawal converts the native token to fiat, eroding the monetary base. The other camp treats an idle treasury as opportunity cost — ADA sitting in the treasury is ADA not building the ecosystem. Champions of preservation: InputEndorsers, $brock, hix_coffeepool, TriangleForces. Champions of deployment: jonahkoch, EMURGO, CaFi. Trajectory: Treasury deployment is winning, but supply preservation voices are shaping the terms (loans vs grants, staged withdrawals).

2. Constitutionality as a Hard Filter

MATURING

A growing segment of DReps explicitly vote on whether proposals comply with the Constitution before evaluating their merits. Constitutional violations — missing audit allocations, undelegated wallet requirements, bundling prohibitions — function as hard blockers regardless of proposal quality. This is a sign of governance maturity, not obstruction. Key practitioners: GAIA, SIPO, CF, InputEndorsers, Cerkoryn. Trajectory: Accelerating — each iteration of rejected proposals cites constitutional defects explicitly.

3. Intersect: Trustworthy MBO or Centralization Risk

CONTESTED

Intersect receives broadly positive ratings for coordinating the first budget cycle, but a consistent minority raises structural concerns: non-elected membership-gated governance, opaque personnel costs, and the tension between being both member-funded and community-funded. The MBO passed after cutting from 20M to 15.75M ADA — direct evidence that pushback shaped the proposal. Critics: InputEndorsers, Dave, TriangleForces. Supporters: BWtake, CaFi, SIPO. Trajectory: Broadly accepted but watched closely. "Purpose-led vs vendor-led" will define 2026 cycle.

4. Accountability and Milestone Verification

STRENGTHENING

The most common NO-vote trigger across all categories: lump-sum asks without itemized cost breakdowns. The ecosystem is progressively adopting smart-contract-enforced milestone disbursements as the standard. YUTA's explicit requirement that "treasury withdrawal governance actions must be performed in reasonably granular chunks" is becoming a de facto community norm. Key enforcers: YUTA, CF, CardanoYoda, HOSKY. Trajectory: Strong and accelerating — proposals that don't conform are increasingly rejected.

5. Geographic and Cultural Representation

POSITIVE SIGNAL

The geographic diversity of the active DRep set is a genuine governance asset. The Japanese community (YUTA, SIPO, BWtake, SASA, AKYO) is disproportionately active and rigorous. African representation (Wada DRep, Addisetech) frames governance through an inclusion lens absent elsewhere. Latin American voices (Arturo) and Asian voices more broadly demonstrate that on-chain governance is reaching communities that traditional Web2 governance never did. Risk: English-language proposals and processes still structurally disadvantage non-native speakers despite bilingual participation.

6. Founding Entity Governance Capture

STRUCTURAL RISK

IO, CF, and EMURGO are simultaneously the largest proposers AND significant governance voters. CF disclosed its CEO sits on the Snek advisory board yet voted YES on the Snek loan. IO is a proposer, voter, and dominant technical contributor. This triple role creates structural conflicts that disclosures mitigate but do not resolve. DReps are beginning to name this explicitly. Explicit flaggers: GAIA, InputEndorsers, $brock. Trajectory: Growing awareness, insufficient structural remedy yet.

7. Quality vs. Quantity of DRep Participation

REQUIRES ATTENTION

The 7,140 rationale files show a starkly bimodal distribution. Fewer than 15 DReps produce the vast majority of intellectual content — multi-page, data-rich, condition-setting analyses that actually move governance outcomes. The long tail writes 1–3 sentence process-enablers. This is not necessarily a problem — liquid democracy is designed so analytical effort concentrates — but if the analytical core burns out or is captured, the rest of the DRep set provides no backstop. The question is not whether the quality tail is thin — it is whether the thin tail is enough.
