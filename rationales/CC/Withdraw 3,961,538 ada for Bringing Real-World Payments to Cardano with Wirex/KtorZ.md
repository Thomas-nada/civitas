**Vote:** Yes

The proposal funds the development of payment infrastructure intended to connect Cardano assets and applications with card payments, stablecoin systems, banking rails and self-custody wallet integrations. Enabling practical payment use cases and broader access to Cardano-based assets presents a reasonable case for advancing the long-term sustainability and utility of Cardano under Article I - Section 1.

The proposal identifies an administrator, states that the vendor has received no Cardano Treasury funding during the previous 24 months, specifies audit and oversight arrangements, defines custody through dedicated treasury management smart contracts, provides milestone-based disbursement controls, and remains compliant with the applicable Net Change Limit.

The metadata would nevertheless have benefited from greater precision regarding the scope of the promised open-source infrastructure. In particular, it is unclear which elements of the Visa card, banking, compliance and operational infrastructure will be released as public goods. The proposal also does not explain the custody and settlement model underlying cards described as being linked directly to on-chain balances. These ambiguities reduce the comprehensibility of the proposal under Article II - Section 6.2, but I do not consider the available information sufficient to establish a clear constitutional violation.

Hence, I do not identify a clear constitutional violation and consider the proposal constitutionally compliant.
