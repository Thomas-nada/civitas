This Treasury Withdrawal Governance Action (TWGA) seeks ₳10,142,000 to support Amaru’s 2026 work towards an interoperable, block-producing Cardano node implementation, with milestones extending from January through December 2026. The proposal’s central aim is to bring Amaru to production readiness by achieving feature parity with the Haskell node, beginning with a relay-capable node able to follow chain selection, diffuse transactions and blocks, and fully validate ledger compliance. It also proposes continued hardening of the system through work on the mempool, performance, observability, and, ultimately, safe block-production capability, while maintaining interoperability and usability on lower-resource hardware.

Article II.6 requires governance actions to be submitted in a standardized and legible format, supported by immutable off-chain documentation, and accompanied by sufficient rationale, including a title, abstract, justification, and supporting materials. On its face, this proposal satisfies those requirements. It was submitted in a standardized format, provides both a URL and hash for immutable off-chain content, and includes the required abstract, rationale, and supporting materials.

Article II.7 imposes additional requirements specific to TWGAs, and the proposal addresses each of them. It identifies the purpose of the withdrawal, specifies the 2026 delivery period, describes the cost structure and refund conditions, and discloses prior treasury funding received within the last 24 months It also provides for periodic independent audits and oversight metrics, designates administrators known to the community who are working through a smart contract, and states that treasury funds will be held in five separate script-controlled accounts that are auditable by the community and delegated only to the predefined abstain governance option rather than to a DRep or SPO. We apply Article II.7.3, requiring “A Net Change Limit must be set” and “Treasury Withdrawals actions must not exceed the Net Change Limit for that period,” concluding this is not a barrier to this action’s constitutionality.
