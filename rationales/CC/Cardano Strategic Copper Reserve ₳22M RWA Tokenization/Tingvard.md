# Tingvard

**Proposal:** Cardano Strategic Copper Reserve ₳22M RWA Tokenization
**Vote:** No
**Voter ID:** `646d1b3ac94568a422b687db6c47acdf849f1674982ae4f9a494be43`

---

This governance action requests 22,000,000 ADA from the Cardano Treasury to Toto Finance Inc. to acquire COMEX Grade 1 copper cathodes, tokenize them as CuTT, hold the copper for 18 to 24 months, and return the net sale proceeds and unspent or unearned balances to the Cardano Treasury by month 36.

This action is properly framed as a Treasury Withdrawals action and must therefore be assessed under Article II, Section 6 and Article II, Section 7 of the Constitution, together with the applicable treasury guardrails in Appendix I.

Tingvard’s assessment is based on the governance action metadata anchored on-chain, whose content is verified by its on-chain hash. The referenced proposal PDF is not hashed in the metadata and is hosted on the same website as the anchor. Tingvard has therefore used it only to corroborate the anchored metadata. Terms that appear only in the PDF, including its milestone schedule, dual authorisation controls, 90-day refund provision and audit sub-cap, have not been treated as terms of the action.

The proposal satisfies Article II, Section 6, §2. It includes a title, abstract, motivation, rationale and a reference to supporting material.

The proposal satisfies Article II, Section 7, §1. It identifies the purpose of the withdrawal, a delivery period of up to 36 months, five cost categories adding up to the full 22,000,000 ADA (copper, execution, an operating and assurance envelope, development and project management, and independent oversight), and the circumstances under which funds are to be returned to the Treasury.

The proposal satisfies Article II, Section 7, §2 by stating that Toto Finance has received no ada from the Cardano Treasury within the last 24 months.

The proposal satisfies Article II, Section 7, §3 and TREASURY-01a and TREASURY-02a. The applicable Net Change Limit of 500,000,000 ADA covering epochs 613 to 713 has been agreed on-chain, and the enacted withdrawals in that period leave sufficient remaining capacity for this request. The withdrawal is denominated in ada, satisfying TREASURY-03a.

The proposal satisfies Article II, Section 7, §4, with a concern. It allocates funds for independent annual physical and financial audits and for reserve and token-supply reporting within a 3,533,859 ADA operating and assurance envelope, and reserves 660,000 ADA for independent oversight. However, the audit allocation shares a single ceiling with warehouse and insurance costs and with Toto Finance’s own disclosed margin of up to 30% on third-party invoices.

The constitutional defects are found under Article II, Section 6, §1, Article II, Section 7, §5 and Article II, Section 7, §6. Each is sufficient on its own to render the action unconstitutional.

Article II, Section 6, §1 requires a URL hosting a document that outlines the proposed governance action, and the hash of that document. It further requires that “The document hosted by such a URL shall be immutable and incapable of being altered after submission”.

The anchor is hosted at an ordinary website URL controlled by the proposer, whose content can be changed after submission. The website was redeployed approximately 17 hours after the action was submitted, which illustrates that capacity. The anchor file still matches its on-chain hash, and Tingvard does not find that its content has changed. Article II, Section 6, §1, however, requires the hosted document to be immutable and incapable of being altered after submission, not merely unaltered. A matching hash shows whether a document has changed; it does not make it incapable of change. If a matching hash were sufficient, the requirement of immutability would add nothing to the requirement to provide the hash. The proposal itself instructs that the final documents be published “at immutable URLs” before submission, and this was not done.

Tingvard therefore finds that the action fails to satisfy Article II, Section 6, §1.

Article II, Section 7, §5 requires a Treasury Withdrawals action to designate one or more administrators responsible for monitoring how the funds are used and ensuring the deliverables are achieved.

In form, this requirement is met. The proposal names Toto Finance Inc. as the “named accountable project administrator” for the full term.

In substance, it is not met. Toto Finance is at once the Treasury Withdrawal Recipient, the administrator, the legal owner of the copper, the tokenization operator, the sole controller of the receiving wallet, and the payee of 2,640,000 ADA in development and project-management fees and of a margin on operating invoices. The proposal states that “A Toto-controlled receiving wallet is NOT independent escrow”. It states that the independent oversight provider “is to be appointed”, that a PwC member firm “is preferred but not engaged”, and that “PwC and Intersect are NOT on-chain recipients, current custodians or confirmed project partners”.

The wording of Article II, Section 7, §5 does not expressly require an administrator to be independent of the recipient. However, as Tingvard held in the “Eternl: Path to Sustainability - v2” ruling, the administrator role has a specific constitutional function, and it must have substance. Where the recipient is also the sole administrator and no independent check exists when the funds are withdrawn, the requirement is reduced to self-certification.

Every independent safeguard in this proposal is prospective. The oversight provider is unnamed and unengaged. The milestone acceptance on which the 2,640,000 ADA fee depends is to be performed by a party that has not been designated. There is no escrow, no multi-signature control and no external party with authority over the funds at the point of withdrawal. As Tingvard held in the “Alchemy by Sundial x Charms” ruling, a suggested or conditional administrator is not enough. The same reasoning applies to an independent oversight function that the action describes but does not designate.

This is especially material given the size of the request, which is more than nine times that of the self-administered proposal Tingvard found unconstitutional in June 2026, and given that a substantial part of the funds flows to the administrator itself.

Tingvard therefore finds that the proposal may satisfy the word “administrator” in a narrow formal sense, but fails to satisfy the substance and purpose of Article II, Section 7, §5.

Article II, Section 7, §6 requires that any ada held by an administrator prior to further disbursement to the Treasury Withdrawal Recipient be kept in separate accounts auditable by the Cardano Community, and that such accounts shall not be delegated to an SPO but must be delegated to the predefined abstain voting option.

This requirement is engaged. The proposal designates Toto Finance as administrator and states that it will hold “undisbursed Treasury ADA”. From those funds, amounts are to be paid out over the programme, including 2,640,000 ADA payable to Toto Finance itself upon milestone acceptance. As Tingvard held in the “Governance Incentives Framework 2026” ruling, the custody requirements cannot be avoided by directing an ongoing programme’s funds straight to an account controlled by the proposer. The proposal itself accepts that the no-SPO-delegation and abstain requirements apply.

The withdrawal destination identified in the action is a key-hash stake account controlled by Toto Finance. It is delegated to the predefined abstain voting option, but it is also delegated to a stake pool (pool17ahr5ygy48vpdfnatqn2z4wfu2te4quapk2yx3k50ce6kd7feg0). Tingvard confirmed this delegation on-chain at epoch 662, after submission. The same stake credential is also used by Toto Finance’s general payment address disclosed in the metadata, so the account is not a dedicated account established for the treasury funds.

A statement that the recipient will comply with the custody requirements does not satisfy Article II, Section 7, §6 where the account identified in the action does not comply. Tingvard therefore finds that the action fails to satisfy Article II, Section 7, §6.

Tingvard has considered Article I, Section 1, Tenets 7 through 10. The fee structure, copper price exposure, ADA/USD exposure, bridge risk and the possible use of the proposer’s own TOTO token in settlement are disclosed. Tingvard does not find a clear, objective failure under the Tenets on these points.
