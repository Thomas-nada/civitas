As a DRep, I cast a No vote on the proposed New Constitution Governance Action (NCGA) for the following procedural and substantive reasons: Article VIII(1) strongly encourages that constitutional revisions be proposed after broad and inclusive discussion within the Cardano community. In this NCGA, no such community-wide forum was convened for review. Key stakeholders—including many DReps, SPOs, and CC members—encountered the draft only at the GA submission stage, without prior structured review or opportunity to propose amendments. The Info Action phase, designed to allow open community feedback and catch issues before binding votes, was completely bypassed. Even post-submission, substantial feedback and improvement suggestions continue to flow in, indicating the process was rushed and incomplete. The draft constitution fails to require that Treasury Withdrawal Governance Actions (TWGAs) undergo review by the Constitutional Committee at the Info stage—removing a critical constitutional compliance checkpoint. It omits a clear requirement for “Auto-Abstain” from vote participation by any stake address that receives Treasury funds, which is essential to prevent conflicts of interest and uphold Treasury neutrality. Other important guardrails—such as auditability and transparency mechanisms—have been diluted, undermining enforceability. Although the phrase “encourages” in Article VIII(1) is not a binding mandate, it forms a key part of the intended context of the amendment process. Submitting a fundamental constitutional rewrite without observing this expectation undermines both the legitimacy and procedural integrity of governance. Given both procedural shortfalls and substantive omissions, this NCGA does not meet the minimum standards for legitimacy.
