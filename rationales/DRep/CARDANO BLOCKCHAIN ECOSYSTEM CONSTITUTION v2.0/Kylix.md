I concur with Philip DiSarro's vote and rationale on this proposal:
> To be honest, this isn't ideal from my perspective. The scope of changes proposed is too large. In the future I would prefer constitutional amendments to be extremely focused, ie. to change a single item. It's very hard to get community consensus on a bundle of proposed changes (ie. some voters might like one change, but dislike another and thus choose not to vote for it). The only way we can get rapid iteration is via many proposals that have small scope (if the change proposed is about a single item, and the majority of stake agrees with the single item change then it will be accepted).
Future amendments must be made considerably smaller and must focus on specific parts and sections of the constitution:
1. To avoid the situation Phil mentions where people will vote against the proposal even though they agree with some of the proposed changes due to other proposed changes they do not support
2. And to minimize the content to review in one single proposal, enabling DReps to focus and formulate more complete opinions on the material being voted on. 
> That said, redundancy in the current situation is so unbelievably bad (budget & withdrawals being separate proposals) and is wasting an immense amount of money & time for the ecosystem, that I am compelled to vote for this proposal simply because it removes the concept of budget info actions from the constitution.
Once more I agree with Phil here. The current budget process is overly complicated. Yes, separate information and withdrawal proposals should be a tool in our arsenal; No, they should not be forced on proposers. This causes considerably friction and delay in an otherwise already complex and bureaucratic process.
