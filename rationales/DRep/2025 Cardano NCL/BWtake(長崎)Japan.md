As a DRep, I cast a NO vote on the proposal to cap the Net Change Limit (NCL) at 200 million ADA.

Cardano is currently at a pivotal turning point, where multiple core technologies that constitute Cardano 2.0—such as Ouroboros Leios, Hydra, Midnight, Chain State, Bullet, and Atlas—are being launched simultaneously. During such an early-stage phase, it is essential not to evaluate each technology in isolation, but rather to enable integrated development with technical harmony. This requires a funding design that is both sufficiently sized and flexible.

From this perspective, the NCL proposal—which would set the annual treasury spending cap at 200M ADA—was, in my view, misaligned with actual funding needs.

First, the Cardano Treasury already sees annual inflows of around 350M ADA. Setting a ceiling at 200M ADA means utilizing less than 60% of available funds. This is not “fiscal prudence” but rather a limitation that risks missing growth opportunities.

Second, the outcome of the 2025 Budget Reconciliation showed that actual demand far exceeds 200M ADA. The total requested amount for proposals that received majority YES votes easily surpassed 300M ADA, demonstrating strong expectations across diverse implementing entities such as Pragma, DC Spark, and World Mobile. In a decentralized ecosystem, funding demands are naturally distributed, and a single rigid cap can easily become a bottleneck for growth.

Moreover, ecosystems like Solana and zkSync have already deployed hundreds of millions of dollars in growth funding, gaining a lead in both speed and capital strength. To remain competitive, Cardano must adopt an offensive investment posture, not a defensive one.

For these reasons, I voted NO on the NCL proposal.

This position is also consistent with my voting behavior during the 2025 Cardano Budget Reconciliation. I supported 137 out of 194 proposals (approximately 71%). The breakdown is as follows:

- Core (infrastructure, scalability): 41 proposals  
- Marketing & Innovation (RWA, stablecoins, integrations): 52 proposals  
- Governance Support (Voltaire, GovTool): 13 proposals  
- Research (long-term development and standardization): 12 proposals  
- No Category (regional initiatives like AIRA): 5 proposals  
- Advance Support (SPO engagement): 2 proposals  

All proposals were evaluated against the following six criteria:

1. Whether they advance the core technical foundations of Cardano 2.0 (e.g., Leios, Hydra, Midnight)  
2. Whether they strengthen decentralized governance, especially around GovTool  
3. Whether they connect Cardano to institutional use cases via RWA and stablecoins  
4. Whether they support public infrastructure and regional diversity, such as AIRA  
5. Whether they balance short-term feasibility with long-term sustainability  
6. Whether they present clear deliverables and metrics (contract-based clarity)  

The NCL proposal is not merely a financial parameter—it represents a political choice about Cardano's stance toward growth. I believe that Cardano should evolve into an ecosystem that balances harmony with competitiveness. For that reason, I advocate for strategic and flexible treasury execution, and I voted NO on this proposal.
