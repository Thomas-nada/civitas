## Vote: No

I do not support withdrawing 4,207,967 ADA for the Governance Incentives Framework in its current form. Research into governance incentives is useful, but this request commits a full year of funding before the detailed public cost basis and the rules for its paid pilot are settled.

Under the HOSKY Cardano First framework, the funding sequence and the evidence available to voters do not justify the commitment.

## Pillar analysis

### Governance Transparency (lead pillar)

The proposal includes seven milestones, public datasets, monthly reporting and a final financial report. Those are useful commitments. However, it explicitly keeps the detailed operational budget separate from the public proposal. Voters receive workstream totals, including 884,000 ADA for modeling, 472,000 ADA for administration and coordination, and 476,666.67 ADA for science and research. Those totals do not show enough about staffing, rates and allocated effort to assess value for money.

The 126,239 ADA audit and oversight allocation also needs more detail about who provides independent review, who approves spending and what happens when a milestone fails. Allocating money to oversight is only part of establishing an accountable delivery process.

### Economic Sustainability

The project has already mapped 49 related initiatives and acknowledges overlap with funded research. Its commitment to coordinate and return unused overlapping allocations is welcome. I would resolve that division of work before approving the full budget, so voters can assess the incremental work they are buying.

A smaller first stage could publish the evidence review, detailed budget, experiment design and independent oversight arrangements. The remaining work could then return for a funding decision against that evidence. I do not consider the current full-year commitment proportionate to what is settled today.

### Decentralization

Incentives can help participants who cannot afford to contribute without compensation. They can also reward readily measured activity or concentrate rewards among participants best placed to optimize for a formula.

The proposed pilot recognizes those risks and commits to publishing findings. Its eligibility rules, safeguards and implementation details can nevertheless change before launch, and its testing plan is a later deliverable. I would want the experiment's measures of success, distribution tests and stopping conditions available for review before funding the paid experiment. That is particularly important when the subject of the experiment is the behavior of the governance participants themselves.

## Risks I'm accepting with this No

### 1. Slower progress on compensation

Declining participation and contributor burnout may continue while another approach is prepared. I accept that cost in asking for a smaller, more clearly specified first stage.

### 2. Losing useful shared research

The dataset, models and public reporting could improve future decisions and reduce repeated work. A No may delay those benefits. The proposal can preserve that useful scope while making the cost basis and experimental controls reviewable before the larger commitment.

## Decision

Publish the detailed costs, settle the independent oversight and separate the initial research decision from the later paid pilot. This version asks the treasury to commit too much before those decisions are made. **No.**
