I will vote **No on this proposal in its current form**. I value the intention to connect Cardano with real-world assets, but I believe the costs and financial, custody, and implementation risks do not yet justify committing these treasury funds.

I would like to keep the conversation open and explore other opportunities involving copper and other metals in Latin America. Chile and Peru are among the world’s leading copper producers, and existing financing programs for small and medium-scale miners, such as those offered by ENAMI in Chile, could provide useful lessons for this discussion.

One avenue worth exploring is how tokenization could support this financing and improve coordination among producers, buyers, and financial institutions. Recording payment rights, improving payment traceability, and facilitating access to capital are applications we could evaluate through a limited pilot with identified participants and concrete needs.

In that context, I would prioritize assets originally issued on Cardano, with interoperability with Ethereum and other networks. The goal would be to develop RWAs that help address real business needs, generate transactions tied to productive economic activity, and expand Cardano’s use beyond speculation.
