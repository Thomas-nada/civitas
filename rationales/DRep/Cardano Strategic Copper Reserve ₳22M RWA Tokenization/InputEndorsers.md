Tokenized physical commodities - interesting! Thanks to the proposers for disclosing the costs and the risksof what they are promising. 

However, I find it rather surprising that a proposal asking for ₳22 million to tokenize copper does not seriously examine what happened with earlier attempts to tokenize gold and other physical commodities on Cardano. We have already funded some of these efforts through Catalyst. Shouldn't we first establish what worked, what didn't, and why, before committing a much larger amount to what is essentially another experiment in the same field?

Take [Kinka Gold](https://ssl4.eir-parts.net/doc/8746/tdnet/2896406/00.pdf), for example. It launched a gold-backed token on Cardano in cooperation with EMURGO in 2025. On 16 October 2026, its parent company announced that it was winding down its crypto-asset business. The Cardano-issued tokens had reportedly achieved no sales. 

Kinka did not receive Catalyst funding. But it is a very relevant Cardano RWA case that shows that real physical backing and even a well-known Cardano partner doesn't mean that there will be a market for the token.

There is also the [APEX tokenized gold project](https://projectcatalyst.io/funds/12/cardano-partners-and-real-world-integrations/tokenized-gold-on-cardano-with-the-apex-group), which received approval for ₳1.18 million in Fund 12, and the [metal and minerals RWA ecosystem](https://projectcatalyst.io/funds/14/cardano-use-cases-partners-and-products/cardanos-first-end-to-end-metal-and-minerals-rwa-ecosystem) funded with ₳550,000 in Fund 14. Neither had completed all its milestones at the time of this assessment. Where is the assessment of the progress of these aforementioned proposals, their difficulties, their actual commercial outcomes, and the possibility of building upon what has already been financed?

Imagine 3 houses being built in the same field. One was completed but nobody bought it, while the other two are still unfinished, well behind schedule. Would we invest in a much larger 4th house without first looking carefully at what happened with the previous three? The location, underground waters, the subsoil, the building quality, the market, the demand, availability of infrastructure and distance from grid ... something must be learned from all this before we start building again.

I would expect this to be among the first things addressed in a serious ₳22 million proposal. Instead, we get a rather extensive explanation of why copper and RWA tokenization are promising. 

Second, the economics do not look particularly attractive for the Treasury. Only 66.93% of the requested ADA would actually purchase copper. The other 33.07%, or approximately ₳7.28 million, is allocated to development, operations, oversight and other costs. The proposal states that copper would have to appreciate by approximately 49.4% in USD just to recover the original ₳22 million, assuming that the full expense allowances are used and ADA/USD remains unchanged.

The risks are real, especially with a mandatory liquidation within 36 months. 

Are we providing capital and covering a substantial part of the development costs of a private commodity-tokenization business, without even looking properly at the outcomes of earlier projects?

The canonical copper token being issued on Ethereum and then bridged to Cardano is not the most efficient and safest architecture for a Cardano-funded RWA project. Why introduce the additional risks of a bridge if the purpose is to demonstrate Cardano's own capabilities? And why route warehouse and insurance payments through Toto's own TOTO token? I mean... tokens .. .bridges... each new layer of complexity introduces another opportunity for something to go wrong.
