As a DRep, I decided to vote NO on the proposal: Cardano Strategic Copper Reserve | ₳22M RWA Tokenization

My rationale:

Cardano Treasury should fund public goods and ecosystem growth, not take a large, leveraged bet on the future price of copper.

Only ₳14.724M (67%) of the ₳22M withdrawal would buy copper. The remaining ₳7.276M (33%) is allocated to technology, operations, assurance, execution, and administration.

At the proposal's illustrative entry price of $6.73/lb, copper would need to rise to approximately $10.06/lb (around 50%) just to return the original ₳22M if the full budget is spent.

This is not a conservative strategic reserve. It is a high-cost commodity position whose principal recovery depends on substantial price appreciation.

In addition, the full amount would be transferred before the essential protections are in place. PwC is not engaged, the independent oversight provider is not appointed, the warehouse, auditor and legal structure are not contracted, and the bridge is not deployed.

The proposal correctly acknowledges that a Toto-controlled wallet is not independent escrow, yet this wallet is still the Treasury Withdrawal destination. That is a material contradiction.

Finally, the proposal primarily finances an Ethereum product. The canonical CuTT would be an ERC-20 token on Ethereum, while Cardano would hold a bridged representation. This adds bridge, custody, and smart-contract risk while weakening the case for a Cardano-native Treasury investment. Toto’s own public materials describe CuTT as Ethereum-based and still in development.

I support the broader goal of verifiable RWA infrastructure on Cardano. But this proposal should return only after it provides independent escrow, executed counterparties, legally enforceable Treasury rights over the collateral, milestone-based disbursement, and a genuinely Cardano-native architecture.
