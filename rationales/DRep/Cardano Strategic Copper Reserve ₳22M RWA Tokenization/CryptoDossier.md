I'm voting NO on the **"Cardano Strategic Copper Reserve"** Proposal. Here are my reasons:

**1: CuTT will Canonically be on Ethereum as ERC-20 token, not originally on Cardano:**
If Cardano treasury is funding the entirety of the process, including the cost of purchasing the actual copper and taking the risk of copper price exposure, then the tokenized copper should exist primarily on Cardano as a CNT and not a bridged token from Ethereum. The proposal frames this as a time-limited RWA tokenization initiative to demonstrate Cardano’s capability to handle real-world asset tokenization and to develop reusable tokenization infrastructure on Cardano. However, the canonical CuTT token is minted as an ERC-20 on Ethereum, with only a bridged representation issued on Cardano. This undercuts the stated purpose: it is not a pure demonstration of Cardano-native tokenization, and it risks signaling that Cardano is not considered suitable for direct issuance of such assets. A genuine Cardano-focused RWA demonstration would issue the token natively on Cardano rather than relying on an Ethereum primary issuance plus a bridge.

**2: Copper price and ADA/USD risk**
Copper has risen roughly 60% over the past two years. If that pace continues, it will imply around a 60% gain over two years and roughly 90% over three years. But this is not a reliable forecast, and commodity prices can reverse sharply. Under the proposal’s own assumptions (full budget spent and a constant ADA/USD rate), copper must rise from the illustrative entry price of about $6.73/lb to approximately $10.06/lb (roughly 49–50%) simply for the Treasury to recover the full ₳22 million. At current levels near $6.60/lb, that still requires substantial appreciation. In addition, the proposal’s break-even calculation holds ADA/USD constant. If the ADA price rises during the holding period, the same USD proceeds from selling the copper convert into fewer ADA, offsetting or eliminating copper gains. The Treasury therefore bears dual risk: copper must appreciate significantly and ADA must not rise enough to dilute the recovery. Principal is not guaranteed, and the combination of commodity-price exposure and FX risk places too much uncertainty on Treasury funds.
