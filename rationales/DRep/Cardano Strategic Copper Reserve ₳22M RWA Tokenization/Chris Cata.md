# Chris Cata

**Vote: NO**

**I support credible RWA tokenization on Cardano. I do not support this transaction.**

The Treasury is being asked for ₳22M, yet only ₳14.7M — about 67% — actually purchases copper. Under the proposal's own assumptions, copper would need to appreciate from approximately $6.73/lb to $10.06/lb, roughly 49%, simply to recover the original ₳22M value if the full budgets are used and ADA/USD remains unchanged. **That is an extremely high hurdle before the Treasury sees any economic gain.**

The risk/reward allocation is also difficult for me to justify. **Cardano supplies the capital and assumes the commodity, ADA/USD, custody, counterparty, bridge and liquidation risks**, while substantial funding goes toward Toto's technology, execution and project management. Toto can continue developing and commercially benefiting from its broader CuTT business, **while the Treasury receives no equity or revenue participation in that business.**

The control structure raises additional concerns. The full withdrawal goes to a Toto-controlled address even though the independent oversight provider, collateral arrangements and several implementation contracts are not yet finalized. The proposal does require protections before capital is deployed into copper, which is important, but for a ₳22M withdrawal I would prefer those protections and counterparties to be established before Treasury funds leave community control.

I also see no compelling reason to permit Treasury operating expenses to flow through Toto's own TOTO utility token when direct fiat settlement is available. That adds a related-party transaction and additional execution risk without providing an obvious benefit to the Treasury.

Finally, this single proposal would consume more than half of the roughly ₳42.6M remaining under the current Net Change Limit. Even if proceeds eventually return to the Treasury, they do not restore that withdrawal capacity during the current NCL period. That opportunity cost matters.

The problem is not copper, and it is not RWA tokenization. The problem is the transaction. A smaller, staged RWA implementation with independent custody and enforcement established up front, clearer Treasury ownership protections, and economics better aligned with the risks being taken would be much easier to support.

As proposals become more sophisticated, our scrutiny as DReps has to become more sophisticated as well. We are increasingly being asked to evaluate investment risk, legal structures, custody, related-party economics and Treasury opportunity cost—not simply whether an idea sounds beneficial to Cardano. Governance is learning, and the bar should get higher as we learn more.

**A good idea does not make bad terms acceptable. I am voting NO.**
