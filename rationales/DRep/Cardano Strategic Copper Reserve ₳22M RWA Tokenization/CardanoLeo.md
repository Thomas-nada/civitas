# Cardano Strategic Copper Reserve (₳22M): Why I’m Leaning Towards a **NO Vote**

**My position:** Leaning towards voting **NO**.

This proposal requests **₳22 million** from the Cardano Treasury to purchase physical copper and tokenize it through CuTT.

While I support exploring RWAs (Real-World Assets) on Cardano, after reviewing the proposal, I have **three major concerns** that make it difficult for me to support it in its current form.

---

### 1. Treasury Allocation and Priorities

* **Capacity Constraints:** Cardano’s current Net Change Limit is **₳500 million**, covering the period through July 2027.
* **Remaining Headroom:** As of mid-October, approximately **₳457.4 million** had already been used, leaving only **₳42.6 million** in withdrawal capacity. This single proposal would consume more than half of what remains.
* **The "One-Way" Limit:** Even if Toto Finance returns the unused funds and net profits within 36 months, those incoming funds will *not* restore the Net Change Limit capacity used by the withdrawal.
* **Timing & Priorities:** With other proposals of similar size also seeking funding, we need to think carefully about our priorities. Having a promising idea does not automatically make it the right time to fund it.

### 2. Lack of Independent Fund Management

This is my **biggest concern**.

* **Concentrated Control:** Toto Finance is both the proposer and the designated fund manager. This means the same party requesting ₳22 million would also control and manage the funds after withdrawal.
* **Industry Standards:** Other Treasury proposals have used arrangements involving the Cardano Foundation, Intersect multisig controls, and independent oversight committees.
* **Governance Concerns:** The underlying principle is simple: *fund management and oversight should not be concentrated in the hands of the party requesting the money.* Without sufficient separation of responsibilities, independent custody, and oversight, this arrangement raises legitimate governance and constitutional concerns that must be addressed before approval.

### 3. Financial and Technical Risks

* **The Break-Even Hurdle:** According to the proposal’s own calculations, assuming the full budget is spent and the ADA/USD exchange rate remains unchanged, copper prices must rise from **$6.73 to $10.06 per pound** (approximately **49.4%**) just for the project to break even.
* **Budget Breakdown:** Around **₳14.72 million** goes towards purchasing copper, while the remaining budget covers development, execution, and operating costs, including up to **₳441,732** in execution fees for Toto Finance.
* **Foreign Exchange & Principal Risk:** There is *no principal protection mechanism*. Even if copper prices rise, adverse changes in the ADA/USD exchange rate could mean the Treasury receives back fewer ADA than it originally invested.
* **Technical Readiness:** The proposal acknowledges that the Treasury-specific issuance infrastructure, Cardano minting policy, and independent security audit are *not yet completed*. The process involving Ethereum issuance, asset locking, and cross-chain bridging to Cardano still lacks a finalized, independently audited implementation. For a ₳22M withdrawal, these fundamental details should be established *before* the community votes, not after approval.

---

## My Conclusion

I support bringing real-world assets into Cardano, and I believe RWA deserves further development. However, this proposal currently combines three significant concerns:

* **Treasury constraints:** More than half of the remaining withdrawal capacity would be committed to one proposal.
* **Governance:** The proposer would also control the funds, without sufficient independent oversight.
* **Risk management:** No principal protection, with key technical infrastructure still awaiting independent security audits.

In my view, the risks are **too high** for the Treasury to accept under the current conditions.

I am open to reconsidering my position if the proposal provides stronger independent fund management, completed security audits, and a more convincing approach to Treasury allocation.

*This is my assessment based on the information currently available. Voting closes on **November 15**.*
