# **1.0 Introduction**

## **1.1 Summary**

We are voting **NO (remediable)** on the Cardano Strategic Copper Reserve, and the more interesting question this proposal poses is not whether to oppose it but what kind of opposition it warrants — whether what is wrong with it is a defect in the submission that a better submission could repair, or a defect in the thing being submitted that no revision of it reaches.

Some of what is wrong is plainly of the first kind. The governance action is anchored to off-chain content held at a mutable location, when the Constitution requires the document behind every action to be immutable, and when the proposal's own text tells DReps that an immutable, hashed final version will be the authoritative record. The document also carries the scaffolding of its own preparation — pre-submission checklists, a step-by-step GovTool procedure, and editorial instructions to its drafters — and several of the steps that scaffolding names as conditions of a credible submission were not carried out before the action went on-chain. The proposal, in effect, wrote its own falsifiers and then did not meet them. Each of these is a property of this action and each is repairable.

The deeper findings concern structure, and they are the reason this rationale takes care over the word *remediable*. The withdrawal pays the full ₳22 million at enactment to a wallet controlled by the recipient, which is also the action's designated administrator, the legal owner of the metal, the tokenization operator, the beneficiary of three separate fee lines, and the issuer of the utility token through which part of the treasury's storage costs would be routed. Every protection the document describes — the independent oversight provider, the collateral agent, the warehouse and title agreements, the legal opinions, the bridge audit, the reviewers who would approve the recipient's own invoices — is a future deliverable, and the failure path for each runs back through the party it would constrain. The proposal discloses this with unusual candor. Disclosure, however, is not a limit: an arrangement can be fully known and still unbounded. And beneath the custody question sits an asymmetry the document also states openly: the treasury would supply all of the risk capital that founds a private product, absorb every drawdown in copper, in the dollar and in ada, and receive none of the commercial return the product generates — while roughly a third of the withdrawal is spent before a single pound of copper is bought, so that the metal must rise by about half merely to return the treasury's ada.

Our answer to the remediability question is that the No is remediable in its purpose and not in its instrument. A demonstration of verifiable real-world-asset tokenization connected to Cardano is a legitimate thing for the commons to want, and there are ways to fund it that this framework could support. None of them runs through this structure. The specification at the end of this rationale is therefore not a list of patches; it describes two different proposals, either of which the proposer is free to bring.

The external fiduciary instrument this DRep uses for the capital-allocation axis reaches No on the same structure from an unrelated foundation, failing the proposal at the constitutional preflight and at most of the gates its systemic size invokes. Two instruments built on different ground arriving at the same verdict is worth naming as what it is.

## **1.2 Description of Governance Action**

This Treasury Withdrawal requests exactly ₳22,000,000, paid to a reward address controlled by Toto Finance Inc., a Delaware corporation based in New York that provides technology for the tokenization of physical commodities. Toto Finance is named as both the on-chain recipient and the accountable project administrator, and declares that it has received no ada from the Cardano Treasury in the preceding twenty-four months.

The funds are to acquire COMEX Grade 1 copper cathodes, held under Toto Finance's legal title in a COMEX-approved warehouse in the greater New Orleans area whose name is not publicly disclosed. The verified copper would be tokenized as CuTT, one token per attested pound, issued first as an ERC-20 on Ethereum; the treasury-financed tranche would be locked in an Ethereum escrow and an equal quantity of bridged CuTT minted on Cardano into a reserve wallet. The position is to be held for eighteen to twenty-four months, then liquidated over a further one to twelve months under a minimum sell-down schedule — at least a quarter of the pounds by month thirty, sixty per cent by month thirty-three, all by month thirty-six — with realised net sale proceeds and unspent budget returned to the treasury through the treasury-donation mechanism. No return of principal or profit is promised.

The ₳22 million is divided into five ceilings. ₳14,724,409, or 66.93 per cent, buys copper. ₳3,533,859 covers up to three years of warehousing, insurance, audits, legal and collateral work, bridge security review and an operating contingency, calculated at eight per cent a year on the copper principal; within it, Toto Finance may earn a thirty per cent margin on storage and insurance invoices, capped at ₳278,291, and those invoices may be settled by converting treasury ada into Toto Finance's TOTO utility token and then into dollars. ₳2,640,000, twelve per cent of the gross request, is paid to Toto Finance across five milestones for the token contracts, the Ethereum escrow, the Cardano bridge policy, reserve dashboards and an intended Reeve reporting integration. ₳441,732 is a one-time execution fee to Toto Finance of about three per cent of the copper principal. ₳660,000 is reserved for an independent professional oversight provider; a U.S. PwC member firm is named as preferred, and the proposal states repeatedly that no such provider has been engaged. Taken together, up to about ₳3.36 million of the withdrawal is payable to the recipient itself.

All commercial rights in CuTT outside the treasury-financed tranche — DeFi integrations, platform fees, licensing and the expansion of the token on other networks — remain with Toto Finance. The proposal's own sensitivity analysis shows that, with every fee ceiling spent and the ada–dollar rate unchanged, copper would need to rise about 49.4 per cent, from a reference of \$6.73 to about \$10.06 a pound, for the treasury to recover ₳22 million.

The proposal records Net Change Limit headroom of ₳42,604,371 for the period covering epochs 613 to 713; a public snapshot on 16 October showed the same figure. This request would consume about fifty-two per cent of what remains for the nine months left in the period.

# **2.0 Discussion**

## **2.1 Method**

Every governance action gets read; not every action earns the same reading. This DRep runs each one first through an intake that decides how much analysis it is owed and which instruments run on it, before any merits are read and before any standing policy applies. The intake returns a depth, never a verdict. The token is chosen last, under a discipline whose operative question is whether a warrant has been derived to deploy delegated stake against an action — not whether a vote helps or hinders its passage. 

This action earned the deepest reading the framework gives, and the reason is not its size or its subject. It is what happens if it goes wrong. Once the withdrawal is enacted, the whole amount sits in a private company's wallet, and every route back to the treasury — a halt, a refund, a claim on the copper, a recovery after a bridge failure — runs either through that company's cooperation or through a contest in a foreign court in which no one has yet been named to stand for the treasury. Losses realised on the metal or the bridge cannot be undone at all. An action whose failures are this hard to reverse is read in full whatever it is for.

## **2.2 What this proposal gets right, stated properly**

The proposal is honest about its arithmetic in a way few treasury requests are. It computes, and prints, the price at which the treasury breaks even, and it shows the losses at prices below that point without euphemism: at today's copper price the treasury would recover about two-thirds of its ada; at five dollars, about half. It says plainly that a bullish bank forecast of ten dollars a pound would still leave the treasury marginally short, that the forecast is for a quarter that falls well before the earliest sale date, and that rising ada can erase gains in copper. It tabulates bearish forecasts beside bullish ones. It declines, explicitly, to call itself the first treasury allocation with a return of capital, and names the precedent that makes the claim unsupportable. It refuses the vocabulary of guarantees throughout. When my own preliminary diligence on this action described it as exhibiting a high standard of drafting transparency, that was a fair reading, and it remains one.

The construction also contains real discipline. The holding period is bounded and the liquidation deadline is absolute, with a minimum sell-down schedule that narrows discretion as the deadline approaches rather than leaving the whole position to be sold in the final week. Unused and unearned funds are committed to return. The token supply is tied to verified net pounds rather than nominal contract lots, futures positions are excluded from backing, and double-counting across the Ethereum escrow and the Cardano representation is prohibited in terms. The technology fee is milestone-gated with a statement that market losses do not excuse non-delivery of funded work. The related-party economics — the execution fee, the technology fee, the invoice margin, the TOTO route — are disclosed rather than discovered. A reader who wants to know what this proposal would do can find it out.

The underlying idea is not frivolous either. Tokenized claims on physical assets are a real and growing use of public blockchains, Cardano's position in that market is modest, and a well-run, independently verified demonstration of the full lifecycle — purchase, attestation, issuance, reconciliation, sale and retirement — would be a useful reference for builders. The proposer's background was examined as part of this DRep's diligence and nothing in it carries this vote in either direction; the framework reads the structure of a claim, not the reputation of a claimant.

None of what follows disputes any of this. The difficulty is that every strength listed here is a strength of description, and the findings are about what the description describes.

## **2.3 The document that was meant to be submitted**

The first finding is procedural, and it stands independently of everything else in this rationale: it would carry a No on its own.

Article II, Section 6 of the Constitution requires each governance action to carry a link to a document giving its context, together with the document's hash, and requires that document to be immutable and incapable of alteration after submission. The off-chain record for this action resolves to a mutable location *\[[https\://totofinance.com/cardano-treasury-proposal-strategic-tokenized-copper-reserve/data.jsonld](https://totofinance.com/cardano-treasury-proposal-strategic-tokenized-copper-reserve/data.jsonld)\]*. A hash fixes the bytes that were hashed; it does not stop the content served at a mutable address from changing, and so it does not secure what a delegator or a DRep reading the action later will actually see. The requirement exists because a vote on a document that can change is a vote on nothing in particular. Whether this renders the action unconstitutional is for the Constitutional Committee to determine, not this DRep. What this DRep can say is that the requirement is not satisfied in substance, and that a treasury withdrawal of this size should not need a constitutional adjudication to establish what was voted on.

What makes the defect telling is that the proposal anticipated it. In its own anticipated questions, it asks whether the proposal can be changed after DReps vote, and answers that no material terms can be changed by a mutable web page, because an immutable and hashed final version is the authoritative representation of the action. Its submission checklist instructs that the final PDF and metadata be published at immutable, content-addressed URLs and the hash verified against the hosted bytes before the action is signed, and adds, correctly, that those URLs cannot exist until the final bytes are hosted.

That checklist is one of several passages that were evidently written for the people preparing the submission rather than for the people deciding it. The document carries a full GovTool submission procedure, down to connecting a wallet and keeping recovery phrases offline. It carries instructions to verify the receiving address in the signing wallet, to recheck the Net Change Limit before signing, and to seek DRep consultation before submission and revise and re-anchor the metadata if that consultation materially changes the proposal. In answer to whether this is the first treasury investment with capital returns, it instructs its reader to present the distinct structure and measured novelty instead of an unverified absolute claim — an editorial note to a drafter, left in the published text. Elsewhere, phrasing such as "none is falsely described as live or audited today" reads as a guardrail addressed to the drafting process.

I want to be careful about what this finding is. The use of drafting tools, including AI tools, is not a defect, and this DRep's own rationales are prepared with them; a criticism of that kind would have to be turned on this document first. What matters is that the submitted object should be the decision object — that the person signing an action has read, verified and stood behind what it says. Here the residue of preparation shows something more specific than haste. The checklist the proposer supplied is, in effect, a statement of what the proposer itself regarded as necessary for a credible submission: an immutable record, verified addresses, DRep consultation, and an independent administrator appointed or at least engaged. The record shows the action was submitted without the first and the last. We are not holding the proposal to this DRep's standard here. We are holding it to its own.

The same residue produces at least one internal contradiction that bears directly on the vote. In its description of participants, the proposal says that Intersect is an option for oversight only with Intersect's own agreement and is not an automatically appointed fallback. In its budget, it allocates the ₳660,000 oversight ceiling to the independent provider "if it accepts (otherwise preappointed Intersect MBO)." Those cannot both be the terms. A reader cannot tell from the document whether the treasury would, on a PwC refusal, have a designated fallback administrator or none at all, and nothing on the public record suggests Intersect has agreed to be either.

The repair for all of this is simple and entirely in the proposer's hands: an immutable anchor; a document addressed to its decision-makers rather than its drafters; and the steps the checklist names carried out before the action goes on-chain rather than after.

## **2.4 One party in every chair**

The second finding concerns who does what once the money moves.

Toto Finance would receive the ₳22 million directly at enactment, into a wallet it controls. It is the designated administrator — the role the Constitution gives the job of monitoring how funds are used and ensuring the deliverables are achieved. It is the legal owner of the metal and the depositor of record at the warehouse. It is the tokenization operator, controlling the Ethereum contracts, the escrow and the Cardano policy. It earns the execution fee, the technology fee and the invoice margin. It is economically related to the TOTO token through which part of the treasury's storage costs would be routed. It conducts the sale process through a committee whose independence is described but not constituted. And, under the budget's own terms, it "will appoint independent invoice and milestone reviewers before making the relevant related-party disbursements" — that is, the party being paid selects the people who will approve its payment.

The proposal is explicit that independent custody or third-party veto authority does not exist until separately implemented, and that the receiving wallet is not to be represented as third-party escrow. That candor is creditable. It also describes exactly the arrangement the Constitution's treasury provisions are built to prevent. Article II, Section 7 requires each withdrawal to designate an administrator to monitor use of funds, and requires ada held by an administrator before further disbursement to the recipient to be kept in separate, community-auditable accounts delegated to abstain. Those provisions presuppose a gap between the party that holds the money and the party that spends it — a moment at which funds pass from custody to use and someone other than the user is watching. When the administrator and the recipient are the same entity, there is no such moment. The custody protection has nowhere to bite, and the monitoring is the recipient monitoring itself. Whether a self-designated administrator satisfies the letter of Section 7 is again the Committee's question. In substance, the function the provision describes is designated and the independence it presupposes is absent.

Read against the standard this DRep applies wherever one party acquires authority over others' resources — is that authority matched by an accountability running in the same direction — the reading is a clear inversion. The treasury's ada becomes fully transferable to the recipient by a single vote; the recipient's subsequent discretion over that ada becomes answerable, until some unspecified future date, only to bodies the recipient itself will appoint. The repair is not more disclosure. It is an administrator that exists before the vote, is not appointed by the recipient, holds the funds, and can refuse a disbursement.

## **2.5 Disclosed is not the same as bound**

The third finding is the one the proposal's own virtues make easy to miss.

Read closely, almost every protective commitment in the document is conditional on something that does not yet exist. The first-priority security package over the copper, the warehouse receipts and the insurance proceeds is "to be perfected" before purchase. The collateral agent who would hold it is "to be appointed." U.S. legal opinions on the token, custody and sale characterisation are to be obtained before metal is acquired. The bridge and escrow are to be audited before launch. The independent oversight provider is to be engaged if it accepts. The controls on the TOTO conversion route must be operational before it is used. The proposal is unfailingly clear that none of these is in place.

What it is less clear about is what happens if they never are. The answer, assembled from its refund and failure provisions, is that Toto Finance "must halt the affected expenditures and arrange return of unused funds." The obligation is real. But the party that would recognise the failure, halt the spending and arrange the return is the party holding the funds, and the independent administrator the refund clause names as the one who "shall return uncommitted/unspent ADA" is the administrator that has not been engaged. If the security package is never perfected, the treasury's position against a Delaware corporation is that of an unsecured counterparty with no named representative — and, should the recipient become insolvent, a claimant in U.S. bankruptcy proceedings behind any secured creditor, with no one yet designated who has standing to make the claim. The limitation-of-recourse section, which is reasonable in its own terms, confirms that directors and shareholders give no personal undertaking and that bona fide market losses are not the recipient's responsibility.

The principle here is one this DRep's framework treats as load-bearing: a discretion that is disclosed is known, but not thereby limited. "We will appoint," "to be documented before procurement," "where feasible," and an operating contingency line of ₳1.09 million described as external collateral administration, valuation, reserve-assurance platform and contingency — each of these is published, and each can expand to cover whatever the future requires. The document's exhaustive risk disclosure does a great deal to inform. It does very little to constrain, because the constraints it describes are promises about arrangements the promisor will make.

The repair is to move the conditions from after the vote to before it. The controls that matter — the administrator, the collateral agent, the escrow arrangement, the warehouse and title agreements, the legal opinions — should exist, at least as signed or term-sheeted engagements, when DReps are asked to fund what they protect. Where that is genuinely impossible, the ask should be staged: a small first withdrawal that funds the legal, custody and engineering preparation and returns with executed terms.

## **2.6 Who carries the risk, and who keeps what it builds**

The fourth finding is economic, and it is where my preliminary diligence and the framework's reading converge most directly.

The treasury in this design is the sole source of risk capital and a first-loss holder on every axis the proposal names. It bears the copper price over thirty-six months. It bears the dollar, because the copper is bought and sold in dollars. It bears ada itself, because the result is measured in ada and the treasury holds ada: the proposal is clear that ada appreciation can turn a rise in copper into a loss. It bears the fee load, which is fixed and paid regardless of outcome, the bridge and escrow risk, the counterparty risk on the recipient, the warehouse and lien risk, and the slippage on the TOTO route. A third of the withdrawal is committed to costs before the position is opened, which is why the breakeven sits at nearly fifty per cent.

Against that, the treasury's upside is capped at the realised value of its own copper. The proposal reserves to Toto Finance every other economic return the reserve makes possible: the platform and integration revenue from CuTT, the fees from DeFi markets built on it, the licensing, the expansion of the token on other networks with separately financed metal, and whatever value accrues to a product that has been launched with ₳14.7 million of third-party capital behind its first reserve. There is no revenue share, no participation in the operator's economics, no discount, no equity or warrant, no stake in the TOTO demand the fee route creates, and no claim on the brand or the infrastructure beyond the treasury's own tranche. The proposal is again candid about this; it calls the carve-out a material related-party disclosure. The treasury would be paying to seed a private product and leaving every return except the return of its own capital, less fees, on the table.

The TOTO route deserves its own sentence because it is the clearest instance of the asymmetry. Storage and insurance are billed in dollars and paid in dollars. The proposal would nonetheless have treasury ada converted into a token economically related to the recipient, transferred to the recipient's settlement wallet, converted back into dollars, and paid out, with a thirty per cent margin retained by the recipient on the invoices so settled. The stated purpose is to demonstrate an on-chain utility path for custody charges. That demonstration has no evident value to the commons that a direct dollar payment would not provide more cheaply, and it routes public funds through demand for a related-party asset. The proposal's own fallback — direct fiat payment — is the right default.

Instruments of this kind exist and are ordinary. Where a public funder provides seed capital to a commercial product, it typically receives something for bearing the product's early risk: a share of revenue, an equity or token interest, preferential terms, or ownership of the asset it financed held in a vehicle that survives the operator's failure. The absence of all of them here is not an oversight; the proposal states it as a design choice. It is the choice a resubmission would need to reverse.

## **2.7 What the commons retains**

The treasury may fund the maintenance of commons relations; it may not fund the transfer of a private surplus. That is a test of the structure of a claim and not of the recipient, and a for-profit firm maintaining a good the ecosystem depends on passes it routinely. The question it asks is what capacity, security or standing the commons holds afterwards, stated as a relation rather than a quantity, and how its degradation would be recognised.

There is a private surplus here and it should be named without color: a tokenization firm would establish its flagship commodity product with public capital, keep the product's commercial economics, and earn fees for managing the public's position in it. Nothing in that is improper. It is exactly the circumstance in which the burden falls on the proposal.

The proposal's case for value is stated almost entirely in two currencies that do not discharge that burden. The first is positioning: the reserve would "help establish Cardano as infrastructure for the tokenization of the real-world economy," create "a potential new market," and give financial media "an intelligible global real-world-asset narrative." These are claims about attention and standing that would accrue if they accrue, not about a capacity the commons would hold. The second is the copper itself — pounds attested, analyst forecasts, scenario tables — which is a position, not a relation. The proposal is careful to say the copper is not a speculative price promise. But once the price thesis is set aside, as the proposal asks, what remains of the copper's contribution to the commons is that it is there to be tokenized.

The relational element the proposal does offer is real and should be credited: reusable tokenization infrastructure, a Cardano bridge policy, public reserve dashboards, metal-to-token reconciliation, developer documentation, and an auditable reporting integration. That is the kind of thing a commons can hold. It is offered, however, without the terms that would make it a holding. No licence is stated for any of the code. The canonical token lives on Ethereum, under Toto Finance's control, with Cardano receiving a bridged representation. The reporting integration is intended rather than agreed. The dashboards report on Toto Finance's reserve. A builder who wanted to reuse this infrastructure for a different asset would find, on the proposal's terms, that the right to do so has not been granted.

And here the translation request the framework makes in these cases does something unusual. Asked to restate the value relationally — what capacity the commons would retain, and how it would be recognised if lost — the honest answer is: open, reusable infrastructure for verified commodity tokenization on Cardano, and a public record of one full lifecycle run through it. Neither of those requires the treasury to own ₳14.7 million of copper through someone else's balance sheet. A demonstration of verified tokenization can be run on metal financed by the operator, by a smaller treasury-owned reserve, or by matched commercial capital, and the infrastructure can be funded as infrastructure. The relational account, stated properly, does not need the instrument that consumes two-thirds of the money. That is the finding that shapes the next section.

## **2.8 Remediable — and what remediation reaches**

The question of whether this No is remediable deserves its own section, because the framework's two species of No meet at it, and the honest reading has to hold that meeting open rather than resolve it by assertion.

A remediable No says the proposal failed a test it could have passed; the defect is in the submission, and the No is a specification. A terminal No says a commons relation is harmed and that no resubmission of the same action would cure it; it offers no path, because offering one would misrepresent the finding. Most of what is wrong here is unambiguously of the first kind. The anchor can be made immutable. The drafting residue can be removed and the checklist completed. An independent administrator can be engaged before the vote. Controls can be executed rather than promised. The TOTO route can be dropped.

The economic structure is harder, and it is where the seam lies. If the "same action" is read as its purpose — a verified demonstration of real-world-asset tokenization connected to Cardano, funded in part by the treasury — then it is remediable, because there are designs that serve that purpose without the defects found here. If the "same action" is read as its instrument — a treasury-financed directional commodity position, held on a private operator's balance sheet, with every commercial return reserved to the operator and the treasury's capital at first loss — then it is not, because each defect is a property the instrument exists to have. A revised version that gave the treasury ownership of its asset in a vehicle that survives the operator, a share of the economics its capital creates, staged exposure and an independent administrator would no longer be this instrument. It would be a different proposal that shares this one's aim.

This DRep reads the No as remediable, on the purpose reading, for two reasons that are stated before the direction of the vote is consulted. First, the species is defined by whether a commons relation is harmed, and the harms found here — custody without independence, risk without return, value claimed without rights granted — are each removable while the stated purpose is preserved. None of them is intrinsic to wanting a verified real-world-asset demonstration on Cardano. Second, offering no path would misrepresent the finding, because paths exist and can be named. But the seam is marked rather than papered over: the remediation this No specifies does not run through the present design. It runs around it.

Two paths are available, and either would be read on its merits. The first decouples the infrastructure from the position: fund the open, licensed tokenization and verification infrastructure as infrastructure, at a scale and on milestones appropriate to it, and let the operator capitalise its own reserve or bring matched commercial capital to it. The second, if the treasury is to hold a position at all, makes it the treasury's: a bankruptcy-remote holding arrangement with an independent trustee or administrator engaged before submission, title or beneficial ownership running to that arrangement rather than to the operator, staged releases gated on executed custody and legal terms, a defined participation in the commercial economics the reserve seeds, direct fiat settlement, and an investment case that names, rather than assumes away, the opportunity cost of holding copper against holding ada. Both are recognisably different proposals. Both are proposals this DRep would read without the presumption this one has earned.

I also want to be candid that the structure's defects interact with its scale. A small, staged, treasury-owned pilot with an independent administrator and the same disclosures would be a modest experiment whose failure would be cheap and whose lessons would be public. At twenty-two million ada and more than half of the period's remaining headroom, the same design is a large, concentrated and largely irreversible commitment. Some of what reads here as structural would read, at a tenth of the size and with real staging, as an acceptable cost of learning.

## **2.9 Why this is a No and not an abstention**

Three grounds for abstaining were live and each is foreclosed.

The first is that the ballot cannot carry what the analysis found. Some of it cannot: the questions in section 2.12, about whether the treasury should be an investor at all and on what terms, are about a class of action, and no single vote reaches them. But the findings that carry this token — an anchor that fails the immutability requirement, a recipient that is its own administrator, protections that exist only as future undertakings, and an economic structure that places all risk on the treasury and all commercial return with the operator — are properties of this action, stated on its face. Where the ballot reaches the finding, the ballot is owed the finding.

The second is jurisdiction: that the copper market and the mechanics of cross-chain escrow lie outside what this framework reads. Not knowing where copper will trade in 2029 is not a jurisdictional limit, and this rationale does not rest on any price view. The findings concern custody, accountability, rights and the structure of the claim, which the framework reads directly.

The third is that the disagreement is a contest at the margin between two honest framings of a stated relation. This is the reading most tempting on a proposal this careful, because its disclosure is thorough, its sensitivity analysis is rigorous and its tone is scrupulous, and it would be easy to read all of that as good faith contested only at the edges. But rigor about the price of copper is precision about the position, not evidence about the relation, and the relation, once stated, turned out not to need the position. That is not a margin. It is an undischarged burden with a translation that exposes the instrument.

What pulled hardest the other way should be said. A No on a proposal this transparent risks teaching proposers that candor is punished — that the ones who print their breakeven price get voted down while the ones who omit it slip through. I take that risk seriously. The answer is that transparency is what made this reading possible, and it is credited here in full; what it revealed is what the vote answers.

## **2.10 Fiduciary verification (external instrument)**

This DRep's own framework reads the relational and structural question and by design does not read the capital-allocation one — price, instrument fit, size-calibrated discipline, upfront exposure and opportunity cost. Leaving that region unexamined would raise vigilance rather than lower it, so the proposal was run through the DRep Treasury Rule Book v17 as a subordinate gate, one that can lower a merits-Yes but cannot raise a merits-No.

By economic substance this is a real-world-asset and tokenized-protocol proposal with a private commercial beneficiary, which the rulebook reads on its commercial, hybrid and infrastructure scorecard with custody, market, legal and systemic-risk overlays. At ₳22 million nominal it is systemic — the highest band — and the rulebook requires a systemic request to pass every systemic gate in addition to a score of ninety or above. It also states that any proposal-side failure of a systemic gate maps to No.

The gate fails before scoring, and at the first step. The rulebook's constitutional preflight treats mutable governance-action content as a material defect and maps it to No. The systemic gates then fail almost uniformly. Exposure is not staged: the whole amount is paid at enactment, against a normal upfront ceiling of ten per cent at this band. Independent pre-approval review has not occurred; every legal, custody, security and financial review is a post-approval deliverable. Separation of duties is absent, for the reasons in section 2.4. Enforceability and recovery depend on a security package and a collateral agent that do not exist. Portfolio resilience fails: a single action would take more than half of the period's remaining headroom with nine months to run. Executable controls — pause, replacement and recovery powers that can actually be exercised — are described but not held by anyone independent of the recipient. Two systemic gates are partly met: stress testing, where the proposal's sensitivity analysis is genuinely good; and public accountability, where the reporting plan is detailed though nothing is yet in place.

On the rulebook's instrument-fit guide, two warning signs appear in their plain form: a treasury paying for a company's growth while the company keeps all the upside, and treasury assets treated as a subsidy for a market position the treasury does not own. Its treasury-return category requires the maximum for a systemic commercial request, and a return of the treasury's own capital net of a one-third fee load with no participation in the commercial economics does not approach it.

So this is convergence at the conclusion rather than corroboration at a soft spot. A rights-derived reading and a capital-stewardship rulebook reach No against the same structure from different foundations, and meet at three points in particular: the immutable record, the separation of the holder of funds from their user, and the allocation of risk and return. Had the fiduciary read come back clean, the relational No would have stood unchanged; had the relational read passed and the fiduciary one failed, the allocation defect would have been dispositive. Here the gate's function is to sharpen the specification.

## **2.11 What would make this a Yes**

The specification describes the two different proposals from section 2.8, not an amended version of this one. Each item is cheaper to meet before a vote than after.

Make the record the decision. Anchor the action to an immutable, content-addressed document whose hash is verified against the hosted bytes, and publish a document addressed to its decision-makers, with the drafting procedure, checklists and editorial notes removed and the internal contradictions resolved.

Separate the holder from the user. Engage an independent administrator before submission — by name, with a signed engagement or binding term sheet, not appointed by the recipient — that holds the funds, approves every disbursement, appoints the invoice and milestone reviewers, and can refuse.

Execute the protections the design depends on. The collateral agent, the warehouse and title instruments, the security package and the legal opinions should exist when the treasury is asked to fund what they protect. Where they cannot, stage the ask: a small first action funding legal, custody and engineering preparation, returning with executed terms and a priced second tranche.

Then choose a structure. Either decouple — fund open, licensed tokenization and verification infrastructure as infrastructure, with the reserve financed by the operator or by matched commercial capital — or, if the treasury is to hold copper, make the position the treasury's: ownership or beneficial title in a vehicle that survives the operator, a defined share of the commercial economics the reserve seeds, staged exposure within the band's normal ceiling, and an explicit case for why holding copper serves the commons better than holding ada.

Grant the commons what it is said to receive. State the licence for every code deliverable and give the commons a durable right to reuse the bridge, escrow and reconciliation infrastructure for other assets and other operators.

Remove the related-party routing. Settle custody costs directly in fiat, drop the TOTO route and the handling margin, and denominate any fee on the work performed rather than the size of the withdrawal.

And size it to the moment. A request that would take more than half of the remaining headroom with three-quarters of a year to run should either show why nothing else in that period could use the capacity better, or come in smaller.

Do those, and either resulting proposal is one this DRep would read on its merits and could vote for.

## **2.12 What the vote does not reach**

A vote is a snapshot; the log is the trajectory. This proposal is a clear specimen of several tensions in how Cardano governs its treasury, and converting them into this vote would misrepresent both the proposal and the tensions. They are recorded here and held open, because none of them has a solution so much as a position the ecosystem occupies and can be watched moving along.

The first is between a commons and a portfolio. This is the another treasury action this year to treat the treasury as an investor with a return-of-capital component, such as the Draper Dragon fund enacted earlier in 2026\. Each such action invents its own investment terms — custody, risk limits, return mechanics, the definition of what comes back — because the ecosystem has no investment policy, no mandate defining what the treasury may hold or why, and no standard for how such proposals should be read. Without one, the treasury's risk appetite is set one proposal at a time by whoever brings the next one. Whether the treasury should hold positions at all is a genuine question, and I do not think it has a crisp answer; but it should be answered once, deliberately, rather than by accretion.

The second is between prudence and the ceiling, and it recurs from my OpenZeppelin rationale earlier this month. The Net Change Limit counts ada withdrawn, not value spent or lost. A return-of-capital proposal consumes headroom now for money that, if it comes back at all, comes back after the period has closed. Under a ceiling this close to exhaustion, that design crowds out work that would have been spent within it. This is a second concrete instance pointing towards the 4-5 competing proposals to reform the limit, which each reach in their own way for some reading of outturn rather than withdrawal.

The third is about how proposals are now made. Drafting tools make it cheap to produce long, comprehensive, hedge-saturated documents, and that cheapness transfers cost to readers: this proposal is twenty-nine pages, much of it repetition of the same address, the same disclaimer and the same conditional, and the decision-relevant facts are distributed through it. Exhaustive disclosure can function as insulation — every risk named, so no risk is owned. And drafting residue in a submitted document is a signal that the submitter's own verification step did not happen. None of this is an argument against tools, which this DRep uses. It is an argument for a submission standard that asks the submitter to certify what the tools cannot: that the record is immutable, that the document says what the action does, and that the steps the document says are necessary have been taken.

The fourth is administrator scarcity. Proposals increasingly name an administrator they have not engaged, or name one as a fallback without its agreement, because the supply of independent administrators willing to take on novel structures is thin. That is not this proposer's doing, and it is a real bottleneck. It is also the gap through which self-administration becomes normal. I will be watching for it across proposals.

Three smaller readings belong beside these. The vocabulary of a "strategic reserve," borrowed from sovereign stockpiling policy, describes the commons as a state balance sheet; that is a shift in how the ecosystem talks about itself, and it is worth tracking. The proposal frames the return of the treasury's own funds as a treasury "donation" — the technical name of the mechanism, but a name under which returning what is owed reads as generosity rather than repayment. And the pattern of claiming Cardano positioning for an asset whose canonical issuance lives on another chain, with Cardano holding a bridged representation, is likely to recur.

Two of these are candidates for a future proposal rather than a future vote: a treasury investment policy, or at minimum a standard for return-of-capital proposals; and a submission standard requiring an immutable-anchor verification, an engaged administrator and a document addressed to its decision-makers.

# **3.0 Conclusion**

We are voting **NO (remediable)**.

The proposal is candid, its arithmetic is honest, and the idea behind it — a verified, end-to-end demonstration of real-world-asset tokenization connected to Cardano — is a legitimate thing for the commons to want. The refusal is not of the idea, the candor or the proposer. It is of a record that fails the Constitution's immutability requirement and the proposal's own checklist; a recipient that would administer itself, hold the funds, appoint its own reviewers and rely on protections that do not yet exist; and an economic structure that places every risk on the treasury and every commercial return with the operator, at a fee load that requires copper to rise by half for the treasury merely to recover its ada. Run separately, the external capital-allocation instrument reaches the same conclusion against the same structure.

The No is remediable in purpose and not in instrument. A proposal that decouples open infrastructure from the reserve, or that makes the reserve genuinely the treasury's, with an independent administrator in place and protections executed before the vote, would be read on its merits. This one, revised at the margins, would not be different enough to be.

What I will carry forward beyond this action is the larger question it puts in front of the ecosystem: whether the treasury is to become an investor, and if so on what standing terms. That question deserves to be answered once and deliberately. This DRep will continue to maintain a state of inquiry around it and will bring it into proposal form when it is ripe.
