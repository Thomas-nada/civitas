I do not oppose Cardano’s research-driven culture itself. On the contrary, I believe peer-reviewed research and long-term technical investment are among Cardano’s core competitive advantages.

Many of the topics covered in this proposal — including Peras, Leios, post-quantum security, ZK infrastructure, and Babel Fees — are important for Cardano’s future, and I understand the need to continue investing in research. Completely weakening Cardano’s research capacity would, in my view, be a strategic mistake in the long term.

However, this proposal is extremely broad in scope and very large in size, requesting approximately 33 million ADA. Given the current Treasury situation and NCL environment, I believe moving forward with the entire proposal at once introduces too much risk.

In particular, the proposal combines a very wide range of workstreams, from early-stage exploratory research to areas that are much closer to implementation readiness. As a result, I believe it becomes more difficult to establish clear prioritization, accountability, and measurable impact.

For that reason, my concern is not with the existence of the research itself, but rather with the scale and structure of the proposal. I believe a more reasonable approach would be to narrow the scope toward the highest-priority areas and progress incrementally. For example, the most critical and implementation-ready workstreams could be prioritized first, while the remaining areas could be staged across future NCL cycles.

In my view, the challenge for Cardano today is not whether research should continue, but how to responsibly balance long-term innovation with Treasury risk management and ecosystem priorities under limited funding conditions.
