## Vote: No

I vote No on the 9,600,000 ADA request to build PFMS over 15 months. Under Cardano First, the proposal does not sufficiently justify having the Treasury fund the entire development cost.

## Pillar analysis

### Economic Sustainability (lead pillar)

The proposal assigns infrastructure, transaction fees and ongoing maintenance to the state, while the Treasury is the sole funder of development. That provides a route to continued operation, but leaves Cardano paying the full build cost for a system whose immediate operational beneficiary is the government. The case for that allocation needs stronger evidence of the value Cardano receives.

The projected 1.53 million mainnet transactions in the first year indicate potential usage. They do not establish economic return or justify the subsidy on their own. Transaction counts depend partly on batching choices, and network fees are not equivalent to direct Treasury income.

The commitment to return 70% of optional paid-verification revenue is positive. However, the proposal provides no evidenced demand forecast or expected annual revenue sufficient to assess its significance. I do not require every grant to repay its cost. Public infrastructure can justify funding through wider benefits, but those benefits must support the amount requested.

The budget maps broad allocations to defined work packages and includes a USD expenditure cap. It does not provide an independently verified cost benchmark or a sufficiently developed comparison with a smaller Treasury-funded scope, government cofunding, or a lower-cost architecture. I cannot conclude that the price is inflated; the value case for the full 9.6 million ADA remains insufficient.

### Adoption

The proposed ledger use has a legitimate purpose: evidence commitments can be checked against a public record beyond the originating institution's sole control. The reusable Apache 2.0 core, identity tools, contracts and verification framework could benefit further deployments.

Those are meaningful potential benefits. A ledger commitment still cannot establish that the original record was truthful or that every relevant record was captured. The proposal also needs a stronger connection between its usage targets, sustained institutional adoption and the resulting value to Cardano.

### Governance Transparency

The published work packages, independent assurance, security testing, milestone reports and final reconciliation are useful safeguards. The mainnet and open-source gate, followed by three months of sustained usage, provides concrete acceptance evidence.

The full withdrawal first passes to the administrator, with subsequent disbursements governed by contractual gates. Assurer procurement begins after the kickoff release. Up to 5.8 million ADA can be disbursed before the mainnet and public-code gate. These controls reduce delivery risk, but do not resolve whether the full subsidy is an appropriate use of Treasury funds.

## Risks of voting No

A No may delay or lose a useful government deployment, reusable public infrastructure and future network activity. Potential verification revenue and further adoption may also be forgone. I accept that opportunity cost because the current proposal has not sufficiently substantiated the value of the funding requested.

## Decision

The open-source commitment, legitimate evidence-integrity use case and delivery safeguards deserve credit. The case for the Treasury bearing the entire development cost is not strong enough. I therefore vote No on this request.
