# RCADA

**Proposal:** PFMS Bringing Government Scale Adoption to Cardano by Trivolve Tech
**Vote:** Abstain
**Voter ID:** `drep1yf7kjx5rlyuyzxkr4dzyccdfs8tesh29amrtcpyplu3asnqjgmdz3`

---

RCADA recognises the significant potential of the Police Force Management System (PFMS) to demonstrate government-scale adoption of Cardano while addressing a meaningful challenge within the criminal justice system: establishing reliable, independently verifiable records of evidence integrity and custody.

We acknowledge Trivolve Tech's existing Forensic Management System deployment on Cardano, which provides a foundation for the proposed expansion. The ambition to develop a reusable, open-source evidence infrastructure capable of serving public institutions also aligns with our support for practical blockchain adoption and lasting ecosystem value.

The proposed allocation of 70% of paid-verification revenue to the Cardano Treasury, alongside government-funded operational costs, represents a constructive attempt to connect public investment with ongoing economic benefits.

However, RCADA has identified several important questions that remain insufficiently resolved for us to provide an affirmative vote on the requested ₳9.6 million Treasury withdrawal.

Our first concern relates to the proposed government deployment commitments.

The proposal states that the relevant Telangana government agreements exist, have been reviewed by the Cardano Foundation, and cannot be publicly disclosed due to confidentiality requirements. We recognise that sensitive government arrangements may require confidentiality.

Nevertheless, given the significance of these commitments to the proposal's viability, we would welcome independent confirmation of their scope, enforceability, duration and the government's obligations regarding deployment, transaction fees, maintenance and ongoing infrastructure costs.

Our second concern is the relationship between PFMS and India's existing evidence-management and criminal justice infrastructure.

We would welcome a clearer explanation of how PFMS will integrate with established national and state systems, including e-Sakshya, and how its verification processes will satisfy the applicable legal requirements governing electronic evidence.

These are particularly important considerations for a system intended to support police investigations, prosecutions and court proceedings, where technical reliability, legal validity and procedural fairness must work together.

Our third concern relates to the proposed funding structure.

Although we welcome the appointment of the Cardano Foundation as administrator and the use of milestone-based disbursements, the initial ₳1.8 million kickoff payment represents 18.75% of the total Treasury request.

This substantial advance would be released before acceptance of the first development milestone, while the independent assurer is expected to be appointed following commencement.

We would therefore appreciate greater clarity regarding the contractual protections, independently verified prerequisites, recovery mechanisms and remedies available to the Treasury should subsequent delivery fail or the project be unable to proceed as intended.

We also encourage further substantiation of the projected 1.5 million annual Cardano transactions, the anticipated revenue from paid verification services and the proposed improvements in case-resolution efficiency.

These outcomes could create meaningful value, but their assumptions and measurement methods should be transparent and independently assessable.

RCADA's decision to ABSTAIN reflects the project's promising potential alongside these material unanswered questions.

We do not oppose the underlying initiative. Indeed, the prospect of providing government institutions with reliable, transparent and verifiable evidence-management infrastructure represents a compelling application of blockchain technology.

However, the scale of the requested investment requires proportionate assurance that the institutional commitments, legal integration, implementation safeguards and expected benefits are sufficiently established.

Our governance philosophy is grounded in the generation and regeneration of environmental, social and economic sustainability. We welcome initiatives that can improve institutional accountability, strengthen public trust and contribute to Cardano's long-term economic resilience.

We encourage Trivolve Tech and the proposed administrator to provide further clarification of the identified concerns, particularly the government agreements, integration with existing justice systems and protections surrounding the initial advance payment.

RCADA therefore ABSTAINS, offering these observations as constructive feedback rather than opposition to the project's objectives.

We remain supportive of responsible government adoption initiatives that combine demonstrable public benefit with transparent governance, accountable funding and sustainable long-term value creation for the Cardano ecosystem.
