I am voting No on the 9,600,000 ADA PFMS proposal.

Trivolve and Quixy have already received Catalyst funding for the Cardano integration of their Forensic Management System, which is recorded as complete and now has a live government deployment. PFMS builds on that work, but the proposal does not sufficiently distinguish previously funded capabilities, reusable components, genuinely new development, and customer-specific integration costs.

My principal concern is funding responsibility. The proposal places the development programme on the Cardano treasury while the government customer funds infrastructure, transaction fees, and ongoing maintenance. Successful initial funding and a proven customer relationship do not establish an ongoing obligation for ADA holders to finance a private company’s product expansion. The open-source commitment is positive, but the additional public value has not been sufficiently separated from the commercial and customer-specific benefits.

I also do not consider the supplied evidence sufficient to establish readiness for the proposed state-scale deployment. Important questions remain around source-data completeness, device and officer identity, key compromise, offline evidence integrity, physical custody, and the distinction between an immutable hash record and a legally defensible chain of evidence. Existing transaction activity does not resolve those requirements.

Cardano Foundation administration, milestone assurance, and proposed revenue sharing are meaningful safeguards. They do not, however, establish proportionate value for the requested expenditure or demonstrate demand for the paid verification service. I therefore do not support this withdrawal.
