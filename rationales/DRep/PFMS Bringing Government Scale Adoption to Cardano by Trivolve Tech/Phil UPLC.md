## Why we are voting NO on PFMS

We are voting **NO** on the **9,600,000 ADA treasury request for PFMS**, Trivolve Tech’s proposed police evidence management system. Our judgment is that the proposal has not demonstrated enough value to Cardano to justify the treasury funding the full development cost.

Our platform places the burden on applicants to demonstrate demand, delivery, and value to ADA holders. PFMS deserves credit for its existing delivery record, proposed open-source release, independent milestone assurance, and government participation. These are meaningful strengths. The funding decision still requires a convincing explanation of why this use of community capital deserves priority.

### 1. The treasury’s responsibility for the entire development bill has not been justified.

The proposal assigns the full software build to the Cardano treasury. The government would fund infrastructure, transaction fees, and ongoing maintenance, with maintenance payments going to Trivolve and its subcontractors under a separate agreement. That is a meaningful government contribution, but it leaves ADA holders financing the entire development programme. [Source: full proposal, §5.4.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Trivolvetech-PFMS/pfms-proposal/914e75f9fe2d2a6cdfe8599af7279b8e3d062fcf/PFMS_Treasury_Withdrawal_Proposal.pdf)

**A customer’s willingness to operate a system does not, by itself, establish that ADA holders should pay to build it.**

We need a stronger explanation of how development costs should be shared among the government customer, commercial beneficiaries, and Cardano.

Open-source licensing and independently verifiable records can be requirements of a customer-funded or jointly funded project. If treasury funding secures additional benefits—broader reuse, a commitment to Cardano, or favourable revenue-sharing terms—those benefits should be identified and valued against alternatives requiring less treasury support.

The relevant question is how much additional public value this subsidy buys.

### 2. Transaction commitments need an economic valuation.

Trivolve has recently offered to put a minimum of 1.53 million first-year transactions, followed by annual growth for ten years, into its administrator contract. That strengthens the offer. The proposed government payment of network fees represents real demand for Cardano’s services.

However, a transaction target does not establish the value of a development subsidy.

We still need to assess the resulting fee income, durability of usage, value of the reusable infrastructure, and benefits to the wider ecosystem relative to the capital committed. A system can generate substantial, legitimate activity while delivering an insufficient return on the resources required to create it.

**We should evaluate what the activity is worth to Cardano before paying millions of ADA to enable it.**

### 3. The verification revenue model still requires validation.

The proposal commits 70% of optional paid-verification revenue to the treasury and 20% to Cardano library maintenance. Trivolve has also described automatic revenue allocation and independent reconciliation. These commitments improve accountability.

The remaining question is the amount of revenue customers will actually generate.

The founder has published a financial scenario based on assumptions about case volumes, paid uptake, verification frequency, and average prices. Those assumptions need evidence that users will purchase this particular optional service at the proposed scale, together with a credible explanation of when those payments will arrive. [Source: the founder’s revenue scenario](https://x.com/Rahulkonudula/status/2116280960828543145) and [subsequent clarification](https://x.com/Rahulkonudula/status/2117009144372764811).

A mechanism that distributes revenue correctly cannot establish customers’ willingness to pay.

Our platform requires that the economic case be sufficiently evidenced before committing treasury funds. The existence of a financial model is a starting point; its consequential assumptions still need validation.

### 4. The milestones leave a material gap between successful delivery and demonstrated economic value.

The published schedule would release **5.8 million ADA cumulatively through M2**, approximately **60.4% of the request**, while the system is being demonstrated on preprod. **7.8 million ADA, or 81.25%,** would be released through M3, before the final sustained-use milestone.

These payments are associated with substantive engineering work, security testing, deployment, and usage requirements. Funding development before production is normal. The concern is that the published acceptance criteria do not require a minimum level of paid-verification uptake or a minimum amount returned to the treasury. [Source: work packages and milestone acceptance criteria; percentages calculated from the published tranches.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Trivolvetech-PFMS/pfms-proposal/914e75f9fe2d2a6cdfe8599af7279b8e3d062fcf/PFMS_Work_Packages_and_Milestones.pdf)

The programme could therefore satisfy its published delivery milestones while the financial assumptions used to justify funding remain unproven.

That does not automatically disqualify a public good. It means the independently useful infrastructure must justify the expenditure even under a weak revenue outcome. We do not believe that case has been established sufficiently for this request.

### 5. Open-source benefits must be proportionate to the cost—and compete with our stated priorities.

We support funding shared infrastructure. That includes work whose value comes from security, developer productivity, or widespread reuse rather than direct revenue.

PFMS’s proposed reusable components deserve consideration. The funding case should clearly identify which components other builders or institutions will use, what evidence supports that demand, and how much of the budget is necessary to deliver those shared benefits.

Publishing useful code does not settle the appropriate size of the grant.

Our platform prioritises stablecoins, liquidity, lending, trading, wallets, bridges, risk tools, and infrastructure that strengthens Cardano’s financial ecosystem. We also support security and developer infrastructure with clear network-wide benefits. That creates an opportunity cost: every allocation reduces the capital available for other work.

**A proposal outside our highest-priority areas must make a persuasive case for the value it delivers per ADA committed.** We do not believe the present PFMS request clears that bar.

### What would justify reconsideration?

A revised proposal would be stronger with:

- A clearly costed separation between reusable infrastructure and deployment-specific work, with an appropriate contribution from customers or commercial beneficiaries.
- Independently reviewable evidence supporting paid demand and a realistic year-by-year cash-flow model.
- Publicly attestable customer obligations, final contractual commitments, and clear consequences if promised outcomes are not achieved.
- Funding conditions that test the economic assumptions—or a sufficiently strong public-infrastructure case to justify the expenditure without relying on those assumptions.

Our responsibility is to decide whether the evidence warrants committing this amount of treasury capital.

We do not need to prove that PFMS will fail to conclude that the present funding request has not met our standard. On the evidence available, the full development subsidy remains insufficiently justified.

**Our vote is NO.**

**Fund the proof, not the promise.**
