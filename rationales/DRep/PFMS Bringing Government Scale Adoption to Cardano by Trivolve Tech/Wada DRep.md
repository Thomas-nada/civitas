# Wada DRep

**Proposal:** PFMS Bringing Government Scale Adoption to Cardano by Trivolve Tech
**Vote:** No
**Voter ID:** `drep1y2jnqfamg4tq5aepxhggzhqg7pq5gunph4wypkyh9q3qt2cru5m2j`

---

As Wada DRep, we vote no on this governance action.

We recognise the strategic ambition behind the proposed Police Force Management System (PFMS) and its potential to demonstrate Cardano's capabilities in supporting large-scale government infrastructure and real-world institutional adoption. Bringing public-sector systems onto Cardano, particularly infrastructure that supports evidence management, verification and accountability within the criminal justice system, represents an interesting opportunity for ecosystem growth. However, the strategic importance of the proposed initiative does not, on its own, justify the requested treasury expenditure of ₳9.6 million. Our assessment identifies several outstanding concerns regarding financial justification, implementation readiness, adoption projections, accountability and long-term sustainability that have not been sufficiently addressed.

First, we have concerns about the budget justification and value for money. Although the proposal provides a breakdown of its expenditure across infrastructure development, engineering, systems integration, testing, security and project management, the level of detail is insufficient to independently establish the reasonableness and proportionality of the requested allocation. Given the magnitude of the funding request, we expect a more comprehensive cost justification, including personnel requirements, engineering costs, subcontracting arrangements and the distinction between reusable ecosystem infrastructure and government-specific implementation expenses. We believe that treasury-funded initiatives of this scale must demonstrate clearly how the requested resources translate into tangible, reusable benefits for the Cardano ecosystem.

Second, we are not sufficiently convinced by the projected adoption and transaction volumes. The proposal anticipates approximately 1.53 million annual transactions, supported by a substantial deployment involving government facilities, personnel, devices and evidence records. While these projections are ambitious, the performance of the existing Forensic Management System (FMS) raises questions that require further consideration. The previous deployment provides a relevant reference point for evaluating the team's adoption assumptions, and the differences between earlier expectations and reported usage should be reconciled before relying on substantially larger projections for PFMS. Expanding the system's scope and securing additional government interest are important developments, but they do not independently establish that the projected levels of operational adoption and transaction activity will materialise.

Third, we have concerns about implementation readiness and delivery risks. The proposed system involves multiple technical components, integrations and operational dependencies across five stages of the criminal justice process. Delivering this infrastructure within 15 months requires significant coordination between the development team, government institutions, technology partners and other stakeholders. Although the proposal identifies an existing technical foundation and outlines its intended architecture, further evidence is needed to demonstrate that the proposed implementation scope, integration requirements and delivery timeline are sufficiently achievable. In particular, the system's security, evidence integrity, identity management, offline functionality and integration with existing government systems require rigorous independent validation.

Fourth, we consider the accountability and treasury management arrangements to require greater clarity. The proposed milestone-based disbursement structure and independent assurance mechanisms provide a framework for oversight. However, the initial release of ₳1.8 million before the independent assurer is established raises questions about the sequencing of financial safeguards. We also expect clearer, independently verifiable documentation concerning treasury custody arrangements, milestone acceptance criteria and the respective responsibilities of the administrator, proposer and independent assurer. For a treasury withdrawal of this magnitude, accountability mechanisms should be demonstrably operational before substantial funds are released, rather than relying primarily on arrangements that will be established after funding begins.

Fifth, the proposed economic sustainability and revenue-generation model requires stronger supporting evidence. The proposal anticipates generating revenue through paid evidence-verification services and allocating 70% of the resulting revenue to Cardano Treasury, 20% to core Cardano library maintenance and 10% to Trivolve Tech. While this arrangement offers a potential mechanism for generating recurring ecosystem value, the projected revenue depends on assumptions about verification demand, uptake, pricing and institutional usage. These assumptions have not yet been sufficiently demonstrated through independently verifiable evidence. Furthermore, projected future revenue should not be treated as a substitute for establishing the financial viability and value for money of the initial treasury investment.

Finally, we must consider the opportunity cost of allocating treasury resources. A request of ₳9.6 million represents a substantial commitment of community funds that could otherwise support multiple initiatives across different areas of the ecosystem. We therefore expect a particularly strong demonstration of public-good value, financial proportionality, implementation certainty and measurable ecosystem benefits. The potential of government-scale adoption should be pursued, but the funding model must ensure that the Cardano community receives a clearly justified and proportionate return on its investment.

In conclusion, we support the broader objective of expanding Cardano's presence in government infrastructure and enabling real-world institutional adoption. However, we do not believe that the current proposal sufficiently demonstrates the financial justification, adoption certainty, implementation readiness and accountability standards necessary to warrant the requested ₳9.6 million treasury withdrawal. Our position is therefore to vote no on this governance action. This decision reflects our assessment of the current funding proposal and its supporting evidence, rather than opposition to the underlying concept or the development of government-scale infrastructure on Cardano.
