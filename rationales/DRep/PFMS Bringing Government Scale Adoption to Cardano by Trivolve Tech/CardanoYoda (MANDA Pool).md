As a DRep, I have decided to vote NO on the proposal: PFMS: Bringing Government Scale Adoption to Cardano” by Trivolve Tech.

My rationale:

First, I commend the team for building and operating a solution on Cardano. However, after reviewing the proposal and following the discussion, I do not believe it is ready for approval in its current form.

The proposal needs stronger evidence and a more transparent budget.

The Catalyst Pilot 2026 offers up to ₳200,000 per project and requires teams to substantiate their applications. A proposal requesting ₳9.6 million directly from the Treasury should meet at least as high a standard of scrutiny. I am concerned by the imbalance between the evidence expected under these funding mechanisms.

DReps have raised many questions, with answers scattered across X and other discussions. I appreciate the team's efforts to respond, but the information needed to evaluate a request of this size should be presented clearly in the proposal and its supporting documents.

For example, I would like to see the FTEs and person-months budgeted for each area of ​​PFMS, the rates for different roles, and how the work and budget are distributed among Trivolve, Quixy, and other vendors. Without this information, I cannot reliably assess whether the requested amount is reasonable.

The relationship between previous funding and the new work needs clarification.

The team previously received ₳550,000 through Catalyst for its Forensic Management System (FMS) project, which Catalyst records as complete. The new PFMS proposal covers a broader scope and a different state, so additional funding is not automatically unjustified. Nevertheless, the team should clearly explain which existing components will be reused, what new work is required, and why that work warrants a substantially larger investment.

The proposal states that the government will cover transaction fees, infrastructure, and maintenance after deployment. I welcome that commitment. However, if the government commissions the system and benefits directly from it, I would also like to understand why it is not contributing to development costs. A co-funding arrangement could demonstrate shared commitment and reduce the burden on the Cardano Treasury.

Previous adoption projections need a clear explanation.

The original Catalyst proposal anticipated approximately one million cases and a similar number of Cardano transactions annually. The current PFMS proposal reports more than 156,000 FMS transactions over its lifetime and approximately 11,000 per month. At that reported monthly rate, the system would generate around 132,000 transactions per year, well below the original projection.

Transaction counts alone do not tell us why the projection has not been reached. Before supporting another large investment, I would like the team to explain the difference and show how it has accounted for that experience in its new projection of more than 1.5 million transactions annually.

The team also has an ongoing Catalyst project.

IndianChain is a separate project focused on agricultural systems in Telangana. Catalyst records ₳570,000 allocated to it, with ₳460,000 distributed and four of six milestones completed at the time of review. The project is still listed as in progress.

Having more than one funded project is not inherently improper. However, before supporting a much larger Treasury request, I would like to see a clear account of progress across the team's existing commitments and an explanation of the resources available to deliver PFMS alongside them.

I encourage the team to use the feedback received during this governance process to strengthen the proposal and resubmit it with a more detailed budget, clearer evidence of government commitment and co-funding, an explanation of previous adoption results, and responses to the main questions raised by DReps.

If you'd like to support my work, consider delegating to the MANDA pool and backing me as a DRep. Your support is the only way I can get time for governance.
