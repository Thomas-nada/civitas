# Mike Rogero (羅邁凱）

**Proposal:** PFMS Bringing Government Scale Adoption to Cardano by Trivolve Tech
**Vote:** No
**Voter ID:** `drep1y2lhe7wn2sr6vme8gnca4jrkhv47uh3tklsykmrqvwzdq4g5s8wju`

---

I found this proposal quite difficult to vote on due to competing beliefs about what I want for Cardano, what I would like to see in a proposal, and the reality of what was actually proposed.

I have multiple biases that affected my vote, so let me name them up front.

I believe blockchain’s best use case is as a trust engine that takes the place of trusted third parties between entities that don’t know or don’t trust each other, but can all accept the impartial and trustworthy nature of the underlying code and chain.
I believe the best way to grow Cardano is through high-stakes, high-value, real-world interactions and transactions that have historically struggled with a lack of trust between the parties, where we can replace the friction caused by that lack of trust with trustworthy systems.
I believe those who gain value from these systems should pay for them.
After a career spent in systems integration, typically involving new technologies previously untried by clients, I understand the importance of a low barrier to entry to get the client onboard, and that the biggest frictions and costs of a project are typically those associated with changing organizational behavior and adopting new technologies.
I tend to default to no on anything EMURGO supports due to their poor multi-year track record in project selection and implementation.
I see the Cardano Foundation as also having a checkered track record in project selection.
I have read a number of opinions from other DReps, especially dori, Yuta, CardanoYoda, and Jonahakoch, whose perspectives stood out to me and informed my thinking.
Regarding my main beliefs, infrastructure is critical and should be a priority for Treasury funds, but we have already seen that just building plumbing alone is not enough to trigger adoption. Cardano needs to find high-value use cases where the parties involved intrinsically do not trust each other.

Victims of crime vs. criminals vs. the state vs. individual police officers meets my criteria for high-impact, high-value situations where the parties naturally cannot completely trust each other. I have firsthand experience of how backward the Indian police system can be, especially regarding records protection and filing, which also inclines me to vote yes on this proposal.

I also see that if this project succeeds, it could become a valuable use case that leads to similar projects around the world. The needs are global, and a success here could be replicated elsewhere.

I also believe the focus on fees paid to the chain is not a comprehensive enough metric to justify the almost all-consuming attention it has received in many of the critiques from DReps voting no. I agree that the value earned from fees is unlikely to justify the Treasury request. The value I’m looking for is in replicable models that demonstrate how Cardano can solve real-world problems involving trust, creating opportunities for adoption far beyond the initial project.

However, I have conflicting beliefs that make supporting this project difficult:

Those who benefit should pay. That the Indian government isn’t taking any of the upfront project risk is something I can’t swallow. I can completely understand subsidizing their implementation, but the deal structure doesn’t demonstrate that the client has evaluated the risks, costs, and benefits of the implementation in the way that would be implicit if they were having to foot part of the bill. DReps don’t have as good a view into the end value that will be generated as the actual customer. If the customer isn’t willing to pay anything for the development, and will only use it if it’s free, that is a red flag I found hard to get past.

The implementation team is covering all of its costs upfront through payments from the project, and I don’t see this being structured as an innovation contract where the team is tied to the long-term success of the project. It is structured as a one-off IT development project, with handsome payouts coming early in the project lifecycle. I’d like to see a deal structure that clearly ties the team’s success and payout to the long-term success of the project. That isn’t how this is structured, in my view. This is a second red flag.

The third red flag is that the Cardano Foundation, which is a key sponsor, is acting as an outsourcing partner and taking its fee. I would have liked to see CF putting some of its own skin in the game, tied to the success or failure of the project, rather than essentially taking a commission as a coordinator.

The Treasury is the entity taking all of the risk here. I want to see projects where the Treasury takes part of the risk, but holds a minority position, while other players, closer to the outcomes, take the major part.

Ultimately, the deal structure is what makes this proposal too difficult for me to support even though I see the value proposition. The client isn’t paying anything beyond a usage fee, while the developers and coordinators are getting paid upfront. All of the risk falls on the Treasury, which is the party most removed from the value and costs of the project.

This makes me believe the risk profile is too lopsided, protecting the interests and lowering the risks of those closest to the value proposition, while being paid for entirely by the entity furthest removed from the calculations of that value.
