Vote: No.

PFMS is a treasury request, so I reviewed it as a business case.

1. Competition. India's Home Ministry already runs e-Sakshya for evidence capture, in use at 15,899 police stations across 35 states and UTs (MHA, Feb 2026). The proposal does not address it.

2. Demand. BSA s.63(4) requires a hash certificate for electronic evidence, not a blockchain, and I found no Indian ruling accepting a public-chain hash as that proof. The only path back to the treasury is optional paid verification. The proposal has no volume forecast; the forecast later posted on X assumes 290,000 cases a year with 20% paying, while Telangana registered 228,695 cases in all of 2025.

3. Verifiability. The state agreement is under NDA, I found no public statement from the state government, and the milestones measure transaction counts, not usage or revenue.

I would reconsider if a public summary of the state's commitment were released and the final tranche were tied to paid-verification usage.
