**Vote:** Yes

I vote Yes. PFMS extends a system already running on Cardano mainnet (FMS) to all five stages of the chain of evidence, with milestone-gated payments, the Cardano Foundation as administrator, open-source code and a state government that covers the costs afterwards.

Rationale Statement

The proposal requests 9,600,000 ADA, in five tranches over 15 months, for Trivolve Tech, with Quixy (Vivid Minds Pvt Ltd) as subcontractor, to design, develop and deploy the Police Force Management System (PFMS). PFMS is an open-source evidence layer that fingerprints every police and judicial record and timestamps it on Cardano mainnet across the five stages of the chain of evidence: pre-crime evidence, crime investigation, evidence storage (Malkhana), prosecution and the courts. Its first deployment will be in the Indian state of Telangana. I support the proposal for five reasons.

The problem is critical and the solution fits existing law. When police records are managed by the same department that creates them and uses them to prosecute crimes, defense attorneys need only raise a reasonable doubt about potential tampering, and when the prosecution cannot disprove it, authentic evidence is frequently declared inadmissible. According to the proposal, cases in the state currently take an average of more than 7.5 years to resolve. The solution also fits a law that is already in force: since 1 July 2024, the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam treats electronic records as primary evidence (Section 57) and, when such a record is submitted as evidence, requires a certificate whose prescribed form includes the hash value of the file (Section 63). The law does not require a blockchain, but a system that automatically produces that fingerprint and anchors it in a record not controlled by whoever holds the evidence matches precisely what the law already asks for. The adoption of technological innovations tends to accelerate when they can be coupled to rules that already exist.
There is a proven track record on Cardano mainnet. FMS, the forensic component, has been live on mainnet since September 2025 with the Uttar Pradesh police. In June 2026, according to the Cardano Foundation’s monthly update, it became the first enterprise-grade project on Cardano to pass 100,000 transactions, and it now exceeds 156,000, at a rate of roughly 11,000 per month. These are entries generated by real police stations and forensic laboratories, in cases that end up before a judge, and the state has run FMS on its own infrastructure for over a year. IndianChain, which brought an entire Indian state government’s agriculture sector onto mainnet in September 2026, has already processed more than 15,000 transactions. The team also discloses six Project Catalyst engagements (Fund 12 and Fund 14, about 1.49 million ADA), five completed and one in active delivery.
The design keeps sensitive data off-chain and gives both parties the same evidence trail. Only cryptographic fingerprints, combined into Merkle roots, are committed to Cardano: no file content or personal data goes on-chain, no sensitive evidence leaves the department, and personal data stays in existing systems (CCTNS, NAFIS and PIS). The store-and-forward architecture, which signs and queues records in numbered order, means that a network outage never breaks the chain and a deleted record leaves a visible gap, while the count of expected records in each batch catches silent hardware failures. When a record is later used in court, the prosecution and the defence receive the same evidence trail, confirming whether it matches, what changed if it does not, and whether the officer held valid authorization when it was stamped.
The structure of the withdrawal limits the Treasury’s exposure. Tranches for Milestones 1 to 4 are released only after the deliverables have been verified by a competitively procured independent assurer and by the Cardano Foundation as administrator, and reported to the community. Funds are held in the administrator’s multi-signature wallet, which is not staked to any stake pool and is delegated to the predefined auto-abstain DRep. The amount is fixed in ADA, expenditure is hard-capped at USD 0.30 per ADA (USD 2,880,000), and any value above that cap and any unspent ADA are returned to the Treasury, while undisbursed tranches are not released if a milestone is not delivered and not remediated. The proposal sets out its compliance with Article II, Sections 6 and 7, of the Constitution and with guardrails TREASURY-02a and TREASURY-03a. The Treasury funds a one-time build: transaction fees, infrastructure and maintenance thereafter are paid by the state government. At the planning rate of USD 0.23 per ADA, the request amounts to USD 2,208,000 for a system that, in its first deployment, will connect around 3,500 facilities, 1.81 million devices, 113,000 personnel and 3.2 million evidence items in a single chain of evidence.
The proposal makes Cardano an integral component of a state criminal justice system. PFMS is projected to generate more than 1.5 million mainnet transactions in its first year and to register around 5.1 million decentralized identifiers. Because the core is open source, it can be extended to the rest of India’s states and union territories without additional build costs to the Treasury, and its potential use cases reach beyond India and police work. The proposal maps this contribution to Pillars 2, 4 and 5 of the Cardano 2030 KPIs and Strategic Pillars. In addition, 70% of the revenue from the paid verification service will be returned to the Treasury and 20% will go to core Cardano library maintenance.

Conclusion

I vote Yes. The proposal funds the extension of a system that already runs on Cardano mainnet, addresses a critical problem in the chain of evidence with a design that keeps sensitive data off-chain and fits Indian evidence law, and limits the Treasury’s exposure through milestone-gated payments, an expenditure cap and the return of unspent funds.
