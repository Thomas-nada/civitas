Vote: YES

This is a Treasury Withdrawal, "PFMS: Bringing Government Scale Adoption to Cardano by Trivolve Tech" (gov_action1wfy3vzxurfgq6wrpxg2qdx3t6wm7ng8xwr26h47kxa8xhd20cr5qqz0wzfc), submitted October 21, 2026 by Trivolve Tech and co-signed by the Cardano Foundation as named administrator, with voting closing November 20, 2026 at the end of epoch 669. Trivolve requests ₳9,600,000 over 15 months — $2,208,000 at the $0.23 per ADA planning reference, with expenditure hard capped at $0.30 per ADA and any value above that cap returned to the treasury — to build and deploy the Police Force Management System, an open source evidence layer that fingerprints and timestamps police records on Cardano mainnet across all five stages of a criminal case, with the state of Telangana, India as first deployment: approximately 3,500 facilities, 113,000 personnel, 1.81 million devices, 3.2 million evidence items and roughly 5.1 million decentralized identifiers. Only hashes reach the chain; no evidence files or personal data leave government systems. No ADA burning is involved.

The accountability structure is among the strongest Dracula DAO has reviewed. The Cardano Foundation holds funds in a multi-sig wallet that is unstaked and delegated to the auto-abstain DRep, so treasury capital cannot influence governance or stake distribution while held. Payment is split into five tranches and milestones 1 through 4 release only after deliverables are verified by a competitively procured independent assurer and reported publicly through a milestone dashboard. Undisbursed tranches are withheld on unremediated milestone failure, fraud, or scope abandonment, and unspent ADA returns to the treasury under a published final reconciliation. The economics also point the right way: the deliverable is Apache 2.0 licensed, so the remaining 35 Indian states and union territories — and justice systems elsewhere — can adopt it with no further cost to the treasury; the state government, not the treasury, pays every mainnet transaction fee in perpetuity and funds operating infrastructure and maintenance; and 70% of paid verification revenue returns to the treasury with a further 20% directed to core Cardano library maintenance. A government embedding a verification layer into its criminal justice workflow is a ten-year commitment, not a campaign, and the counterparty keeps paying protocol fees through every market cycle. That is the ultra long-term posture this DREP exists to defend. Trivolve has shipped on Cardano twice: the Forensic Management System has run on state infrastructure for over a year at roughly 11,000 mainnet transactions per month, and IndianChain brought a state agriculture sector onto mainnet in September 2026.

The concerns are substantial and Dracula DAO names them plainly. ₳1,800,000 — 18.75% of the ask — is released at contract signing before any deliverable exists; payment should follow delivery, not precede it, and this DREP objected to the same 20% kick-off in the OpenZeppelin withdrawal. The entire ecosystem-benefit case rests on a projection of 1.53 million transactions annually, roughly eleven times the current FMS run rate of about 132,000 per year, and the same team's earlier FMS projections were materially higher than what FMS actually delivered; DReps are being asked to fund the larger system on a forecast whose predecessor missed. The state government contributes nothing to development while the treasury funds 100% of capital expenditure, and Trivolve collects the ongoing maintenance contract from the state under a separate agreement — ADA holders de-risk a vendor's government relationship and the vendor keeps the recurring revenue. The project agreements that underpin the "low" adoption risk rating are confidential and have been seen only by the administrator, so the community cannot verify the commitment it is being asked to rely on. Paid verification revenue is speculative: whether Indian prosecutors and defense counsel will pay $1.89 and upward per request is unknown, so the 70% treasury share may be close to zero. At 1.5 million transactions, protocol fees are on the order of ₳300,000 a year — real, recurring, and paid by someone other than the treasury, but roughly 3% of the withdrawal annually. And ADA trades near $0.154, well under the $0.23 planning rate, so the treasury pays in its weakest currency and the team absorbs the shortfall.

Dracula DAO votes YES. Treasury funds should be grants paid on completion of well-defined features, with undelivered money returning to the treasury. This proposal is a single pre-selected team funded against a line-item engineering budget, and Dracula DAO states that gap directly — it is a partial approximation of the model, not the model itself. But the approximation is close: the administrator is an independent entity with no delivery stake, verification is performed by a third-party assurer procured competitively, the dashboard makes acceptance public, and the treasury's maximum loss on failure is the tranches already released. Of the real-world adoption cases the treasury has been offered, this is the most concrete: a working predecessor already in production, a government counterparty that pays the fees, and an open source core that other jurisdictions can deploy without returning to this treasury.

This vote funds delivery, not ambition. Dracula DAO will judge any follow-on request from Trivolve Tech entirely on milestone evidence: if record definitions are not fixed at M1, if stages one through three are not proven on preprod at M2, or if penetration testing does not precede mainnet at M3, continuation should be opposed. The 18.75% unearned kick-off payment should not reappear in future Cardano Foundation administered proposals. And where the treasury funds the build for a deployment from which a vendor earns a maintenance stream, future proposals of this shape should route a share of that stream back to the treasury as well.
