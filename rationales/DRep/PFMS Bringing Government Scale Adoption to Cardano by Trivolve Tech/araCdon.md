PFMS — Eight Governance Concerns Requiring Clarification Before Approval

araC’don | Independent Cardano DRep | 20 November 2026

Trivolve Tech requests 9,600,000 ADA from the Cardano Treasury to develop the Police Force Management System (PFMS), initially intended for deployment by the government of Telangana, India. Quixy (Vivid Minds Pvt Ltd) is a subcontractor, while the Cardano Foundation is the designated administrator.

Beyond the proposed technical benefits, eight governance concerns require clarification before community resources are committed.

1. Transparency of beneficiaries and previous Treasury funding

The disclosure covering the previous 24 months concerns Trivolve Tech and Quixy but does not provide the Cardano Foundation's history of Treasury withdrawals.

Yet the Cardano Foundation is the designated administrator, and 200,000 ADA are allocated to administration, including administrative expenses and related costs.

Who are the actual economic beneficiaries, how is this allocation distributed, and what disclosure obligations apply to each party? [1]

2. Access to essential contractual provisions

The agreements involving the government of Telangana are described as confidential and have reportedly been reviewed by the Cardano Foundation.

A separate legal contract between Trivolve Tech and the Cardano Foundation is also envisaged.

How can DReps assess financial guarantees, liability, recovery mechanisms, and contractual obligations without access to the essential provisions, at least in a suitably redacted form?

Confidentiality should not prevent informed governance decisions. [2]

3. Delegation of effective Treasury oversight

The community authorizes the withdrawal, but the Cardano Foundation and an independent assurer verify milestone completion and determine whether subsequent payments may be released.

Who resolves disagreements concerning milestone acceptance? Who can suspend payments? What independent remedies remain available to DReps after approval?

Administrative delegation should not reduce community oversight to a single initial vote. [3]

4. Asymmetry between collective financial risk and private remuneration

An initial tranche of 1.8 million ADA is scheduled for release upon signing the administrator's contract, before the first technical milestone is accepted.

Staggered disbursements limit future exposure but do not guarantee recovery of funds already paid.

Trivolve Tech receives development funding through the Treasury-financed project, 10% of paid verification revenue, and separate maintenance fees from the state government, shared with its subcontractors as applicable.

Meanwhile, the Treasury receives a proposed 70% share of paid verification revenue, without any guaranteed minimum.

What enforceable protections ensure that collective financial exposure is proportionate to the economic benefits and rights received by the Cardano community? [4]

5. Network capacity and shared infrastructure costs

PFMS projects more than 1.5 million annual mainnet transactions, with potential expansion to additional Indian states and other jurisdictions.

Although the government would pay transaction fees, these payments do not automatically demonstrate that all network externalities have been assessed.

Even cryptographic fingerprints require transaction processing, block capacity, bandwidth, and storage resources.

What independent scalability assessment demonstrates that large-scale expansion will not disproportionately affect other Cardano users, stake pool operators, or infrastructure costs? [5]

6. Constitutional purpose, fundamental rights, and the limits of cryptographic evidence

PFMS proposes an integrated evidence infrastructure connecting surveillance cameras, automatic number plate recognition systems, police records, forensic facilities, prosecution, and courts.

Cryptographic integrity does not establish the truthfulness, completeness, or lawful collection of original evidence.

An immutable record of inaccurate or unlawfully collected information does not make that information accurate or lawful.

What independent legal and human-rights assessments have been conducted?

What safeguards prevent disproportionate surveillance, institutional misuse, or the creation of an infrastructure that strengthens the traceability of police records without equivalent protections for the individuals concerned?

How can affected individuals challenge inaccurate evidence and obtain effective remedies?

The use of local software agents does not, by itself, establish autonomous AI decision-making. Any AI-specific risks should be assessed against the actual system architecture.

The constitutional question is not merely whether Cardano can support this infrastructure, but whether financing this particular application is consistent with the community's principles and responsibilities. [6]

7. Projected government-scale adoption versus enforceable deliverables

The proposal emphasizes state-scale adoption, approximately 5.1 million decentralized identifiers, and more than 1.5 million projected annual transactions.

However, the final milestone M4 requires at least 390,000 transactions over three months and explicitly concerns the first participating districts, not full statewide deployment.

The community may therefore approve funding based on government-scale ambitions while the final payment conditions require only partial deployment.

Which projected benefits are binding deliverables, and which remain long-term expectations?

These distinctions should be explicit before the vote. [7]

8. Incomplete economic disclosure and reputational exposure

The proposed revenue allocation — 70% to the Cardano Treasury, 20% to core Cardano library maintenance, and 10% to Trivolve Tech — applies to the optional paid verification service.

The proposal does not establish a guaranteed volume of paid verifications or a minimum financial return to the Treasury.

Furthermore, the value and duration of the separate maintenance agreements are not fully disclosed.

Technical milestones could therefore be completed without generating significant verification revenue for Cardano.

The open-source software may provide genuine ecosystem value, but this should be distinguished from measurable financial returns.

Financing large-scale policing infrastructure also raises reputational questions for an ecosystem built around decentralization, individual responsibility, and community governance.

These risks deserve independent assessment before public Treasury resources are committed. [8]

---

Conclusion — Who Bears the Risk, and Who Controls the Benefits?

Who funds the project? Who makes the decisions? Who controls the disbursements? Who bears the losses? Who receives the revenue?

Technical performance, constitutional compliance, financial accountability, and fundamental rights are separate requirements. None can be assumed from the others.

The Cardano Treasury is being asked to commit 9.6 million ADA to a project combining public infrastructure, private commercial interests, and institutional oversight.

Milestone-based funding limits future exposure but does not guarantee recovery of capital already committed.

Likewise, alignment with Cardano 2030 strategic objectives does not, by itself, establish that a particular application deserves Treasury financing.

Before approval, the community should receive independently verifiable information concerning essential contractual safeguards, economic arrangements, administrator responsibilities, fund recovery mechanisms, network impact, and fundamental-rights protections.

The burden of demonstrating these safeguards rests with those requesting Treasury funding.

Securing evidence does not establish the truth of its contents.

Securing is not controlling.
