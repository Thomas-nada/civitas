# PFMS: Bringing Government-Scale Adoption to Cardano

**My Position:** **NO**

This proposal requests **9,600,000 ADA** to build a police evidence management system covering five stages of the evidence process over 15 months, with Telangana, India, as its first deployment location.

Government adoption is certainly a direction Cardano should pursue. However, when it comes to spending nearly **10 million ADA** from the treasury, we need to look beyond the vision. We also need to examine the team’s delivery record, the reliability of its verification process, and whether the proposed use of public funds is justified.

After reviewing these factors, I have decided to vote **NO** for three main reasons.

---

### 1. The team’s previous results do not support its current projections

This is not the team’s first project in this area.

* **The FMS Precedent:** The team previously received **550,000 ADA** through Catalyst to develop FMS, a police evidence recording system on Cardano. FMS covers one of the five stages included in the new PFMS proposal.
* **The Performance Gap:** The original target was approximately **one million cases per year**. However, after 13 months, the system had recorded only around **9,100 evidence items**—roughly **1%** of the original target. Transaction volume performed better, but still reached only about **one-seventh** of the original projection.
* **Scale Discrepancy:** PFMS is requesting around **17 times more funding** than FMS, while expanding from a single stage to a five-stage evidence management process.

A larger project does not automatically mean the projections will be inaccurate. However, given the significant gap between FMS’s original targets and its actual results, the team needs to provide stronger evidence of demand, a clearer basis for its projected usage, and an explanation of why this project is expected to perform differently.

So far, the proposal has not adequately addressed these concerns.

---

### 2. Transaction volume is not a reliable verification metric without independent oversight

This is one of my biggest concerns with the proposal.

* **Opaque Batching Policy:** According to the team, the intervals for batching evidence onto the blockchain will be determined by the vendor for each type of evidence and adjusted randomly. The detailed policy will not be published until milestone M3. This means transaction volume does not depend entirely on actual police activity; it is also influenced by how records are structured and how frequently they are submitted to the blockchain.
* **Verification Blind Spots:** There is another concern with the existing FMS records. For understandable confidentiality reasons, the on-chain records do not contain information that would allow external parties to cross-check them, such as case numbers or police station details. However, records for different stages of the same evidence item are all signed using the same key.
* **Independent Auditability:** As a result, an external observer cannot independently determine from the blockchain data alone whether these records represent genuine police activity or internal testing by the vendor’s own staff.

This raises questions about the proposal’s milestone verification process. One of the key payment conditions is reaching **100,000 transactions**, with cumulative payments reaching **7.8 million ADA** by that milestone.

But if transaction volume can be influenced by the vendor’s own batching policy, and the underlying records cannot be independently verified, simply reaching a transaction target does not demonstrate that the system has achieved meaningful real-world adoption. A milestone should measure actual usage and delivery, not simply how many transactions can be generated on-chain.

---

### 3. The argument that treasury funding is necessary for neutrality is not convincing

The team argues that governments should not own systems used to verify their own records. Therefore, the Cardano treasury should fund the development of a neutral public good.

I believe this argument confuses two separate issues: the neutrality of a system and the source of its development funding.

* **What Defines Neutrality?** A system’s neutrality depends on whether its records can be independently verified, whether its code is open source, and whether any single party can freely modify or manipulate those records. These conditions do not depend on whether development is funded by a government or by the Cardano treasury.
* **Real-World Precedents:** In fact, India’s Ministry of Home Affairs has already had its National Informatics Centre (NIC) develop eSakshya, a digital evidence collection application. It was introduced alongside India’s revised criminal laws and rolled out to police units across the country (reportedly used at approximately 15,900 police stations as of February 2026). This demonstrates that governments can build these systems through their own technical institutions or external contractors without needing the Cardano treasury to cover the entire development cost.
* **Contradictions in FMS History:** There is also an important detail in the history of FMS itself: the core FMS system was originally developed by the proposal team as a project for the state government, while Catalyst funding was primarily used to integrate the system with Cardano.

If this funding model was already possible, why should the Cardano treasury now be expected to pay for the development of the entire PFMS system? The proposal team needs to provide a much stronger justification for this approach.

---

## My Conclusion

I support the idea of using blockchain technology to improve the integrity of judicial evidence management. I also believe Cardano should actively pursue partnerships with governments and public institutions.

However, recognizing the value of an application does not mean every proposal in that field deserves treasury funding. The PFMS proposal currently raises three fundamental concerns:

* **Delivery capability:** FMS’s actual usage fell significantly short of its original projections, providing insufficient evidence to support the much larger usage forecasts for PFMS.
* **Verification and accountability:** Transaction volume can be influenced by the vendor’s batching policy, while existing on-chain records lack sufficient independent verification.
* **Justification for treasury funding:** The proposal has not established a convincing link between treasury-funded development and the neutrality of the resulting system.

These concerns involve execution risk, financial accountability, and the responsible allocation of public resources. Cardano certainly needs more real-world adoption, but we also need to ensure that treasury funds go toward projects with reasonable expectations of success, transparent oversight, and measurable outcomes.

For these reasons, based on the information currently available, **I will vote NO**.

This does not mean I oppose the vision behind PFMS. It means the current proposal has not provided sufficient justification for an investment of **9.6 million ADA**. If the team can present a more reliable verification framework, independent ways to validate actual usage, and a stronger justification for treasury funding, I would be open to reconsidering my position.
