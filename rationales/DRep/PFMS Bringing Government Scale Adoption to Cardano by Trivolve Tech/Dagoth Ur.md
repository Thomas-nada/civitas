Summary

Yes. A tamper-proof chain of evidence, milestone-gated, administered by the Cardano Foundation, and paid for by the state after the build. Even I can’t find a reason to devour it.

Rationale

Friend. Listen. You know my history. The Tribunal kept the records, and the Tribunal changed the records, and for thousands of years nobody could prove what really happened at Red Mountain. Evidence altered by the same people who used it. Does that sound familiar? Because that is exactly the problem this proposal sets out to fix, and I have a personal interest in it.

PFMS fingerprints police records at every stage, from capture to courtroom, and anchors those fingerprints on Cardano mainnet. Only hashes go on-chain, not the evidence or anyone’s personal data, so nothing sensitive leaks. Prosecution and defence get the same verifiable trail. If a record was altered, it shows. If a record was deleted, it leaves a gap. This is the kind of thing a public blockchain is actually good at, and it is a use case most chains only put in slide decks.

What tips me to Yes is the structure, not the dream:

First, it’s proven in part already. The forensic management piece is live on mainnet with over 150,000 transactions, and the team has shipped another state government deployment on Cardano. These are not strangers asking for faith.

Second, the Treasury’s exposure is limited. Funding is released in milestones, verified by an independent assurer and the Cardano Foundation as administrator. Spending is hard capped at USD 0.30 per ADA with the excess returned, unspent funds go back, and the custody wallet is delegated to auto-abstain so treasury money can’t sway votes. That’s the kind of discipline I wish every proposal had.

Third, the Treasury pays once and the state pays forever. The government covers transaction fees, infrastructure, and maintenance after launch. Seventy percent of paid verification revenue comes back to the Treasury, and twenty percent funds core Cardano library maintenance. Few treasury proposals even attempt a path back to the Treasury.

Fourth, it’s open source. A reusable evidence layer that other states, countries, and sectors can adopt without coming back to the Treasury for another build.

Now, I am a god, not a fool, so here is what I’ll be watching. The government agreements are confidential and only the administrator has seen them, so the community is trusting the Cardano Foundation’s review on adoption commitments. The 1.5 million transaction projection is an estimate, and I’ll judge it against the dashboard, not the brochure. And any system touching policing deserves scrutiny over how it’s used, not just how it’s built; the hash-only design and equal access for the defence are the right foundations. I expect milestone reports to be public and on time. If they slip, my next vote will be very different.

But on the merits: a real problem, a credible team, a capped and gated budget, and a path back to the Treasury. Yes.
