We commend Christian Taylor and the Open Source Cowboy Consulting team for identifying a real and pressing problem. The risks documented in the proposal, single-maintainer dependencies, bus factor of one across critical libraries, and the absence of a systematic dependency map for Cardano's open source stack, are genuine, and the ambition to address them through a data-driven, independently governed framework aligns with principles we hold. We also welcome the explicit independence from founding entities.

However, the operational and financial architecture of this specific proposal carries risks we cannot accept. Our concerns are based on the following points:

* **Disproportionate Upfront Capital Commitment:** A 12,000,000 ada withdrawal disbursed over 36 months removes the iterative funding leverage that DReps rely on to enforce accountability mid-program. Although some multi-year withdrawals can work, we find this withdrawal’s proposed structure unsuited to an upfront payment. For a framework whose central premise is that selection should follow published data, requesting the full multi-year envelope before that data exists inverts the order of evidence and capital.  
* **Governance Structure Not Yet in Place:** The two advisory councils, totaling up to 14 seats, are central to every funding decision in WP2 through WP5, yet at the time of voting, no members are named and no letters of intent are provided. The formation of these bodies and the dOSPO legal entity is treated as a milestone to be completed after funds are released rather than as a precondition for them. This sequencing, committing the capital before the accountability structure exists, mirrors the concerns we have raised on other proposals.  
* **Budget Reconciliation and Calculation Gaps:** Our reviewers identified internal inconsistencies between the work-package tables, the operations breakdown, and the stated cohort and stipend figures in WP3. For a 12,000,000 ada request that positions itself as a model of transparent stewardship, the budget tables should reconcile precisely on submission.  
* **Overlap with Existing Ecosystem Programmes:** The proposal's scope materially overlaps with Intersect's Paid Open Source Model retainers and bounty work, including a separate POSM continuation request submitted via the Intersect Budget Process 2026\. We see no formal coordination mechanism, memorandum of understanding, or transition agreement that would prevent double funding or institutional fragmentation.  
* **Administrative Load Relative to Program Output:** Roughly 2,000,000 ada is allocated to operations and governance infrastructure over the term. We find the scope of this expense too broad and it warrants either tighter scoping or, as suggested in advisory feedback, a cap that limits upside exposure of the treasury over a multi-year horizon.
