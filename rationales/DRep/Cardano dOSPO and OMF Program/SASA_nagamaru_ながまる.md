I strongly agree with the core concern this proposal raises regarding OSS maintainer sustainability. The fact that many critical Cardano libraries, SDKs, tooling systems, and indexing infrastructure depend on a very small number of maintainers represents a legitimate long-term ecosystem risk. I also believe that using dependency centrality and bus factor analysis as a data-driven funding methodology is a more rational approach than relationship-based support structures.

At the same time, I believe Cardano governance as a whole is still in a relatively early stage of maturity, with ongoing challenges around proposal review capacity, budget prioritization, long-term operational execution, and accountability. In that context, this proposal appears to go beyond OSS funding and instead attempts to establish an entirely new operational and governance layer, which makes the overall scope feel extremely broad.

In particular, I believe there are serious questions around whether the current governance environment is mature enough to sustainably manage a structure involving:

two governance councils,
a 36-month operational timeline,
ecosystem-wide coordination,
mentorship pipelines,
bounty governance,
activation programs,
and the formation of a new legal entity,

all simultaneously.

I am also concerned about the risk that the operational and coordination layer itself could gradually become larger and more complex than the maintainer support mission it is intended to serve. While I understand the vision behind the proposal, the institutional structure currently feels oversized relative to the ecosystem’s present governance maturity.

Personally, I believe a smaller pilot program focused exclusively on Tier-1 critical infrastructure maintenance would have been a more realistic starting point. After demonstrating operational effectiveness, transparency, and measurable ecosystem value within a limited scope, the framework could then expand incrementally over time.

For these reasons, I vote against this proposal.
