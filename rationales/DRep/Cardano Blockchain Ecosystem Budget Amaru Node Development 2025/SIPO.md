As the DRep representing SIPO, SIPO vote YES on the “Amaru Node Development 2025” proposal.

SIPO strongly support this initiative as a foundational investment in the long-term decentralization, resilience, and public infrastructure of the Cardano blockchain.

One of Cardano’s strengths—its robust Haskell-based node—also presents a critical centralization risk: dependency on a single codebase and implementation. Amaru directly addresses this concern by delivering a high-assurance, Rust-based, modular, and lightweight alternative node. The project also aims to be interoperable with emerging platforms such as WebAssembly and RISC-V, further increasing accessibility and diversity in node deployment.

SIPO emphasize the following reasons for our support:

1. Decentralization: This is the first major initiative to reduce single-client dependency within Cardano, providing infrastructure redundancy and removing central points of failure at the node level. A second, independently developed node aligns with the core principle of Byzantine-resilient decentralization.

2. Public Good & Open Source: Amaru is maintained by PRAGMA, a non-profit open-source association comprised of reputable Cardano ecosystem contributors including Blink Labs, dcSpark, Cardano Foundation, TxPipe, and Sundae Labs. The project is fully transparent, publicly documented, and built for the entire community.

3. Technical Innovation: The implementation in Rust ensures modern tooling, increased contributor accessibility, and long-term maintainability. The WebAssembly compatibility paves the way for broader deployment environments and novel use cases.

4. Fiscal Responsibility: The proposal includes a clear and itemized budget, defined scopes per module owner, 25% contingency allocation, and an on-chain smart contract to control fund disbursement. Scope owners can return unused contingency funds to the Treasury. The reporting cadence (quarterly) and regular public demos show operational maturity and transparency.

5. Constitutional Compliance: The proposal is aligned with the Cardano Constitution and falls well within the active Net Change Limit (NCL). The maintainers have thoughtfully included a constitutionality checklist to affirm legal and governance compliance.

From a governance perspective, this proposal is exemplary in its clarity, structure, execution plan, and systemic benefit to the ecosystem.

SIPO consider Amaru not just another software project but an essential national infrastructure for a resilient, multi-client Cardano. It sets a precedent for sustainable public goods development in a decentralized governance system.

For these reasons, we enthusiastically vote YES.
