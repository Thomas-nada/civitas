This is once again a massively excessive NCL. I was right about the last NCL being way too high and causing public caution against buying the Ada token. The NCL is a public declaration of how much Ada can and most likely will be sold over the time period. It is not just an "upper limit" to spending as some suggest, it is effectively a spending target. So long as there is money on the table, people will propose ways to spend it, and as we witnessed last year that Ada will get spent. With a higher ceiling, every proposal is bound to be inflated to take advantage of how much Ada is available. We have seen nearly no returns on our last year's excessive spending beyond core development, and nothing suggests that there will be any change to that in the next year. We must tighten our belt and be much more demanding in our expectations of treasury-funded ventures. We can not spend millions of Ada fronting the bill for conference events and parties that do not pay back the treasury after profiting on ticket prices, all while the Ada token sinks in value. We can not be funding un-proven development teams to build products with no demand. It all starts with the NCL. And beyond the size of the NCL being excessive, the time period is also just absurd. We need to stick to calendar-year timeframes. Throwing in strange timeframes such as 16-month terms appears as an attempt to obfuscate and complicate the tracking of spending data over time. It makes us look both dishonest and amateurish.
