Vote: YES

This Update Committee governance action (729daaf2f9f89f842a61f6e3ebf7e57d16d6fa4116e29c13114780cb39090850) seats the four members elected in Cardano's 2026 Constitutional Committee election — Philip DiSarro, Leandros BSP, Marek Mahut (deliberative.cc), and Cardano Curia — replacing the four of seven seats whose terms expire at epoch 653. The new terms run to epoch 799, the 146-epoch maximum the protocol permits. It was submitted on-chain July 31, 2026 following an off-chain election conducted on the Ekklesia platform that closed July 13 and was verified by an independent third-party auditor. No ADA is requested and no treasury funds are involved.

Dracula DAO votes YES. This is the routine, scheduled renewal of a core constitutional body, and it arrives through the process the ecosystem built for exactly this purpose: an open candidacy period, a stake-weighted DRep election, an independent audit of the raw voting data, and on-chain ratification by DReps and SPOs. Dracula DAO voted YES on July 1, 2026 to reduce committeeMinSize from 7 to 5, and stated in that rationale that we would continue to weight the full and prompt seating of the Constitutional Committee as a governance priority. This action is the direct discharge of that commitment. The consequence of failure is concrete rather than theoretical: with four seats vacated and no replacements ratified, three members remain — below the minimum of five — and every CC-dependent action, including treasury withdrawals and protocol-parameter changes, stalls regardless of how DReps vote on them. Cardano has already lived through one such shutdown following the Cardano Atlantic Council's retirement in November 2025. Repeating it by inaction would be a self-inflicted failure.

The honest weakness here is the thinness of the electoral mandate. Only 38 DReps participated in the election, representing 2.332 billion ada of delegated voting power, and the fourth-placed candidate was seated on 55.26% of participating power — a majority of a small turnout, not a broad expression of ecosystem will. The on-chain tallies show the same pattern: as of August 17, DRep support stood at 32.46% against a 67% threshold and SPO support at 1.95% against 51%. Dracula DAO reads these numbers as apathy rather than opposition, since no substantive case against any of the four candidates has been advanced. That is not reassuring. A constitutional body seated by default, on turnout this low, carries less legitimate authority than the role demands, and the ecosystem should treat the 2026 turnout figures as a problem to fix before the next cycle rather than a quirk to note in passing.

Dracula DAO's ultra long-term view of Cardano's institutions resolves this cleanly. A thin mandate is a weakness to be corrected through better participation in future elections; an inquorate Constitutional Committee is a governance failure with immediate effect on every other action before the chain. Withholding support to protest turnout would punish the ecosystem for a defect the candidates did not cause and cannot remedy. The election was properly run, independently audited, and produced a result. Dracula DAO ratifies it.

Dracula DAO notes that this action requires SPO ratification at a 51% threshold, and that SPO participation as of mid-August was 1.95% — the more urgent of the two shortfalls. The VAMP stake pool, operated by Vampyre Fund, has voted YES in the SPO track. We urge all SPOs who have not yet voted to do so before epoch 653. Abstention here is not neutrality; on an action with a hard expiry and a quorum cliff behind it, a vote not cast is a vote to freeze Cardano's governance.
