I am voting in favor of the Cardano x Draper Dragon: Orion Fund because in my view it represents a necessary shift in how our Treasury manages capital. As the ecosystem scales, we need to move beyond one-time grants toward investment-based models that have the potential to return value to the Treasury. While I would normally be very much against VC involvement in our ecosystem, this proposal is structured in a way that is interestingly different than anything I have seen in other ecosystems. It offers a framework for professional management while maintaining the community oversight that defines Cardano. While I remain cautiously optimistic about the outcomes, the model itself is more sustainable for the long-term health of the ADA holder base. It creates a mechanism where the growth of Cardano projects can directly replenish the funds used in their growth. 
My support is based largely on the structure of the proposal rather than personal familiarity with the team. The document appears to be well-constructed, with clear legal definitions and no obvious loopholes that I can see that would allow for the misuse of Treasury funds. Most importantly, the fund is released in tranches. This means the team must return to the community for future approvals to continue operations. If the fund fails to meet performance expectations or diverges from our values, the community retains the power to vote "No" on subsequent funding. This built-in accountability mitigates the risks typically associated with external venture capital. 
I also strongly believe that at this point we need the operational efficiency. We cannot expect DReps to vote on every individual project or micro-allocation of funds; doing so is too slow and risks a governance quagmire that could leave Cardano behind. By approving this fund, we are delegating the specialized task of due diligence and deal-sourcing to professional managers. This type of broader proposal is actually a great example of what I think Dreps should be voting for. This allows focus on high-level strategic decisions rather than getting bogged down in hundreds of smaller votes that a direct democracy (even a liquid one) is ill-equipped to handle efficiently.
It is noteworthy that a venture firm is even willing to operate under these specific decentralized constraints. Relying on a recurring public vote for future tranches introduces a level of uncertainty that many traditional funds would find unacceptable. Their willingness to work within this paradigm suggests a functional respect for our governance model. I believe this is a pragmatic opportunity to gain professional management and networking reach on terms that still prioritize community sovereignty. I also don’t believe this is the type of opportunity that is likely to present itself again anytime soon. For these reason I am voting “Yes” on this proposal.
