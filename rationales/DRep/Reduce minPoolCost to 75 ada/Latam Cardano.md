Lowering `minPoolCost` to 75 ADA without implementing holistic adjustments to Cardano's pool reward scheme (such as introducing a minimum variable fee(minPoolMargin) floor parameters) introduces substantial adverse selection.

In a market driven heavily by delegation inertia, delegators rarely switch pools based solely on a minor reduction in fixed fees. Instead, lowering the parameter exposes small and independent SPOs to a "race to the bottom." Large operators and institutional exchanges with deep capital reserves can set both fixed fees (75 ADA) and variable margins (0%) to ultra-low levels, effectively pricing out mission-driven single pools. The likely outcome is a net reduction in SPO revenue without achieving meaningful stake redistribution across the network. Governance integrity requires evaluating economic parameter changes holistically rather than in isolation; thus, voting **No** preserves operator sustainability until a comprehensive staking framework redesign is presented.

**Impact on the Latin American Ecosystem:**
For the Latin American Cardano ecosystem, where many independent SPOs rely on pool fixed fees to cover cloud server costs, bare-metal hardware, and community outreach initiatives, this isolated parameter change presents a direct economic risk. Without an accompanying margin floor to protect revenue, regional SPOs face reduced operating income without a corresponding increase in delegation, hindering local infrastructure sustainability and weakening LatAm's representation in global network validation.
