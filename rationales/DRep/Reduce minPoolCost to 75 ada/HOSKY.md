## Vote: No

I do not support reducing minPoolCost from 170 ADA to 75 ADA through this standalone change. The fixed-fee burden on delegators to small pools is real. My concern is whether this adjustment produces durable improvements in independent operator viability, and how that outcome will be measured.

Under the HOSKY Cardano First framework, I need a clearer case for the proposed transition across Decentralization and Economic Sustainability.

## Pillar analysis

### Decentralization (lead pillar)

Lowering the floor gives small pools more flexibility to offer competitive returns. It does not force any operator to lower its fee, and I recognize that the proposal's account of the 2023 reduction does not support claiming an inevitable market-wide race to the bottom.

The intended outcome, however, is more viable independent pools. Improving the return advertised to delegators is one part of that outcome. The proposal expects small pools to respond to competitive pressure by lowering their fees, but whether the resulting delegation gains compensate for lower fixed-fee income depends on actual behavior. I am not satisfied that this tradeoff has been resolved well enough for another standalone reduction.

I would want the evaluation to track independent operators retaining or gaining viable delegation, operator income and concentration together. A fee reduction alone is not a sufficient measure of success.

### Economic Sustainability

This action changes reward distribution; it does not create additional rewards or reduce the cost of running infrastructure. Pools that choose a lower fee accept less fixed-fee income for a given reward pot in exchange for the possibility of attracting more delegation. That can be a sensible choice, but its effect on long-term operator viability remains the decision I need to assess.

The proposal itself describes 75 ADA as an interim step toward more structural reform, including a proportional minimum margin. I prefer a clearer transition plan that evaluates those changes together and explains how independent operators remain viable as reserve-funded rewards decline. That preference carries a real cost: structural reform takes longer than a parameter adjustment available today.

### Governance Transparency

The requested value is explicit, the rationale is public, and the action changes only minPoolCost. The proposal identifies adverse effects that could justify reconsideration, but does not set a review schedule or quantitative decision thresholds for judging whether the change has helped operator viability.

I would want those measures and the responsibility for publishing the evaluation specified in advance. They would give delegators and operators a common basis for assessing the outcome and any subsequent adjustment.

## Risks I'm accepting with this No

### 1. Keeping the current disadvantage for small pools

The existing floor can consume a large share of rewards in pools producing few blocks. A No leaves that pressure in place and may prevent some independent operators from attracting delegation. This is the strongest argument against my position.

### 2. Delaying an available improvement

The proposed reduction could provide useful relief while broader reform is developed. Waiting for a clearer transition may cost time that small operators do not have. I accept that risk, while recognizing that the unchanged setting also needs scrutiny.

## Decision

The problem deserves action, but I am not persuaded that this standalone reduction has a sufficiently defined path to sustainable independent operation and measurable follow-up. **No.**
