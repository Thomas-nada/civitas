Block rewards have dropped a long way since Shelley, so the fixed pool fee now eats a large share of rewards for small pools. That pushes delegators toward bigger pools and works against decentralisation.

The 2023 cut from 340 to 170 ada didn't cause the race to the bottom some people feared. Most pools kept their fees where they were, and smaller pools used the room to compete. IO Research has also revised its view and now sees a high floor as helping large operators more than it protects the network.

This proposal went through the proper process: public discussion as PCP-006, TSC endorsement and the required notice period. Its effect on the treasury is small.

I note that a later reversal could not force existing pools back up to a higher fee, so this change is harder to undo than it looks. I'm comfortable with that given the evidence. I see this as an interim step, and I'd like to see work continue on a proportional fee (CIP-23) as the longer-term fix.
