This standalone submission resolves the previous bundling objection, but it does not establish that reducing minPoolCost from 170 ADA to 75 ADA will improve the sustainable operation of independent small pools. It permits operators to surrender fixed-fee income in pursuit of additional delegation without demonstrating that those inflows will offset the loss or address operating costs, reward uncertainty, and concentration. Delegation also carries weight in SPO governance decisions, so financial competition for delegation must be assessed for its effects on governance independence as well as delegator returns. In my view, lasting reform requires transparent identity, ownership and sponsorship relationships, reputation, and historical accountability, together with a tested incentive design addressing operator viability and pool splitting. This action supplies neither that framework nor the proposed proportional-margin counterbalance, and its reversion would not automatically restore previously registered lower fees. I therefore vote No.
