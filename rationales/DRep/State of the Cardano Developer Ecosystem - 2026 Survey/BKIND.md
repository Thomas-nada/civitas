What I support. A yearly survey of Cardano developers is useful, and the code of the survey and of its report is open source. My No is not to asking developers. It is to the way the answers are collected and presented.

A key per browser, not a person. Keyholder is the CIP-179 role for "control of a payment/spending credential". Such a credential costs nothing, needs no ada and no registration. The survey's documentation states: "The browser makes a key, and the response's credential is that key." The sponsor "pays at most 0.5 ada per response, once per respondent key". Another browser makes another key, and with it another response the sponsor pays for. The chain cannot tell these responses apart from responses by different people.

The proposer pays for and admits answers. Respondents "don't need a wallet: a sponsor, run on Vercel, pays their transactions." That sponsor is run by the proposer, and through its bot check it decides which responses it pays for and submits. A respondent without a wallet can only answer through it. The answers are on chain, but for those respondents, whether an answer gets there is decided off chain by the party that asks the questions.

On chain is not the same as verifiable. The survey's documentation is open about how responses are made. The action, read by every DRep, stake pool operator and Constitutional Committee member, states instead: "Anyone can count the answers and check the results for themselves." Anyone can count the records. Nobody can check from the chain how many people they come from. Recording them on chain gives the results the look of a verifiable count that the method does not support.
