# DraculaDAO

**Proposal:** AURORA Open infrastructure for bringing real credit markets to Cardano
**Vote:** No
**Voter ID:** `drep1ygs99wsy49h9vydggncfeck2xwmngyeltp66pvgzl6jz63s6qmu3p`

---

Vote: NO

This is a Treasury Withdrawal, "AURORA: Open infrastructure for bringing real credit markets to Cardano" (gov_action1znt2fr0chyye50nj0cdld2dvdjg3xsdfvq0fr37n43uehlvfkqdsqjnuq3n), submitted October 18, 2026 by Fairway with technical collaborator Sundial and technical advisor Fallen Icarus, with voting closing November 20, 2026. Fairway requests 940,000 ADA — approximately $188,000 at the proposal's $0.20 per ADA reference, nearer $141,000 at the prevailing price — over roughly five months to build a shared market layer above compatible Cardano lending protocols: metadata and verification standards, the AURORA Discovery Engine, open query and discovery APIs, a Capital Provider Profile Standard, developer tooling and a reference implementation, released under Apache License 2.0 and culminating in a testnet demonstration. The allocation is 800,000 ADA as a fixed implementation fee to Fairway and 140,000 ADA for independent security and legal review (~60,000), independent treasury-use audit (~20,000), hosting (~30,000) and technical contingency (~30,000). No ADA burning is involved.

The custody and oversight design is genuinely good and Dracula DAO credits it. All 940,000 ADA sits in a dedicated 3-of-5 multisignature held by five independent signers — James Meidinger, Christian Taylor, Elder Millennial, Wilco and Eddie D — and no member of the implementation consortium holds a key, so the recipient of the money cannot release it to itself. Acceptance is evidence-based against published criteria, conflicted administrators must disclose and abstain, disbursement can be suspended on material incompleteness, and on termination all ADA remaining in administrator custody returns to the treasury. Sixty thousand ADA is set aside to buy independent security and legal review rather than to assert it, and twenty thousand buys an independent audit of how the treasury's own money was spent. The ask is also modest by the standards of this treasury cycle, the output is permissively licensed public infrastructure rather than a proprietary platform, and standardizing how credit opportunities and verification references are described is the kind of boring, foundational, interoperability work this DREP prefers over promotion.

The problem is not the structure. It is that the treasury is being asked to fund the index before the market exists. AURORA explicitly "does not replace lending protocols, originate loans, custody lending capital, or decide which opportunities receive funding" — it describes, indexes and exposes the credit opportunities that compatible lending implementations create. Those implementations are not yet producing meaningful volume on Cardano mainnet, and the proposal names no lender, originator or capital provider that has committed to adopting the standard it would define. A discovery and filtering layer is worth funding when discovery is the bottleneck; here the bottleneck is supply and demand for on-chain credit itself. Five months of funded work ends at a testnet demonstration and a reference implementation, with no mainnet deployment and no counterparty obligated to use any of it, which means the treasury's realistic downside is a well-audited, well-documented specification that nothing indexes. Fairway's own prior treasury-funded work came through Catalyst Fund 8 and Fund 12 on identity and credential systems, and Dracula DAO can find no record of either converting into sustained mainnet usage; a request roughly an order of magnitude larger than those grants should rest on a demonstrated path from deliverable to usage, and this one does not. The target is also "institutional credit markets" while the proposal concedes that legal and regulatory questions remain open — the exact questions that decide whether regulated lenders can use this at all. There is no revenue or repayment term of any kind.

Dracula DAO votes NO. Treasury funds should be grants paid on completion of well-defined features, with undelivered money returning to the treasury. This proposal is close to that shape in custody and sign-off but inverts it in payment: 140,000 ADA — 14.9% of the total — is available at commencement before any deliverable exists, and the proposal states plainly that funds are "released in advance for each implementation stage" rather than on acceptance of completed work, with each cumulative ceiling raised after the prior milestone is approved. That makes the ceilings a cap on the treasury's exposure rather than a price paid for delivered work, and it leaves a single pre-selected team on a fixed implementation fee with no competitive bidding on any of the work inside it. Independent sign-off plus a treasury refund is a partial approximation of the model Dracula DAO argues for, not the model itself, and Dracula DAO names that gap here as it has elsewhere. On its own that gap would not decide the vote at this size. Combined with an unproven demand side, no committed adopter, no mainnet milestone and no prior record of mainnet traction from this team, it does.

Dracula DAO would reconsider a narrower resubmission. Fund the specification and the metadata and verification standards — the first milestone's worth of work, paid on publication — and come back for the Discovery Engine and APIs once at least one lending protocol has committed to implementing the standard and there is live mainnet credit activity to index. Infrastructure that something is already waiting to use is a far easier YES than infrastructure built in the hope that something will.
