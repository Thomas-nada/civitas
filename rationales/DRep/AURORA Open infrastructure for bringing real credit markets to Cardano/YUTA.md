I vote NO on “AURORA”.

1.There is no track record, evidence or target for demand. Track record: cardano-loans, which this proposal builds on, has no recorded use on mainnet (checked with DBSync: zero beacons for loan requests, loan offers or active loans). On testnet there are only a few, created as examples for its guide. Evidence: no named lenders or borrowers who have committed to use it, and no letters of intent, are given. Target: adoption is not part of how delivery is measured, and there is no target for mainnet operation or usage.

2.The technical part can be built small. Using AI-assisted coding, I implemented this proposal’s requirements, except the third-party reviews and including a testnet demonstration, in about 25 minutes and about 1,300 lines of code (about 2,100 lines including specifications and documentation). It is first-version quality and not ready to use as is. What I want to point out is that the biggest hurdle for this proposal is not technical implementation but adoption: getting people to use it. Having no track record, evidence or target for that is, I believe, the biggest risk. The output, including the testnet transactions, can be checked here: https://adatool.net/open-credit-discovery

3.The track record does not match the size. Fairway has received Catalyst funding three times, but I could not find a track record in lending or market infrastructure. I also could not find any sign that their hiring and identity projects (two completed, and F12 with its final milestone under review) used Cardano mainnet. For F12 (Ethiopia national ID), the team’s own close-out report says Cardano was not used in the pilot. This request is about ten times their largest grant.
