I am voting No on AURORA in its current form.

The proposal has a credible public-infrastructure objective. Shared metadata, discovery, verification interfaces, and developer tooling could reduce duplicated work across Cardano credit applications. Its Apache 2.0 licensing, independently deployable design, and separation from lending capital, custody, and underwriting are positive features.

However, the evidence available for this review does not sufficiently demonstrate adoption by independent implementations, the practical requirements for cross-protocol compatibility, or the proportionality of the requested 940,000 ADA. The proposed outcome is a development-stage infrastructure release and testnet demonstration; wider market benefits remain dependent on subsequent integration and use.

The proposal also incorporates supporting documents covering material funding terms, milestones, administration, audits, and refund conditions. Those documents could not all be retrieved and independently assessed during this review. This does not establish that the provisions are absent, but references to supporting documents and assurances of oversight are not substitutes for verifiable evidence. I am not prepared to authorize treasury expenditure on the assumption that unexamined provisions adequately protect the community.

Independent treasury custody and planned reviews are welcome, but they do not replace precise acceptance criteria, independently assessable pricing, and clear accountability for advance implementation payments. Before supporting the complete programme, I would need concrete integration commitments, testable interoperability requirements, and accessible, fully inspectable delivery and funding terms.

My objection is to the funding readiness demonstrated by this request, not to the potential value of open credit-market infrastructure.
