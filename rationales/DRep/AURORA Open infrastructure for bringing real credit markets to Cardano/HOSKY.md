## Vote: No

I am voting No on the 940,000 ADA AURORA withdrawal. Shared, open infrastructure for Cardano credit markets is useful work. I cannot support this funding arrangement while the entire allocation can leave independent custody before final review and acceptance.

I apply the Cardano First framework here through Governance Transparency, Economic Sustainability and Adoption.

## Pillar analysis

### Governance Transparency (lead pillar)

AURORA has real safeguards: independent 3-of-5 custody, published milestone criteria, progress reports and dedicated audit funding. Fairway and the other implementation contributors hold no keys to the administrator multisignature. Those are useful protections.

The payment sequence weakens them at the point they matter most. The [full proposal](https://github.com/fairway-global/aurora-proposal/blob/main/proposal.md) permits cumulative advances of 140,000 ADA at commencement, 460,000 ADA after M1, 750,000 ADA after M2 and the full 940,000 ADA after M3. Independent review, the final Treasury-use audit and project closure are M4 work. Administrators can therefore authorize the whole allocation before those final checks are complete.

I would keep a meaningful final payment under independent control until the review findings and final deliverables have been accepted. I would also want each named administrator's acceptance and responsibilities publicly confirmed before approving the withdrawal. Naming an oversight panel should come with evidence that its members have taken the job.

Voters also need a fixed record of the terms they approved. The submitted [metadata URL](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fairway-global/aurora-proposal/refs/heads/main/data.jsonld) follows the current main branch. Its bytes match the recorded hash today, so I am not alleging that the proposal has changed. I want the submitted reference to identify a fixed version directly, so future readers can retrieve the agreed terms without depending on the publisher's current branch.

### Economic Sustainability

The 800,000 ADA core-development allocation is a disclosed fixed implementation fee. Fixed fees can be sensible when the outputs are clear. The remaining 140,000 ADA funds review, hosting, Treasury-use oversight and contingency.

My concern is recovery after an advance. On termination, the express refund promise covers ADA still under administrator custody. Fairway remains responsible for unfinished work, but the proposal does not give the same clear recovery protection for every advance already transferred to its implementation wallet. With the full allocation potentially disbursed before final review, that distinction matters.

I would fund this as a public infrastructure grant on the strength of accepted outputs. I do not require lending profits or loan repayments from a project whose funded scope is software and standards. I do require the treasury to retain enough control to enforce final delivery.

### Adoption

The proposed metadata standard, discovery engine and open APIs could reduce repeated integration work. Apache 2.0 licensing and independent deployment make the outputs useful beyond one commercial application.

The funded completion test is an end-to-end testnet demonstration and public release. It does not commit institutional lending capital or establish adoption by borrowers and lenders. I would assess it as an infrastructure build, then ask for evidence that independent applications can use it. That potential is a reason to improve and resubmit the proposal, rather than accept avoidable weaknesses in its funding terms.

## Risks I'm accepting with this No

### Delayed shared infrastructure

A No may delay standards and tooling that Cardano credit-market builders could use. I accept that delay to obtain stronger final-delivery protection. The useful technical scope can be preserved in a revised submission.

### More work for an independently administered grant

Confirming administrator commitments and retaining a final payment adds coordination. Some implementation costs must be paid before delivery. A revised schedule should fund that work while keeping a meaningful amount available until final acceptance.

## Decision

Keep the open infrastructure scope, confirm who accepts responsibility for oversight, preserve the agreed proposal version and retain a final payment through independent review. I would reassess a submission with those protections. **No.**
