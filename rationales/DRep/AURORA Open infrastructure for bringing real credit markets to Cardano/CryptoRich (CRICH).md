I am abstaining on AURORA.

AURORA asks 940,000 ADA for open standards, a verification framework and a discovery engine that would let institutions find and screen loan requests on Cardano. It does not lend money itself, and everything it builds is released under Apache 2.0. So I do not judge it by revenue. In the CEBP discussion I wrote that public goods need a different yardstick from proposals that promise to return revenue to the treasury. For a public good, that yardstick is who will use it and who will keep it running.

On both, the proposal does not yet give an answer. No lending protocol or capital provider is named as committing to use the standard, and the milestones measure documents, a testnet demo and reproducibility, so every milestone can be met even if nobody adopts it. Hosting is budgeted for five months; after that, the proposal only says third parties could run the engine, without naming one.

The scope is well built, the amount is small, and the 3-of-5 independent multisig is a sound safeguard, so I am not voting No. I would vote Yes on a resubmission that names at least one lending protocol or capital provider committed to use it, or an operator and a period for running it after delivery.
