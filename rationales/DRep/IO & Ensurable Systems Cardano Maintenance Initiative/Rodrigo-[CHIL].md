Vote: Yes
Core node maintenance is non-negotiable infrastructure. The nine functional areas covered — bugfixing, DevOps, monitoring, documentation, open source support, performance, QA, release management, and component maintenance — represent the full support envelope without which no other ecosystem initiative can safely deliver. The Cardano Blueprint deliverable adds strategic long-term value by enabling alternative node implementations.
The treasury governance structure is well-designed: milestone-based smart contract disbursement through Intersect, multi-sig oversight with five independent entities, and unspent funds returning to treasury. The collaboration with Ensurable Systems to distribute infrastructure stewardship is a welcome step toward reducing single-entity dependency.
However, this approval comes with noted concerns:
The KPI framework is insufficiently concrete. "Monthly Uptime" is listed as the primary KPI but no target threshold is defined. Most Cardano 2030 KPI alignments are marked "Yes: Partially" without specifying what partial means in measurable terms. For a ₳62M ask, the community deserves quantifiable success criteria.
The budget breakdown lacks granularity. 74% allocated to "Development" covers the entire engineering team across all nine areas, but no FTE counts, team size estimates, or per-area cost breakdowns are provided. This makes it difficult to assess whether the pricing is proportionate.
The prior execution record warrants attention. The IOE workstream from the current cycle shows ₳47.1M received against its allocation, described as 49% disbursed. The proposal does not explain whether this reflects pacing (on track with remaining milestones) or underdelivery. This context matters when evaluating a new ask of similar magnitude.
