I understand the intention behind this proposal to improve operational resilience by reducing the minimum size of the Constitutional Committee. Mitigating the risk of governance inoperability due to temporary unavailability or resignation of members is a reasonable objective, and I acknowledge that the proposal complies with the relevant guardrails from a technical and formal perspective.

However, I do not view this as merely a numerical adjustment. In my view, it represents a meaningful shift in governance design.

I do not currently perceive the Constitutional Committee to be in a state of critical dysfunction. At the same time, I believe the overall governance structure is still evolving. In particular, I observe concentration dynamics within DRep voting power. In such an environment, lowering the minimum size from seven to five may increase the relative influence of individual members and, over time, heighten the risk of centralization. This is a concern for me from a structural governance perspective.

While operational continuity is important, I believe it should not be achieved by reducing decentralization safeguards. If strengthening functionality is necessary, I would prefer exploring options that expand committee capacity while maintaining a strong minimum threshold, rather than lowering the minimum requirement.

For these reasons, I cannot support this proposal.
