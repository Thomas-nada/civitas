This treasury withdrawal gives effect to the already-defined Cardano Critical Integrations Budget and, in my view, now includes enough governance safeguards and reporting commitments to justify moving ahead.

The strategic case remains strong: Cardano is missing core infrastructure that every serious DeFi and RWA ecosystem treats as basic utilities – tier-one stablecoin rails, institutional custody and wallets, analytics, bridges and a globally recognised oracle. Without these, TVL, liquidity depth and institutional participation will remain capped regardless of how good the base layer is. This withdrawal aligns funding directly with those gaps, and keeps scope tightly focused on integrations rather than vague ecosystem spend.

What has tipped the balance for me are the controls and guardrails around how the ₳70m can be used:

Constitutional and NCL alignment – The withdrawal is explicitly chained to the Critical Integrations Budget Info Action and the 2025 Net Change Limit extension. It can only be enacted if the budget info action passes, the extended NCL is in force and the Constitutional Committee is in confidence and at minimum size. That prevents funds moving without the broader governance picture in place.

Clear administrative accountability – Intersect is named as Administrator under Article IV, with Cardano Development Holdings holding the funds. There is a single, publicly viewable address delegated to Auto-Abstain, and no delegation to any SPO, which avoids governance distortion and staking yield on treasury funds.

Milestone-based disbursement – Funds do not simply move on approval. Drawdowns require signed statements of work, with milestone-based verification and an independent Oversight Committee checking that amounts, scope and recipients match the contracts. Upfront “milestone zero” payments are explicitly constrained to setup items like conversion fees, custody and legal onboarding.

Defined Steering Committee and conflict handling – A five-member Steering Committee (IOG, CF, EMURGO, Midnight Foundation, Intersect non-voting) holds executive decision power over allocations, with a 3/4 quorum for spend decisions and conflict of interest policies for both the Steering Committee and Oversight Committee.

High-level budget breakdown and capped admin – The withdrawal adds a concrete split (₳65m integrations, ₳3.5m legal/audit/compliance, ₳1.5m contingency) and caps administrative/legal/compliance overhead at a small share of the total, keeping most of the value directed at actual integrations.

Reporting and audits – Intersect commits to bi-annual public reports covering spend, remaining funds, milestone status, impact assessment and independent audit findings, as well as a final reconciliation and return of unused funds after 24 months.

While commercial confidentiality still limits vendor-level line items, the combination of constitutional pre-conditions, public custody, strong administrator accountability, milestone-gated disbursements and structured reporting provides a reasonable balance between practical negotiation constraints and the community’s need for oversight.
