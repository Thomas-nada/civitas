## Rationale

**Vote: No.** PRIME addresses a real weakness in Cardano DeFi, and AlphaGrowth has credible experience. Compound approved its 2024 growth mandate through [Compound Governance Proposal 251](https://compound.finance/governance/proposals/251?target_network=mainnet), and independent Arbitrum research later found that Compound’s incentive campaign produced clear product-led results. However, a later full-year renewal vote failed, so the public record is positive but mixed. This proposal still asks Cardano to take too much risk. It requests ₳120 million. Up to ₳40 million goes to AlphaGrowth, including the performance reserve, and ₳15 million goes to marketing. The Month 4 gate and Intersect custody are useful controls, but key TVL attribution rules and legal contracts will be completed after approval. The proposal does not give a final governing law, a clear dispute forum, strong clawbacks for money already paid, or firm public licence and data rights. Most liquidity spending is also not required to remain Treasury-owned. The performance fee can use TVL that has remained for only 30 days, while longer retention is mainly a reporting measure.  The request equals 24% of the proposed ₳500 million Net Change Limit and about 34% of the existing ₳350 million limit; the new limit itself remains under vote, as shown in the [CardanoCube governance record](https://www.cardanocube.com/governance/gov_actions/gov_action15atytcy8ru7mkcs8m7r8mx7k5x36t0h6grtgmak6v5wmf4nq07lsqhakceq). I score PRIME **66/100**. It falls well below the **90-point requirement for a very large proposal** and fails hard minimums for public assets, Treasury upside, enforceability, sustainability, and portfolio fit.  A smaller pilot with completed contracts, fixed KPI rules, Treasury-owned liquidity, lower compensation, vendor price evidence, and payment after durable results would deserve a new vote.
