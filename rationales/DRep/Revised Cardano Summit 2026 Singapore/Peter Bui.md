I am voting YES on the Cardano Summit proposal.

This is not a small amount of ADA, and I understand why many people are cautious about funding major events during poor market conditions. Treasury spending needs to be scrutinised carefully, especially when the ADA price is low, and every proposal is competing for a limited budget.

For me, this proposal is worth supporting because the Cardano Summit is not just a community meetup or a branding exercise. It is one of the few ecosystem-wide events that brings together builders, institutions, regulators, enterprises, governance participants, developers, DReps and community members in one place.

It matters because Cardano is now entering a different phase.

The ecosystem is no longer only trying to attract retail users, NFT traders or short-term speculation. A lot of the current opportunity centres on stablecoins, real-world assets, institutional adoption, enterprise integrations, regulatory conversations, developer onboarding, and long-term partnerships.

Those outcomes do not usually appear overnight. They often start with conversations, follow-up meetings, technical demos, workshops, hackathons and repeated exposure to the right people.

The interview with Laura Mattiucci from the Cardano Foundation and Nathaniel Acton from EMURGO helped clarify several important points for me. The Summit is being planned around ecosystem coordination, governance activities, Battle of the Builders, hackathons, workshops, enterprise and institutional discussions, regulatory panels, builder stages, and an expo area that gives Cardano projects a platform to be seen. They also explained that prior summits have led to ongoing relationships with organisations such as Petrobras, the European Commission, the German parliament, Draper University and other institutional conversations that are still developing.

I also think the timing matters.

Cardano has spent years building core infrastructure and is now seeing momentum across critical integrations, Bitcoin DeFi, stablecoin infrastructure, partner chains, governance tooling and DeFi applications. If we believe those efforts are worth funding, then we also need to support credible ways to put that work in front of people who can use it, integrate it, fund it, regulate it or build on it.

I do not expect the return from a Summit to be measured only in immediate on-chain metrics like TVL or wallets created. Some outcomes should be tracked through clear KPIs such as developer participation, hackathon submissions, project attendance, qualified leads, enterprise and government engagement, media reach, follow-up collaborations and public post-event reporting.

My support is based on the expectation that the Cardano Foundation continues to publish transparent reporting, milestone progress and measurable outcomes after the event.

The proposal is expensive, but I believe the cost is justified if it helps Cardano maintain visibility, coordinate the ecosystem, support builders and create the conditions for serious adoption conversations at a time when the ecosystem needs more than just technical development.

For those reasons, I am voting YES.
