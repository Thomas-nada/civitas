# **1.0 Introduction**

## **1.1 Summary**

We are voting **NO (remediable)** on Cardano Global Events 2027\.

There is a real good inside this proposal, and it should be named first. Conferences are where builders meet the developers, enterprises and regulators they cannot reach from a Discord server, and most Cardano teams cannot afford to stand in those rooms. The proposal's best idea, new for 2027, is to pay for builders to get there: economy travel and accommodation, booked or reimbursed against receipts, never advanced as cash, selected with Intersect's Growth and Marketing Committee against published criteria, with reviewers declaring interests and recipients named in the final report. The Foundation donates its own staff time, takes no profit, publishes audited financials on-chain, and has disclosed in the action itself something its last administered withdrawal did not say plainly: that the receiving credential is single-signature on chain, with multi-signature control applied off chain. The 2025–26 program it renews was delivered across five continents, and on its own reported figures it beat every target it set.

That last clause is where this reading turns. Every piece of evidence that the 2025–26 program worked comes from the party that ran it. The Foundation set the targets, counted the results, reports them on its own dashboard, and in this action proposes to administer the renewal itself, report on it itself, and sit — if the proposed 2027 budget process goes ahead as drafted — as steward of the budget domain that would later set and judge this spending. Every step is defensible, and nothing here suggests the reporting is anything but honest. But the structure gives no point at which an account of the program's effect can enter that does not pass through the program's operator. A program built this way cannot register its own failure, however well it is run. The question this rationale therefore puts first is the counterfactual: what would have happened without this money, and how would anyone outside the Foundation know?

The No rests on three findings, each a property of this action and each repairable.

First, the program's measure of success is written, counted and graded by the program. Its targets are counts of contacts, developers, conversations and sign-ups, calibrated to its own prior counts, verified by no one outside it, and followed by no measure of what happened afterwards. No result is named that would cause the Foundation to drop an event or advise against renewal. That makes the program unfalsifiable, which is a different problem from making it ineffective.

Second, once the money moves, nothing can steer it. The full ₳24,995,775 goes in one transaction to a key held by the Foundation, with no administrator, no assurer, no contract with the Treasury, and no party other than the Foundation able to pause, redirect or recover funds. More than half the event spending falls after the current spending limit expires in early July 2027, so the action draws next year's money from this year's headroom before next year's limit exists.

Third, the action asks to be counted inside a 2027 budget domain that does not yet exist, whose steward the Foundation has offered to be, and says its spending should be deducted from that domain's future cap "even if a different steward is chosen". A spend that writes itself into a structure DReps have not yet approved narrows the decision they will be asked to make about that structure.

Run separately, the external fiduciary instrument this DRep uses reaches No on its own grounds. At the current ada price the request falls in its highest size band, which requires staged exposure, independent verification and separation of duties, and the proposal provides none of the three.

What pulled hardest the other way was the builders. The travel pool is the most additional and most relational thing in this proposal, and a No in December may cost the February events. Section 2.10 is written so that a resubmission can be quick: fund the first half of the calendar now, with the builder pool ring-fenced and the outcome measures fixed before the first booth goes up, and put the second half behind an independent mid-year review.

## **1.2 Description of Governance Action**

This Treasury Withdrawal requests ₳24,995,775 for the Cardano Foundation to run a single Cardano-branded presence at twelve industry conferences between January and December 2027, each with a side event, four of them hackathons, and to fund builder travel to those events. At the proposal's assumed rate of USD 0.20 per ada, the request is US$4,999,155:US$4,528,000 in direct event costs, US$233,100\ in\ fees\ for\ local\ delivery\ partners\ at\ six\ events,andUS$238,055 (five per cent) for audit fees and taxes. The arithmetic is correct throughout; every partner fee is exactly ten per cent of that event's other costs.

The calendar runs from Africa Tech Summit in Nairobi (10–11 February) through TEAMZ Tokyo, Money20/20 Asia, Consensus Miami, TOKEN2049 New York, Signal Week Paris, Rare Evo and Dev Gov Day in San Francisco, TOKEN2049 Singapore, LABITCONF Buenos Aires, the Australian Crypto Convention, Web Summit Lisbon, and India Blockchain Week in December. Sponsorship packages are the largest line at US$1,989,000(TOKEN2049\ NewYork\ alone\ is\ US$490,000), followed by booth build and AV (US$1,060,000),travel\ for\ projects\ and\ staff(US$702,000 for about 210 trips), side events and hackathons (US$695,000)and\ merchandise(US$82,000).

The Foundation self-administers. Funds go to a Foundation credential that the chain confirms is registered, unstaked and delegated to Always Abstain. Reporting is quarterly, with a final report by 31 March 2028 and a live dashboard. Grant Thornton is to audit the withdrawal, subject to final engagement terms. If the ada price falls, later activations will be scaled back; if it rises, the surplus returns. Unspent and unused funds return to the Treasury within six months of the last activation. The Foundation may replace an event that stops offering value, announcing the replacement and its budget on the Cardano Forum before committing funds, and no replacement may raise the total. Against the ₳500,000,000 Net Change Limit for epochs 613 to 713, ₳457,395,629 has been enacted, leaving ₳42,604,371. This request would use 58.7 per cent of that remainder, and five per cent of the whole limit. It fits alongside the one other withdrawal now pending.

# **2.0 Discussion**

## **2.1 Method**

Every governance action gets read; not every action earns the same reading. This DRep runs each one first through an intake that decides how deeply it should be read, before any merits are weighed, then reads the merits, and selects the vote last. The question for the vote is not whether it helps something pass but whether a warrant has been derived to deploy delegated stake against it.

This action earned the deeper reading for three reasons, none of them its size. Spent ada cannot be unspent, and the action as written leaves no one but the recipient able to stop the spending partway. The program's reports are the lens through which the ecosystem will judge whether events spending works at all, and through which the proposed 2027 budget domain would set its cap, and those reports run through the party being funded. And the proposal's value claim rests largely on counts produced and collected by its operator, which is the pattern this framework treats as a request for translation rather than as evidence.

On interest. I hold ada and use the chain, which disqualifies no one. I am not eligible for the travel pool and have no relationship with the Foundation, Rare Network or any of the named events beyond ordinary ecosystem participation. One association should be declared: I previously contracted as a governance consultant with Xerberus, one of the five entities on the oversight committee of the Intersect-administered treasury contracts under which the 2025–26 program was paid. Section 2.6 compares that structure with the one proposed here, and readers should weigh that. I also hold a view of the proposed 2027 budget process that this action places itself inside, and section 2.7 is written to stand on this action's own terms rather than on that view.

## **2.2 What this proposal gets right, stated properly**

The builder travel design is careful. Calls open ahead of each quarter, selection closes ten weeks out so that visas can be secured, eligibility requires a product live on mainnet and willingness to staff the booth and report afterwards, and assessment runs on five published criteria: a working demonstrable product, fit with the event, need, relevance to the host market, and balance across sectors and regions. Selection is shared with an Intersect committee rather than held by the Foundation alone. Reviewers declare interests and step out. Support is economy travel booked directly or reimbursed against receipts. Recipients and amounts are named in the final report, and selection is expressly not an endorsement. That is how a public fund should hand money to private teams, and few proposals in this ecosystem get it this right.

The spending side of the accounting is among the best on chain. The Foundation publishes audited financials through Reeve, its auditor attests them on chain, milestone reports will reference transaction hashes, and the action allocates five per cent for audit and taxes rather than leaving them unpriced. The Foundation contributes staff time it estimates at US\$640,000, about thirteen per cent of the request, at no cost to the Treasury. It takes no profit. Its downside rule on price is sound: if ada falls, the program shrinks; the Treasury is not asked to top it up. The anchor metadata is signed by the Foundation and its hash matches the chain.

The disclosure of custody deserves its own sentence. When this DRep read the PFMS withdrawal in October, the Foundation's receiving credential was described as multi-signature while the chain showed a key credential, and the difference was left for a reader to find. This action states it plainly in the text. That is a correction registered as a correction, and it should be noticed.

And the earlier program did what it set out to do in the terms it set. It ran twenty activities across five continents, gave seventy-nine projects a stage, and reached its reported targets by wide margins. The people who organised booths in Rio, Nairobi, Paris and Hong Kong did real work, and none of what follows is about them.

## **2.3 An account with no outside**

The proposal's case for renewal is that the first program worked. Here is how that conclusion was reached.

The Foundation and its partners defined the problem: Cardano appeared at conferences fragmented, under many brands. They designed the response: one coordinated booth. They set the targets, counted contacts, developers, conversations and sign-ups at their own booths, and reported the results against those targets. Spending is accounted for within the Foundation's own audited books, and the final accounting of that program has not yet been published; outcomes were audited by no one. On that record the Foundation now proposes to run the renewal itself, administer its own funds, report on its own dashboard, and, under the budget process it has helped design, steward the domain whose cap and strategy would decide future rounds. If the program works as described, every one of these steps is reasonable. If it does not, no step in the sequence would show it, because each takes its evidence from the step before.

This is a structural reading, and it survives the most charitable account of everyone involved. A perfectly honest operator, reporting perfectly accurate counts, still produces an account with no outside. The issue is not whether the numbers are true. It is that the numbers are all there is, and they were chosen, collected and interpreted by the party with the most at stake in them. Under this framework that pattern raises vigilance on the reading, never on the people: where the only available account of an institution's effect is the institution's own, a reader is not entitled to treat the absence of contrary evidence as evidence of effect.

What breaks a loop like this is a counterfactual: a claim about what would have happened otherwise, stated so that someone outside the institution could test it. A program can carry three such commitments. It can state an impact hypothesis — what relation it expects to create, for whom, measured how — in terms that could turn out false. It can fix in advance a falsifier: a result, in a variable the program does not count itself, that would cause the operator to change course. And it can build a correction path: a point, partway through, at which someone other than the operator can act on what the falsifier shows. This proposal has none of the three. The next three sections take them in turn, beginning with the counterfactual itself.

## **2.4 What would have happened anyway**

The proposal states its counterfactual in a sentence: without a funded program, Cardano would return to the fragmented presence of before 2025, with fewer projects at the events that matter and no shared booth. That is a claim about the world without this withdrawal, and the public record lets it be checked in part.

The Foundation does not depend on the Treasury to attend conferences. Its 2025 activity report says it kept Cardano visible across some two hundred events worldwide in that year, and it allocated CHF 23.6 million across its work in 2025 from assets of CHF 287.5 million. In its community Q\&A on 9 November, the Foundation said it had used its own resources to cover shortfalls at one or two events in the 2025–26 program. The six side events that ran alongside that program were funded outside the Treasury proposal; in 2027 the Treasury is asked to fund twelve, at US$695,000.And\ the\ Foundation\ is\ already\ contributing\ staff\ time\ worth\ US$640,000 to this program, which shows it budgets for this work internally.

None of that makes the program unnecessary. It changes what the program is buying. The realistic alternative to this withdrawal is not Cardano's absence from TOKEN2049. It is a smaller presence, funded by the Foundation and others from their own budgets, with fewer builders in the room. On that comparison the most additional parts of this proposal — the parts the Treasury buys that would not otherwise exist — are the builder travel pool and the hackathons. The least additional are the sponsorship packages at the largest events, which make up 42 per cent of the direct budget and are exactly the presence an institution with a marketing function would be most likely to buy for itself.

The proposal does not say which of the twelve events the Foundation would attend anyway, or at what scale. Nor does it size the builder pool: the US\$702,000 travel line covers "participating projects and staff", and the split between them is not stated. So the part of the program that most clearly justifies Treasury funding is the one part whose size the proposal leaves open.

The step from these facts to a conclusion is not deductive, and it should be marked rather than smoothed. That the Foundation attends events from its own budget does not by itself mean the Treasury should not fund a Cardano presence. A strong case runs the other way: a Treasury-funded booth belongs to the commons and not to any one institution, so it frees the ecosystem's public presence from dependence on a single balance sheet and a single brand. If that is what the Treasury is buying, it is worth buying. But a booth is the commons' only if the commons can see what it does, change who runs it, and hold its operator to account. Section 2.6 shows that under this action it can do none of those. Treasury funding can turn an institution's presence into the commons' presence, or it can move an institution's marketing costs onto the commons while the institution keeps control. Which of the two this action does depends on its terms, and the terms as written point to the second. This rationale does not settle the tension; it asks the proposal to show which side of it the program sits on.

## **2.5 A measure the program writes, counts and grades**

The 2027 targets are counts: 9,520 booth contacts, 955 developers engaged, 184 collaborations "initiated or in discussion", 20 panels and keynotes, 137 workshops and builder presentations, 6,900 merchandise items, 910 wallet sign-ups, 1,200 Academy subscribers, 120 sign-ups to future hackathons, a 5.5 per cent social engagement rate, and side-event registrations and check-ins. Two further measures, media coverage and "post-event on-chain transactions attributable to event leads", are to be "tracked and reported", with no target and no stated method.

Three things about this set matter.

The targets are calibrated to the program's own past counts. Per activation, the developer, collaboration and keynote targets sit slightly below what the 2025–26 program reported and the wallet target sits level with it; booth contacts, workshops and Academy sign-ups sit above it. For side events the gap is wider: twelve side events in 2027 are asked to produce 4,750 registered contacts and 1,290 attendees from outside the ecosystem, where six side events last year reported 9,621 and 2,735. Each target can be defended. Together they make a system that measures the program against its own record, and largely against the easier half of it.

Nothing in the set measures what happened after the event. A contact is a scan at a booth. A developer "engaged" is a person the booth staff classed as a developer. A collaboration "in discussion" is a conversation one side thinks may continue. The 2025–26 report counts 219 of these, including 50 with government and regulatory bodies, and does not say how many led to a signed agreement, a deployment, a pilot or anything else that could be checked. Wallet and Academy sign-ups are reported as counts without retention. The one outcome measure in the 2027 set, on-chain activity attributable to event leads, is attributed by the operator with no published method. None of this is unusual; it is how most event programs report. But it is precision about activity, not evidence of effect, and the more exact the percentages of target (145, 324, 201, 222), the less they say about anything other than how the targets were set.

And no result is named that would count against the program. The proposal does not say what finding would cause the Foundation to drop an event, change the mix, or advise DReps against renewal. A measurement system that cannot produce bad news about its own subject is not a weak instrument; it is not an instrument at all, because nothing it reports could change a decision.

This is the most repairable finding in the rationale, and the proposal already contains most of what is needed. The builder travel calls will receive more applicants than there are places. Teams that applied and were not selected are a natural comparison group: follow both groups for six months — partnerships signed, integrations shipped, users or revenue from the host market — and the ecosystem learns for the first time whether paying builders to attend changes what they achieve. The same follow-up can be run on a sample of the 2027 "collaborations in discussion" and on wallet retention at ninety days. Publish the definitions of every count, and the method for counting it, before the first event. Have someone other than the Foundation hold the source data and run the follow-up. Then state the impact hypothesis in one sentence and fix the falsifier: the result that would cause the Foundation to recommend against funding the program, or a part of it, in 2028\.

## **2.6 Nothing to steer with once the money moves**

The third commitment a counterfactual needs is somewhere to act on it. Suppose the Africa Tech Summit and Consensus results in the first half of 2027, read honestly, show that a particular kind of activation produces little. What can anyone other than the Foundation do?

As written, nothing. The whole ₳24,995,775 is withdrawn in one transaction to a Foundation key. There is no administrator, no third-party assurer, no contract between the Foundation and anyone representing the Treasury, and no party with standing to pause, redirect or recover funds. Every adjustment the proposal anticipates — replacing an event, scaling back after a price fall, moving funds from a cancelled event to another one — is the Foundation's to make, announced on the Forum before funds are committed, with the total as the only limit. Announcement is welcome, but it is not a check. An announcement that no one can answer with effect is disclosure, and disclosure tells people what will happen without giving them any say in whether it does. DReps' only real recourse is to vote differently in 2028, on a report written by the same party.

The 2025–26 program ran on a different structure, and the comparison has to be stated fairly. It was submitted by Intersect as administrator, under a treasury contract framework audited externally, with an oversight committee of five entities whose signatures were required to disburse and which could pause or sweep funds, and with a third-party assurer signing off milestones. In practice that structure checked little after the start, because about 85 per cent of the ₳6,000,000 was disbursed at the first milestone in August 2025\. So neither program has had a working mid-course correction. The difference is that the earlier one had the formal machinery, and this one removes it at four times the ada and with the whole amount paid at once.

The price clause shows the same gap from another side. At the time of writing ada trades near USD 0.15, so the withdrawal would cover about three-quarters of the dollar plan. The proposal's answer — review quarterly and scale back later activations — is the right instinct, but it does not say which activations go first, so the shape of a quarter-smaller program is the Foundation's to decide after the vote. And refunds of anything unspent are due within six months of the last activation, so the commons' claim on unused funds runs until mid-2028, enforceable only by the Foundation's own commitment.

The timing of the spending sharpens all of this. The current spending limit covers epochs 613 to 713 and expires in early July 2027\. Of the program's direct spending, 45 per cent falls in the first half of 2027 (Nairobi, Tokyo, Bangkok, Miami, New York) and 55 per cent in the second half (Paris onward). The proposal's case for urgency — booths sold months ahead, the February start — is a good case for the first half. It is not a case for withdrawing the second half now, from the remaining headroom of a limit that will have expired before that money is spent, ahead of whatever limit DReps set for 2027\. Doing so takes a decision that belongs to next year's ceiling and makes it under this year's.

The repair is the most natural staging available. Withdraw the first half now. Put the second half — a separate withdrawal after the 2027 limit is set — behind a short independent review of the first half's results against the falsifiers fixed in section 2.5. That gives the program the mid-course correction it lacks, gives DReps a decision made with evidence rather than before it, and costs nothing in event readiness, because no second-half booth needs paying for before the spring. If the Foundation prefers to keep the whole calendar in one action, the same effect can come from an independent administrator or a multi-party oversight arrangement like the 2025 one, holding real pause and redirect powers, together with a published order in which activations would be cut if the price falls. The Foundation could also front the first events from its own resources, as it has before, and be reimbursed on verified delivery, which is the instrument the fiduciary rulebook prefers for event spending.

## **2.7 Spending from a domain before it exists**

The proposal places itself inside a process that does not yet exist. On 28 October Intersect published a concept for a 2027 Cardano Ecosystem Budget Process, grouping spending into domains, each with a steward, a strategy and a cap that DReps approve before withdrawals are submitted. Events would sit in a Marketing, Analysis and Events domain. The Foundation says it has worked on the concept with Intersect and has put itself forward as that domain's steward. The concept has not been put on chain; at the time of writing no governance action for it exists.

Three sentences in the proposal matter here. It says this action and the budget process "could run in parallel". It says that if this action is approved, it would fall under that domain and be deducted from the cap DReps approve for it. And it says this "could apply even if a different steward is chosen", the domain is renamed, or the limit is categorised differently.

Read charitably, as it should be, this is an attempt at good faith: an undertaking not to be funded twice. The effect is still worth stating. The first and largest spend in a proposed domain is approved before the domain, its cap or its steward, by the party that has offered to steward it. When DReps later set that cap, about US\$5 million of it will already be committed, and a cap below that figure would leave an approved program unfunded. The steward question, too, will be decided by DReps who have already handed the candidate the domain's largest line. None of this binds anyone formally; the proposal says "envision". But a commitment that lands before the decision it belongs to shapes that decision, and does so by being first rather than by being right.

The reference the proposal gives for the budget process, Intersect's announcement, no longer resolves; six days after submission the link returns a page-not-found error. The proposal's claim to fit within that process now points at a page a voter cannot read. Most of the proposal's references are mutable web pages, which matters little where they are context and more where, as here, they carry part of the case.

What is owed is simple and costs the proposal nothing on the merits. Withdraw the language that pre-assigns this spend to a future domain cap, or make it conditional on that cap being approved first. Keep the 2027 second half out of this action, as section 2.6 already asks. And if the Foundation remains a candidate steward, say in the action how it would recuse itself from setting or recommending the cap of a domain from which it is the largest recipient. That leaves DReps free to decide the budget process on its own merits, which is the point of having one.

## **2.8 What the commons retains**

Treasury funds may maintain commons relations; they may not transfer a private surplus. The test is the structure of the claim, not who the recipient is, and on most of its face this proposal passes it: the recipient is a not-for-profit, takes no profit and donates its staff time.

There are two smaller surpluses, and both should be named without color. Local partners receive a ten per cent fee at six events, which is ordinary service compensation; but the partners are not named, and in at least one case the arrangement may pay the same party three ways. Rare Evo is Rare Network's own conference. The Treasury would buy a US\$55,000 sponsorship of it, \$140,000 Dev Gov Day that Rare Network runs, and — if Rare Network is the United States partner, as it was for the Americas in 2025–26 — pay it a ten per cent delivery fee on top. That may well be the most efficient arrangement. It should be stated rather than inferred. The second surplus is positional: the Foundation becomes the single counterparty for the ecosystem's public presence, which is a role of value, and section 2.7 shows how it compounds.

The value claim comes in two registers. In one it is relational: builders who could not otherwise reach a market get a stage, a booth to do business from and paid travel, and the ecosystem gets a presence it holds in common. That is the right register, and it is genuinely present. In the other, the evidence for the value is entirely counts. Under this framework, a value claim resting on counts does not discharge the burden, however well they are measured. The finding is a translation request: restate the value as the relation it serves — teams that formed partnerships they would not otherwise have formed, launches in host markets, developers who went on to build — and measure that. Section 2.5 shows how.

One more question belongs here, because the rulebook asks it of every events proposal: what public rights does the Treasury get in what it pays for? The booth collects thousands of contacts. The proposal does not say who holds that data, whether the builders on the booth receive the leads from their own demos, or whether anonymised outcome data will be published for the ecosystem to learn from. A booth paid for by the commons should leave the commons with something more than a report.

## **2.9 Fiduciary verification (external instrument)**

This DRep's framework reads the relational and structural question and by design does not read the capital-allocation one. Leaving that region unexamined would raise vigilance rather than lower it, so the proposal was run through the DRep Treasury Rule Book v17 as a subordinate gate, one that can lower a merits-Yes but cannot raise a merits-No.

By economic substance this is marketing and adoption — sponsorships, booths, side events, media and lead generation — with an embedded builder-support component, and it is read on the rulebook's marketing scorecard. Nominally it is a systemic request: the rulebook's top band begins above ₳20 million, and a US\$5 million program now crosses it because the ada price has fallen. Against the spending limit it would be very large on its own, and it takes 58.7 per cent of the remaining headroom. The highest band governs, and the rulebook does not move a request down for having a valuable purpose.

The constitutional preflight passes: the anchor is immutable and its hash matches, the author has signed, the limit is respected, and custody and delegation are as stated. Most of the supporting references are mutable, and the one that carries the budget-process claim no longer resolves.

The systemic gates do not pass, and a proposal-side failure of any of them maps to No. There is no staging: the whole amount is withdrawn at once, against a normal ceiling of about ten per cent upfront at this size. There is no independent pre-approval review. There is no separation of duties: custody, disbursement, delivery, verification of delivery and replacement decisions all sit with one party, and the planned audit is financial rather than an audit of outcomes. There is no executable pause, replacement or recovery power outside the recipient. Stress testing is partial: a price fall is addressed, but without a cut order. Public accountability is the program's strength, and it does not repair the others.

On the scorecard, the marketing minimums for conversion and retained impact and for independent verification and metric integrity are not reached, because outcomes are counts, self-reported, with no retention or conversion measure and no independent source data. Prices are estimates without quotes or benchmarks. Co-funding is real but modest, in the form of donated staff time. Audience quality and Cardano-specific strategic fit score well, helped by last year's developer-share data. The rulebook prefers, for a request of this size, a competitive multi-supplier program and pay-after-delivery terms.

So this is convergence at the conclusion. A rights-derived reading and a capital-stewardship rulebook reach No from opposite directions and meet at the same places: staging, independence of verification, measures of retained effect, and who can act once the money has moved. Had the gate come back clean, the relational No would have stood; had the relational reading passed and the gate failed, the allocation defect would have been decisive. Here the gate's use is to sharpen the specification.

## **2.10 What would make this a Yes**

The specification is organised around the three things a counterfactual needs, plus the structural items. Every part of it is cheap now.

**State the impact hypothesis and the counterfactual.** Say in one or two sentences what relation the program expects to create, for whom, and how it will be measured. Say which of the twelve events the Foundation would attend from its own budget anyway, and what the Treasury adds at each. Ring-fence the builder travel pool, separate from staff travel, with the number of funded places per event.

**Fix the falsifier before the first event.** Publish the definition and counting method for every KPI. Add outcome measures in variables the program does not count itself: six-month follow-up of selected and non-selected travel applicants; a verified sample of the "collaborations in discussion"; wallet and Academy retention at ninety days. Have someone other than the Foundation hold the source data and run the follow-up. State the result that would cause the Foundation to recommend against the program, or part of it, in 2028\.

**Build the correction path.** Withdraw the first half of the calendar now, about US\$2.27 million including the audit share. Seek the second half in a separate action after the 2027 spending limit is set, behind a short independent review of the first half against the falsifiers. If the calendar stays whole, place the funds under an independent administrator or multi-party oversight with real pause and redirect powers. Either way, publish the order in which activations would be cut if the price falls. Consider front-funding the first events and seeking reimbursement on verified delivery.

**Keep the budget process free.** Withdraw the language assigning this spend to a future domain cap "even if a different steward is chosen", or make it conditional on that cap being approved first. State how the Foundation would recuse itself from setting or recommending the cap of a domain from which it is the largest recipient. Replace the dead reference with an immutable copy.

**Make the commons' share visible.** Name the local partners and disclose where a partner is also the organiser of the event sponsored. State who holds the contact data from the booth, that builders receive the leads from their own demonstrations, and that anonymised outcome data will be published. Publish the final spend accounting of the 2025–26 program — which the Foundation's own 2025 report says will be disclosed on completion — before or alongside the resubmission, so the renewal can be read against what the last round cost.

Do those, and this becomes a proposal this DRep would expect to vote for. Most of it is drafting; the rest is a choice about staging that the Foundation's own urgency argument already supports.

## **2.11 What the vote does not reach**

A vote is a snapshot; the log is the trajectory. Several things this reading surfaced are larger than this action, and converting them into the vote would misrepresent both.

The first is the self-sealing pattern itself. An institution that defines a problem, designs the response, measures it, reports on it and then stewards the budget that judges it will not be checked by the vote on any single step, because each step is reasonable. What is missing is not a better proposal from the Foundation but an independent evaluation function the ecosystem owns: something that reads the effect of events and marketing spending from outside the parties that run it. That is a candidate for a future proposal, not a future vote, and this DRep intends to argue for it whoever ends up stewarding the domain.

The second is a trend across the actions the Foundation administers. The 2025–26 events program was paid under an Intersect-administered contract with multi-party oversight. The PFMS action in October was administered by the Foundation, on a key credential. This action is self-administered, on a key credential, with the whole amount paid at once. Each step was disclosed, and the most recent disclosure was better than the one before. The direction is still toward less external control as the Foundation's role grows. This DRep will be monitoring it.

The third is what Treasury funding does to institutional budgets. Side events that ran outside the Treasury proposal in 2025–26 are inside it in 2027\. If Treasury money comes to fund what institutions previously paid for, the commons pays more without getting more, and the institutions' own budgets shift elsewhere unseen. Whether that is happening cannot be read from any one proposal; it can be read across them.

The fourth is how this action relates to the proposed budget process. If the budget-process concept arrives on chain while this action is live, the two should be read together, as a single decision split across two ballots. Whatever happens to this action, the question of whether a steward candidate should be a domain's first and largest applicant before the domain exists will come back, and it should be answered in the budget process rather than by default.

The fifth is the ada denomination. The same dollar ambition cost ₳6,000,000 in 2025 and asks for ₳24,995,775 now, which carries a \$5 million program into the rulebook's top size band. Dollar-denominated programs funded in ada will keep growing in ada as the price falls, and the ecosystem has no settled view of whether the spending limit should be read in ada, in dollars or in what the money buys.

Finally, the questions this DRep's intake asks that no proposal template yet asks — what reverses this if it is wrong in eighteen months, what reads through it, and what would register its degradation — went unanswered because they were never put. Sections 2.5 and 2.6 are, in part, answers to them.

# **3.0 Conclusion**

We are voting **NO (remediable)**.

Cardano should be in the rooms this program targets, builders who cannot afford to get there should be helped to, and the Foundation has shown it can run the logistics. The travel pool is well designed, the spending will be accounted for better than almost any other withdrawal on chain, and the Foundation has corrected a disclosure this DRep asked for last month.

The refusal is of a structure in which the only account of the program's effect is the operator's own. The program sets, counts and grades its measures, with no outcome measured afterwards and no result that could count against it. Once the money moves, no one but the recipient can steer it, and more than half of it is drawn from this year's headroom for spending after this year's limit has expired. And the action writes itself into a budget domain DReps have not yet approved, whose steward the recipient has offered to be. The external capital-allocation instrument reaches the same conclusion from the other direction, on staging, independent verification and separation of duties.

Each finding has a repair, and the repairs make the program better rather than smaller in the part that matters. State what the program expects to change and what would have happened anyway. Fix the falsifier before the first booth. Fund the first half now and the second half behind an independent mid-year review. Leave the budget process free to be decided on its own terms. The builder pool can be in Nairobi in February on that basis.

Beyond this action, I will carry forward the need for an evaluation function the ecosystem owns, the trend in how Foundation-administered withdrawals are controlled, the movement of institutional costs onto the Treasury, and the order in which the 2027 budget process and its first spending are decided. This DRep will continue to maintain a state of inquiry around them.
