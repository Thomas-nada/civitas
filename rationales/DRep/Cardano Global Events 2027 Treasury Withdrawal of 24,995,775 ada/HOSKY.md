## Vote: No

I am voting No on the 24,995,775 ada Cardano Global Events 2027 withdrawal. This proposal asks for roughly a quarter of a year's treasury draw for conference floor space, booth builds, travel and merchandise, and every success metric it reports is off-chain. Cardano is a blockchain. Its treasury should buy activity on it. A program that cannot point to a single on-chain measurement after two years of reporting does not get 25 million ada more.

Under the HOSKY Cardano First framework, Economic Sustainability and Adoption both fail here.

## Pillar analysis

### Economic Sustainability (lead pillar)

The budget converts US$4,999,155 into ada at an assumed US$0.20, about a third above the market price at submission. If ada stays where it is, the withdrawn amount buys about US$3.8 million of a US$5.0 million program, and the Foundation has said it would scale back events rather than ask for more. The proposal is open about this. Being open about a gap does not close the gap.

The line items are the concern. Sponsorship packages (US$1,989,000), booth build and audiovisual (US$1,060,000), travel (US$702,000), side events and hackathons (US$695,000), local partner fees at 10 percent of six events (US$233,100), merchandise (US$82,000) and audit, taxes and fees (US$238,055). Over US$3 million of the ask is payments to other organizations' conferences for floor space and staging. The most expensive single event is TOKEN2049 New York at US$882,200, followed by TOKEN2049 Singapore at US$671,000 and Consensus Miami at US$512,600. These are marketing rents charged to a community treasury.

The 2025-2026 unified events program this renews was funded at 6,000,000 ada, and the 2026 Singapore requests around it totaled another 14,076,539 ada before a 22 percent reduction. This single renewal asks for 24,995,775 ada in one year, more than four times the original unified events ask. Event budgets here only ever grow.

### Adoption (contested)

The reported results of the 2025-2026 program are real work: 20 activities across five regions, 8,191 booth contacts against a 5,650 target, 1,169 developers engaged, 219 collaboration discussions, a stage for 79 ecosystem projects, 147 published articles and interviews. All of it is measured off-chain. Not one metric counts a transaction, a smart contract call, a token policy, an app deployed to mainnet, or a wallet with activity after the event.

The closest thing to an on-chain number in the whole results report is 1,045 Cardano wallet sign-ups across 20 treasury-funded activities, about 52 per event.

In the same program window, during TOKEN2049 Singapore week in October 2026, the busiest recorded stretch of on-chain activity in Cardano's history was produced by a meme token faucet. HOSKY reopened the Doggie Bowl for one week: send 2 ada, receive 1.xx ada and a random amount of HOSKY. The network processed 1,780,896 transactions in that five-day span, an all-time record, 13.65 percent above the previous record set in November 2021, which the same token also drove, with record fee generation of roughly 337,700 ada. Treasury cost: zero. HOSKY-related activity has accounted for more than 9 percent of all transactions on Cardano since launch, by its own accounting.

That is the comparison that matters. One week of a self-funded joke produced about 1,700 times more on-chain activity than 20 funded events produced wallet sign-ups. The booth model buys presence in other people's rooms. The faucet model put 1.78 million transactions on our own chain. Visibility is not adoption, and an adoption program with no on-chain target is a marketing budget.

### Governance Transparency

The proposal is well documented, and I want to credit that: a full public budget table, dates published a year ahead, question-and-answer sessions with DReps before submission, no staff-time charges, no profit, quarterly value reviews, refunds of unspent funds within six months of the final event, and a single accountable counterparty in the Foundation. This is what a competently drafted withdrawal looks like.

But the KPI ledger is self-graded. The proposer reports "met or exceeded every KPI" on its own program and arrives with the next year's request attached. There is no third-party verification, and the framework itself contains no on-chain measure, so meeting it can never demonstrate chain activity. Accountability that cannot fail is reporting, not accountability.

## Risks I'm accepting with this No

### Absence from the rooms

Conferences produce real conversations, and the 219 collaboration discussions include 50 with government and regulatory bodies. A No risks Cardano being less present where peers and institutions are physically gathered. I accept that. If those conversations convert, the proposers can document the conversions on-chain, and a future events proposal can make that its central KPI.

### The faucet is not a strategy

One record week driven by a meme token proves demand for cheap, immediate on-chain interaction, not a growth plan. Record transaction counts in a single week are not sustained adoption. I accept that this No leans on a comparison that flatters a stunt.

### Losing a trimmed-down renewal

The Foundation may return with a smaller, on-chain-accountable version of this program, and a No now risks being read as categorical rather than specific. My record on events funding is not categorical: I abstained on the original unified events proposal in 2025, voted No on the first 2026 Singapore request, and Yes on the revised 22 percent-reduced version. The objection is the missing on-chain return at this scale, not events funding itself.

## Decision

A treasury that pays for booths it cannot measure, on a chain whose record week of activity came from a community faucet that cost it nothing, is subsidizing other people's conference businesses. No on-chain activity, no funding. **No.**
