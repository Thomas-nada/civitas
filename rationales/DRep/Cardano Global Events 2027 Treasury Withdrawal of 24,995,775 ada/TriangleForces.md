There's no doubt that builders must have access to developers and investors. My objection here is whether a dozen-city conference tour, packaged with impressive-looking attendance figures, justifies another 25 million ADA (almost US$5 million).

**Visibility is not adoption.** Booth contacts, folks saying "hi" to pick up up free merch and collaborations "in discussion" are weak activity metrics. What I'd expect instead are strong KPIs. For example, **how many developers shipped on Cardano because of a booth contact? How many introductions became lasting integrations? How many "wallet registrations" were still active after twelve months?** This ain't rocket science. You got the data. Or should have it. The proposed on-chain tracking is nice, but it has no defined methodology or thresholds. Also, several 2027 targets are even below last year's results, and nobody bothers to explain why.

**And if that didn't already sunset this proposal, there's Midnight.** Blockchain channels keep pointing out that Midnight wasn't represented at the TOKEN2049 booth, not even with a logo! **How CF in all earnestness claim to represent the ecosystem while leaving out the obviously most relevant and attention-grabbing project?** I'd love to understand here who decided what and why.

Furthermore, is this a globetrotting tour or a commercial strategy? New York (US$882k) and Singapore (US$671k) alone cost over US$1.5 million. CF needs to justify the expense better, event-by-event value for money, not merely a "competitors are there too" line. By the way, **CF's contribution is mostly staff time. Aren't there any staking rewards from the Genesis ADA to chip in?**

**I want proof that funds are well spent. That CF has "skin in the game." And, most importantly, that we're funding actual value, not just another year of being professionally present and enjoying cocktails on the other side of the world, while the ecosystem crashes and developers and staking pools throw in the towel.**
