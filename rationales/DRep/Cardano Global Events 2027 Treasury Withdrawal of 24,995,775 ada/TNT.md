I find it hard to understand the NO votes cast against proposals like this one or the Trivolve Tech proposal. The latter is a particularly telling case. It aimed to expand a police evidence management system that is already operating in the Indian state of Uttar Pradesh and that records the hash of every piece of evidence on the Cardano mainnet, with more than 156,000 transactions to date. We rarely see a real-world use case with a government as a client, verifiable on-chain activity and an explicit return model. This is not the way forward. A governance system that only knows how to say no tells builders, companies and institutions that Cardano is not a reliable place to develop projects. The cost of that signal is far greater than that of any single treasury withdrawal.

A proposal for the future of governance

I believe Cardano governance needs a coordination effort. Today, DReps largely vote in isolation, without shared evaluation criteria and without effective forums to resolve disagreements before they reach the chain. This effort should be led by people with a background in economics, able to assess capital allocation, opportunity cost and the return of each proposal, who also have political skills and experience: the ability to build consensus, negotiate between opposing positions and turn debate into decisions. Technical analysis alone is not enough, and political skill without economic rigor is not enough either. Decentralized governance needs both.
