We, the Dutch Drep, vote "Yes" on this proposal. The context in which this proposal finds itself is an important determinant of this decision. 

Primary Supporting Arguments

Contextual Necessity: The proposal is situated within a context that demands progress. The current stage of development and the needs of the ecosystem make it important to move forward with the budgetary process. Productive community builders find themselves lacking funds.  

Need for Budgetary Progress: It is widely recognized that advancing the budgetary process is essential. Delays or stagnation would hinder the growth and effectiveness of the Cardano ecosystem.

Strong Community Participation: There has been substantial participation from the active voting stake. This high level of engagement demonstrates broad community interest and legitimacy in the decision-making process.

Primary Concerns

Script Witnesses Not Included: The off-chain voting process did not include script witnesses, which impacts a measure of the true will of the community (the Dutch Drep was not able to participate in the Ekklesia vote).

Administrator Role Lacks Context: The administrator role, as currently defined, does not have sufficient context or clarity, which could lead to challenges in effective oversight and governance.

Risk of Aggregating with 'low-impact' Projects: By aggregating proposals, there is a risk of including projects that may fall under fraud, waste, or abuse. Although not directly visible, such a "poison pill" effect is a serious concern, as it could undermine the integrity of the funding process.

Recommendation

Include Script witnesses capability for off-chain voting.

Improve the administrator oversight transparency including how milestones shall be assessed and by which persons, teams, committees, or subcontractors.

The risk of low-impact proposals bundled with effective proposals is significant, and may require additional time, effort and loss of funds from the teams involved. Collaboration and transparency can help mitigate this issue, teams are encouraged to assess with prudent risk management with whom they submit proposals and verify with experts before entering into such bundled constructs.

Conclusion

On balance, the need for progress and the strong community participation outweigh the identified risks. We are voting "Yes" on this proposal, with the expectation that the community and administrators will work together to resolve outstanding concerns.
