# Tony Boersma

While I deeply value the intention behind sustaining and expanding open-source governance infrastructure for Cardano, I am voting No on this proposal primarily because it fails to meaningfully integrate or harmonize with the development of Tempo.vote. 

Tempo.vote has emerged as a lightweight, efficient, and highly usable interface for ADA holders and DReps alike. It demonstrates clearer design, improved delegation flows (particularly for hardware wallet users), and a stronger focus on transparency and accessibility for end-users. Despite this, the GovTool proposal does not recognize Tempo.vote as a foundational pillar in the broader governance UX ecosystem, nor does it propose coordinated development pathways or shared architecture.

Cardano’s governance tooling should be modular, interoperable, and responsive to actual community usage patterns. A continued siloed approach—where parallel tools are developed independently without shared planning, funding, or architectural coherence—risks fragmentation, wasted treasury spending, and stagnation in UX innovation.

Until proposals like this actively align with promising ecosystem tools like Tempo.vote and build toward an interoperable, composable governance UX stack, I believe treasury funding should be withheld. I strongly encourage a revised proposal that recognizes Tempo.vote not just as a dependent or adjacent application, but as a core strategic component of Cardano’s governance infrastructure.
