## **Rationale for Voting Against the GovTool Budget Proposal**

### **Commitment to Cardano’s Long-Term Success**

As a DRep aligned with the principles of **Cardano First**, my role is to ensure that treasury funds are deployed responsibly, with clear justification and demonstrable community benefit. This responsibility is rooted in both the **Cardano Constitution** and the **HOSKY DRep Platform commitments**. Fiscal conservatism, sustainability, and decentralization are my guiding principles.

### **Past Performance Must Inform Future Funding Decisions**

GovTool has been an important experiment in governance tooling, but its actual performance to date raises serious concerns. The platform has consistently faced:

* **Reliability issues** that have impacted governance participation.
* **User experience problems** that create barriers rather than reducing them.
* **A track record of instability** despite previous funding rounds.

Funding decisions should not be based on intentions or potential alone. They must consider **prior execution and delivery**, especially when large sums of community treasury funds are involved.

### **The Proposal Lacks Sufficient Justification for Renewed Investment**

While the GovTool budget proposal outlines a **tiered funding model** and claims cost reductions, it remains focused on further expanding GovTool’s footprint. However, it fails to adequately address the core problem: **the current tool has not proven stable or reliable enough to justify this level of additional investment**.

Spending millions of ADA to continue building on a foundation that has yet to meet basic expectations is **not fiscally responsible**. The community deserves a higher standard of performance before further committing significant resources.

### **Decentralization Requires Supporting Diverse Solutions**

One of Cardano’s core constitutional tenets is **decentralization**, not just in block production or voting but also in the tooling ecosystem. Concentrating treasury funding into a single governance platform—especially one with unresolved technical issues—risks creating a **de facto monopoly on governance interfaces**, which undermines healthy competition and decentralization of tooling.

There are **other governance tools emerging in the ecosystem** that are showing promise, including platforms with better uptime, more focused scope, and innovative approaches. Treasury funds should encourage a **competitive and diversified governance ecosystem**, not reinforce reliance on a single solution.

### **Fiscal Conservatism Protects the Treasury for Long-Term Needs**

As a DRep, I take my duty to safeguard the **Cardano Treasury** seriously. The Treasury belongs to the entire community and must be preserved for initiatives that have:

* **Clear, measurable impact**
* **Proven operational success**
* **Alignment with long-term decentralization and security goals**

GovTool has not yet demonstrated that it meets these criteria. Approving additional funding without clear evidence of past success would set a dangerous precedent for how we manage communal resources.

### **Conclusion: A Vote for Accountability and Diversity**

For these reasons, I will vote **against the current GovTool budget proposal**. This decision is not anti-GovTool but pro-accountability, pro-diversification, and pro-Cardano. I support funding governance tools, but only when they have proven their reliability, demonstrated clear return on investment, and contributed to a competitive ecosystem that protects decentralization.

I encourage the community to **support a broader funding strategy that rewards proven results and fosters multiple governance options**, ensuring resilience, competition, and the best outcomes for Cardano’s future.
