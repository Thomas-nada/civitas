Vote: YES

This governance action is a protocol-parameter change that reduces committeeMinSize from 7 to 5. Submitted by Intersect on June 5, 2026 on behalf of the Civics Committee and the Technical Steering Committee, with the recommendation of the Parameter Committee, it lowers the minimum number of active Constitutional Committee members required for the committee to ratify governance actions. It does not shrink the Constitutional Committee itself and does not change the committee's vote threshold: the intent is to continue seating a full seven-member committee through regular elections while creating an operational buffer against vacancies. No ADA is requested and no treasury funds are involved.

Vampyre Fund votes YES.

This is foundational governance infrastructure, and the risk it addresses is not hypothetical. When the Cardano Atlantic Council retired effective November 25, 2025, the Constitutional Committee fell below its required membership and Cardano experienced its first governance shutdown: CC-dependent actions — including treasury withdrawals and protocol-parameter changes that require concurrent majorities from DReps and the committee — could not be ratified until the committee was replenished, even where DReps approved them. A governance system that can be paralyzed by a single resignation, term expiration, or vacancy is structurally fragile. Reducing the functional quorum to five while continuing to target a full seven seats is a conservative, sensible hardening of the mechanism. Vampyre Fund takes an ultra long-term view of Cardano's institutions, and resilience of the ratification process is exactly the kind of durable, non-tactical improvement that view favors.

The honest trade-off is that a lower floor allows governance to proceed on a smaller active base, which modestly reduces the redundancy of the constitutional safeguard: five members rather than seven would suffice to keep CC-dependent actions moving. This deserves acknowledgment rather than dismissal. It is materially mitigated by three facts — the committee's vote threshold is unchanged, the standing intention remains a fully seated seven-member committee, and the alternative on display in late 2025 was not a stronger safeguard but a complete stall. A committee that cannot act is not a safer committee; it is an absent one. Five active members remains a substantial and credible quorum.

Vampyre Fund supports this change as a matter of operational resilience, not as a step toward a smaller committee. We would oppose any subsequent action that used this lower floor as cover to leave seats unfilled or to erode the committee's vote threshold, and we will continue to weight the full and prompt seating of the Constitutional Committee as a governance priority.
