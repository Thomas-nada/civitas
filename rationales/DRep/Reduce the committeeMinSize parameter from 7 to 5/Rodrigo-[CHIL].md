Summary

I vote NO on this parameter change. While I acknowledge the operational fragility it attempts to address, reducing committeeMinSize from 7 to 5 does not resolve the underlying problem — it relocates risk and introduces new governance vulnerabilities.

Reasoning

The proposal acknowledges that if the Constitutional Committee operates at exactly 5 members — which this change permits — a single member failure still freezes governance. The operational buffer only exists if the CC consistently maintains 7 or more active members, which is a process and institutional discipline problem, not a parameter problem.

Furthermore, reducing committeeMinSize to 5 lowers the veto threshold from 3 votes to 2 votes. At a 5-member committee, a single inactive member grants another member effective unilateral veto power over any governance action. This is a meaningful concentration of institutional power.

The correct solution is to increase the Constitutional Committee size beyond 7 — not to lower the minimum. A larger committee provides genuine operational resilience, distributes veto power more broadly, and reduces dependency on any individual member's continued participation. I encourage Intersect and the Civics Committee to pursue an expansion of CC membership as the primary resolution to this issue.
