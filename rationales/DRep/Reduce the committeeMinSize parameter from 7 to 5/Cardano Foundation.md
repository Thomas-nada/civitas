We recognize the work of Intersect’s Civics Committee, Technical Steering Committee, and Parameter Committee in advancing a narrowly scoped parameter change to address an operational risk in Cardano governance.

The Cardano Foundation’s decision to vote YES is based on the following factors:

* **Operational Resilience:** The current Constitutional Committee has 7 members, matching the current \`committeeMinSize\`. This creates a fragile configuration: if one member retires, reducing the on-chain committee size below the minimum, governance actions requiring Constitutional Committee approval become ineligible for ratification. This includes Parameter Changes, Constitution Updates, Treasury Withdrawals, and Hard Forks. We view this as an avoidable risk to the continuity of Cardano governance.  
* **Reduction of Individual Blocking Risk:** With the minimum set at the same level as the current committee size, any single committee member can, in practice, create a condition where the Constitutional Committee is no longer able to support actions that require its approval. We believe that lowering the minimum to 5 reduces the risk of indirect blocking while preserving the Constitutional Committee's role as a constitutional safeguard.  
* **Minimum, Not Target Size:** This governance action changes the minimum required committee size. It does not directly reduce the current number of Constitutional Committee members, nor does it set 5 as the desired long-term committee size. We support the proposal and encourage the ecosystem to continue to maintain a Constitutional Committee composition above the minimum size, so that there is sufficient operational buffer.  

We acknowledge that reducing the minimum committee size changes the dynamics if the committee were actually to fall to 5 members. At that size, and assuming the current 2/3 Constitutional Committee approval threshold, 4 constitutional votes would be required, while 2 non-votes could prevent the threshold from being met. This is a real consideration. However, in our view, the greater and more immediate risk is the current absence of any buffer between the active committee size and the minimum required for governance to operate fully.
