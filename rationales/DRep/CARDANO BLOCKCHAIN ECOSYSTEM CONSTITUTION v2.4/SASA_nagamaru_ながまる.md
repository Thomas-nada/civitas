I voted YES on the governance action to adopt Cardano Constitution v2.4.

I consider this proposal to be a revision that clarifies the role of the Cardano Constitution and strengthens the soundness and credibility of governance operations.

First, the removal of non-binding expectations and encouragements is appropriate, as it refines the Constitution into a normative document that is actually applied and interpreted, rather than a collection of aspirational statements. This change aligns with the fundamental nature of a constitutional framework.

In addition, the removal of the Budget Info Action and the integration of its requirements into Treasury Withdrawal governance actions simplifies the governance structure without weakening transparency or audit requirements. Instead, this change clarifies responsibility and improves practical effectiveness.

Furthermore, explicitly defining the immutability of proposal documents is an important improvement that prevents modifications after submission and enhances trust in on-chain governance. The design that applies audit and oversight requirements to all treasury withdrawals also strengthens transparency and accountability in treasury management.

With respect to the changes from v2.3 to v2.4, reverting certain wording to the original v1.0 expressions in response to concerns raised by EMURGO should be understood as a pragmatic and reasonable decision to avoid governance stagnation and maintain broad consensus. These adjustments do not undermine the fundamental principles or the substantive framework of the Constitution.

Based on these considerations, I conclude that this proposal is consistent with the intent of the Cardano Constitution and the existing governance framework, and that it contributes to improved clarity, transparency, and long-term operational stability.
For these reasons, I voted YES on this governance action.
