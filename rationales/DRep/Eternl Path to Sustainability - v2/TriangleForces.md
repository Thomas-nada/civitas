I see Eternl as a legit wallet that actually gets used, has a wide reach, and a solid tech background. But this proposal is asking the Cardano Treasury to foot the bill for another year of operating costs for a mostly closed-source commercial product after years of support from Catalyst, Intersect, and the Treasury. That just isn't right.

I'm not arguing against the numbers this time. Six full-time employees at $70,000 each isn't crazy; in the software world, that’s pretty reasonable. The real issue lies in the logic behind the funding. Eternl claims it needs $420k because previous Treasury funding didn’t keep up in USD terms after ADA dropped. That's exactly why Treasury budgeting shouldn't turn into a volatility safety net for teams that price in USD, manage ADA risk poorly, and come back asking for the same amount! If a major wallet requires ongoing public support to stay afloat, Cardano needs to consider if it's really investing in stability or just keeping something fragile alive.

Wallet diversity is important, but subsidizing every "important" wallet risks turning into an expensive mess, and the Treasury that I represent as a DRep for my delegates isn't meant to be a zookeeper.
