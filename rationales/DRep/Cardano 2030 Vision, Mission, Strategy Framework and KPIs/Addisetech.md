Why Vote YES 

A key reason might some people like me hesitate to vote yes is the absence of person-level or grassroots adoption metrics. That concern is reasonable. It was raised, discussed, and answered honestly: we don’t yet have reliable ways to measure unique individuals or real community participation. That’s a limitation of today’s infrastructure, not a statement about what matters.

Voting YES is the pragmatic choice because it enables progress rather than freezing it:

It acknowledges reality without pretending it’s permanent.
The governance tool reflects what can be measured responsibly today, while leaving room to add better metrics as identity and measurement capabilities mature.

It treats the Vision as a living framework, not a final verdict.
Passing it creates a shared baseline that can evolve—especially on hard problems like SSI, personhood, and community adoption—once the right tools exist.

It avoids blocking progress over missing prerequisites.
Waiting for perfect measurement would delay coordination. Governance should move forward and surface the gaps we need to close next.

It creates the conditions to fix what’s missing.
You can’t meaningfully improve how we measure humans and communities without a governance mechanism that can prioritize, fund, and iterate on those capabilities.

It aligns incentives toward improvement instead of stalemate.
A “YES” vote doesn’t deny grassroots adoption; it keeps the door open to make it measurable and first-class in future iterations.

In short: vote YES to move forward with honesty, keep the document adaptable, and create the governance capacity needed to eventually measure what truly matters—real people and real communities.
