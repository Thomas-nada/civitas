# intertreeJK

**Proposal:** Decrease Treasury Tax from 20% to 10%
**Vote:** Yes
**Voter ID:** `drep1yfkg5ryqwaleunwqg0dtcj8g8yw8sgd9kskg2zlzdr8355gpghcvv`

---

At first I had a concern about what effect reducing the treasuryCut (tau) by half would have on the future setting of Nett Change Limits Year by Year. As reducing the amount that is coming *into* the Treasury may necessitate a reduction on how much goes back *out* of the Treasury each year.

Further thinking had me start considering that this may actually be a *good* thing, though.

As I am a fan of the argument that reducing the replenishment rate of the Treasury helps to force a more fiscally conservative approach to the Budgetary Policy of Cardano in the future.

The 2nd consideration I had of this Proposal is that it will increase the Staking Rewards going to Delegators by a stated 12.5% above current ROA percentages of each Pool.

This is indeed a nett positive and will help, in at least the shorter term, to encourage people to continue to Delegate to Pools and keep the Security Level of the overall Cardano Network as high as it can be.

I say ‘in the shorter term’ as the Rewards Rate will, of course, continue to decrease as the Reserves Level decreases, so this increase is more of a ‘kicking a can down the road’ positive effect, but still a good one.

Transaction Volume will ultimately be the protector of the Rewards Rate to Delegators. 

Well, Transaction Volume AND potentially alternative revenue streams from things like Partner Chains, but this is outside the scope of this Rationale.

The final consideration is that Parameter Changes like this one are fully reservable, and if any genuine economic issues are identified as a result of halving the Treasury Cut, a further Governance Action can be posted later On-Chain to reverse the negative effects that came out of Ratifying this Proposal.

Given these 3 considerations, I am voting in support of this change of the tau Parameter to a value of 0.1. Knowing that reversal in the future is possible if needed, and because I genuinely consider the outcomes of this change to at least potentially be good. 

Whether or not the outcomes ARE good will only be seen by allowing the change to take place. So I do not wish to make a stand against this change, in the interest of being able to actually gather data following such a change to help see if it was, in fact, good.
