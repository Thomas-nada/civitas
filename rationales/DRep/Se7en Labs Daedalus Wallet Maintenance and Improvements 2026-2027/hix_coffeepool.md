I vote YES on the Daedalus Wallet Maintenance proposal by Se7en Labs (1,785,333 ADA). Daedalus is, at present, the only full-node desktop wallet on Cardano, generating all wallet and governance data directly from the chain with no third-party APIs or trusted backends, and with keys generated and stored on-device. This is Tier 1 infrastructure that the market cannot supply, and the proposal is structured as a pure public-good grant under Apache 2.0, with no monetization, no captured users, and all outputs public in perpetuity. As any wallet can claim it does not exfiltrate keys but only an open-source wallet can prove it, this is exactly the model the Treasury should fund. The recent SecondFi incident is a direct illustration of why this matters. A browser-extension wallet is updated unilaterally through the browser's update channel, and a closed-source wallet cannot be independently verified, so users can be exposed to fund loss through no fault of their own, even from a previously trusted vendor after an acquisition or a change of control. A full-node, open-source, self-custodial wallet is the structural answer to that risk. The value of Daedalus is also not measured by active user count. As the only full-node wallet, it is the ecosystem's last-resort infrastructure, remaining functional when lite wallets fail, for example under high chain utilisation where lite wallets can be unavailable for hours, and across hard forks. This is option value: even a few thousand users today justify guaranteeing that a fully self-sovereign, auditable, API-free path to the chain always exists. On scale and discipline, the ask is modest at roughly 1.78M ADA on time-and-materials terms with unspent funds returned, in a different category from the large open-ended maintenance budgets I have opposed. This vote is consistent with my support for genuine Tier 1 infrastructure such as consensus and Layer 2 scaling, and with my opposition to over-scaled or commercial proposals that the market should fund. I vote YES.
