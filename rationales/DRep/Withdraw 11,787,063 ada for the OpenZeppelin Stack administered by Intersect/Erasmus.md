I am voting NO.

The direction is sound — an audited contracts library and reference implementations could lower the barrier for Cardano developers. But as written, the plan does not meet the bar I require for a treasury withdrawal of this size.

1. The core technical architecture is deferred until after funding. The proposal states the target smart-contract language will be chosen during the initial evaluation phase. Language choice (Aiken, Plutus, or off-chain frameworks) drives staffing, cost, audit methodology, and long-term maintenance. A budget cannot be meaningfully assessed when the central technical decision is unmade.

2. There is no real cost breakdown — no named FTEs, roles, rates, or maintenance commitment behind the $1,831,000.

3. Overlap risk is unaddressed. Input Output's contracts-library work and the Cardano Foundation's CIP-113 and regulated-asset reference implementations already cover material parts of this scope. The proposal includes no gap-and-overlap analysis, creating a risk of duplicated spend and ecosystem fragmentation.

4. No demonstrated Cardano eUTXO delivery record. OpenZeppelin's strength is EVM; its UTXO experience (Midnight/Compact) is not the Cardano toolchain.

I would reconsider a resubmission that fixes language and scope before funding, provides a complete role-and-rate budget, includes a gap analysis against existing IOG and CF work, and names a Cardano-experienced eUTXO team. The milestone gating, published audits, and treasury sweep are good; the under-specification is the problem.
