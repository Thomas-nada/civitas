We value the OpenZeppelin team and welcome their engagement with Cardano. Our objection is to this proposal's scope, structure, and price.

What the ecosystem needs from OpenZeppelin is security credibility, standardization practice, and distribution. This proposal spends most of its budget on engineering where Cardano solutions already exist. 

Our vote is based on the following factors:

* **It does not build on what the ecosystem already funds.** In May 2026 DReps approved 3,601,926 ada for Input Output's Developer Experience Initiative, which commits to "an OpenZeppelin-like library of ready-to-use smart contracts" shipping at least five ready-to-audit contracts by the end of Q4 2026\. That work is under way in input-output-hk/contracts-library. MeshJS and TrustLevel ship an open-source Aiken contract library, and Anastasia Labs maintains aiken-design-patterns. None are named here. The proposal's own motivation notes that code is "scattered across Cardano Foundation repositories, Intersect resources, and individual project codebases." Consolidating that should cost less than writing a library from scratch, not more.  
* **CIP-113 work is already published and funded.** The Cardano Foundation maintains the CIP-113 programmable-tokens repositories, with reference implementations, a completed security audit and re-audit, and a KYC substandard covering credential registries. The Tokenized Money Market Fund is the one Reference Implementation addressing a genuine institutional gap. Its credential verification should build on that work rather than reinvent it.  
* **Two of the three Reference Implementations are poorly targeted.** Liqwid Finance has been live on mainnet since February 2023, issuing a transferable yield-bearing token against supplied ada, and ada delegation is already non-custodial and liquid. Self-repaying loans are a single product line rather than infrastructure others compose on. Several Cardano MCP servers and AI coding-assistant integrations are already published by other teams.  
* **The security retainer is sized to the wrong scope.** Twenty-two researcher-weeks are budgeted against a from-scratch library plus three Reference Implementations. Under a reduced scope, the treasury pays for capacity against code that is not written.  
* **The counterparty changed after submission.** On 17 September 2026, S\&P Global announced an agreement to acquire OpenZeppelin. This proposal is dated 13 August 2026 and carries no disclosure of the transaction and no change-of-control provision. We would expect the team to address this change in ownership and explain its implications.

Cardano should fund one comprehensive developer support strategy for 2027, with vendors selected through an RFP or allocated by an expert facilitator, beginning with a discovery phase that translates what the business layer needs into defined work packages. Standardized, audited contracts for institutional use cases would lower business development costs and open a channel into the EVM world. OpenZeppelin could be an excellent partner in that. Funding a second library outside such a strategy is inefficient.

We look forward to engaging on any resubmission and strongly recommend that it account for the factors listed above.
