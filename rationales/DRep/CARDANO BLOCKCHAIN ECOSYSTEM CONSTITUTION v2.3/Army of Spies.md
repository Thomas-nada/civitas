Please see my August 6, 2025 post on X with respect to the previous v.2.0 vote.    While I appreciate that many changes were made in v.2.3 that accommodated comments made by myself and others, I am voting "no" on this proposal as to v.2.3.   As I noted in my v.2.0 comments, it is still very difficult to track the changes in this proposed new constitution.   One document was attached to the proposal that tracked changes from v.2.0 to v.2.3.   That document did what I suggested in my August 6, 2025 comments in showing changes to sections side-by-side.   However, the bulk of the changes are in another document which shows changes from v.1.0 to v.2.3 and that document does not show the changes side-by-side.   This makes it extremely difficult to track substantive changes.   Many changes to the document were fine and involved things like capitalization, grammar, and punctuation.   Those were improvements or necessary corrections in many cases.  Thank you for those changes.    However, many other changes were stylistic changes that seemed to be made at the whim of whoever made them.   I don't think we should be changing things in the constitution based on anyone's whim.  An example is the deletion of much of the aspirational language in the constitution.   As I noted in my August 6, 2025 comments, Cardano itself is aspirational and I believe our constitution should be as well.    A full set of explanatory comments (much more itemized and detailed than the current comments) would be helpful to justify all the changes in the proposal.   An example that shows the need for side-by-side changes, and seems to include deletion of aspirational language (although it's difficult to track), is Art. II, Section 1 which shows up on Line 99 in one column of the v.1.0 to v.2.3 changes and in Line 160 of the other column.   Many other no voters have pointed out substantive changes that are equally difficult to track and understand.  I may have missed them if not pointed out by others.  It makes me wonder what changes we all may be missing here.  The starting point for understanding these changes should be a side-by-side tracked changes draft or a draft with in-line changes (deletions and insertions all marked in one single text) as you would see in any other legislative environment (e.g. any markup of a bill in the U.S. Congress or most modern parliaments in industrialized countries).  Otherwise, it's too difficult to say what we are voting for here.  I previously pointed this out on August 6, 2025.   Once I can actually track the small grain substantive changes in a reliable way, I will be able to discern if I actually agree with them.  But, I will vote "no" on this current proposal.   I like many of the grammatical and other smaller corrections.  But, the other substantive changes are too hard to track and the stylistic changes seem too arbitrary to justify a "yes" vote on something as important as the Cardano Constitution.
