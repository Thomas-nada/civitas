The third paragraph of the preamble attempts to divorce the constitution from “traditional nation states”, yet proceeds to outline itself precisely as a traditional nation state, with all the same characteristics, except without any national borders. Thereby creating a globalized, and centralizing one world government.

The nature of a traditional nation state is a system which governs people. The nature of a blockchain governance system ought to be a system to govern the blockchain. This document explicitly seeks to “guard the rights of those who utilize it”, (preamble paragraph 3) (without outlining what those rights are) and thus at best is an iteration of a traditional nation state, and not a new decentralized financial governance model that we all hoped for.

This document, both implicitly and explicitly, astonishingly exceeds the rights and authorities it ought to be imbued with.

1. It ought to be explicitly forbidden to wage wars, inflict, promote, or fund violence against any person, place, or state.
2. It ought to be explicitly forbidden that any “artificially intelligent” programs be elected into governance positions, specifically and especially members of the constitutional committee, but also otherwise wherever possible. There are multiple ways to enforce this.
3. It ought to be explicitly forbidden to accumulate any outsized centralizing power. The CIP-1694 model directly allows for outsized centralization. The common response: “it’s not enforceable to prevent people from being a DRep, on the CC, and an SPO, so there is nothing that can be done”  is not a sufficient response. Nor is “community enforcement” sufficient as a “check-and-balance” as it is a standardized benefit across any model. If it’s not enforceable, we ought to construct an entirely different decentralized model that inherently doesn’t allow for the outsized accumulation, and centralization of power. It is especially bad, then, to also lie about the model citing “three independent governance bodies”, (Article III section II) when they are not, in fact, independent.
4. It ought to be explicitly and implicitly congruent with a set of principles. The tenets are neither sufficient, nor are they congruent across the articles.
5. It ought to be explicitly and implicitly understood that we are not creating a competing or combative model against traditional markets or market participants, whether they be governments, corporations, or people.

As an elected alternate for the convention, and active participant in this process, as well as within Intersect, I have seen many, many, many VERY concerning instances of censorship, severe lacks of transparency, dictatorial actions, amongst other numerous disturbing experiences. There is no need to detail them all here, but those experiences, the reasons I listed above, and the 350 million ada net change limit from the budget proposal that was just released, finalized my decision.
