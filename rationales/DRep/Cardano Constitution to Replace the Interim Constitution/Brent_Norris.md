Assumption of Future ADA Price Appreciation:

The proposal argues that the treasury’s sustainability will depend heavily on future ADA price appreciation rather than transaction volume growth. This is a risky assumption and a classic case of wishful thinking or appeal to future possibilities. If ADA’s price does not appreciate as expected, the treasury might face significant funding shortfalls, potentially jeopardizing long-term development, ecosystem funding, and governance initiatives.

Overemphasis on Staking Incentives as a Decentralization Solution:

The argument that increasing staking rewards will necessarily lead to greater decentralization is a hasty generalization. While higher rewards may attract more participation, they do not guarantee that large stakeholders won’t dominate the system. Centralization risks may still persist if the top stakeholders continue to control a large portion of ADA, undermining the decentralization goal.

Economic Sustainability Risks:

Reducing the treasury cut reduces the immediate inflow of funds into the treasury, even if staking incentives increase. This could lead to a false dichotomy by presenting the choice as either incentivizing staking or maintaining treasury health, without thoroughly exploring hybrid solutions or incremental reductions that balance both priorities.

Misleading Precedent with Plutus V3 Cost Model Update:

Drawing a parallel between this economic adjustment and the Plutus V3 cost model update could be a false analogy. The Plutus update focused on technical optimizations with minimal economic impact, whereas treasury adjustments directly affect the network’s financial foundation. The procedural similarity does not guarantee similar outcomes or levels of risk.

Potential Undermining of Treasury-Funded Development:

The proposal assumes that lower treasury funding will drive developers toward building transaction-heavy applications to secure funding through fees, which is an appeal to incentive fallacy. Developers might instead move to ecosystems with more immediate and guaranteed funding sources, especially during bearish market conditions when transaction volumes typically decline.
